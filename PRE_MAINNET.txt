CORRECTIONS THAT SHAPED THIS FILE
  A first version of this list was wrong in five ways, and the corrections are
  worth keeping because they are the kind of error that repeats:
    - it omitted who may create a launch, which is a product decision with a
      live answer in the code already
    - it said no trade is bounded, which is false: the execution guard exists

A2  FEES ACCRUE AND HAVE NOWHERE TO GO - CLOSED, ON THE FORK
    src/curb/FeeEscrow.sol replaces the mock. Balances are kept per recipient
    and per token; a claim pays the CALLER from the CALLER'S balance to the
    CALLER'S address, and there is no claim-on-behalf, no administrator, no
    owner, no pause and no recipient argument anywhere in the claim path. The
    creator takes the creator's share and we take ours because those are the
    only two things the code can do.
    EVIDENCE  A real launch, real buys, the creator's own sweep, then both
              parties claiming - asserted on their REAL NVDAc balances, not on
              the escrow's ledger (B20Graduation, test_escrow_curveFees...).
              Plus separate balances across two launches and two creators,
              partial and full claims, a stranger reaching nothing, an
              over-claim, a refused transfer leaving the balance intact, a
              short delivery refused, and a reentrant RECIPIENT paid once.
              Eight mutations behind those, in script/b20/mutate-escrow.sh.
    THE WAY OUT WAS NOT MEASURED AT FIRST, and the asymmetry cost the claimant.
              creditToken measured what ARRIVED from the start; claiming only
              called safeTransfer, which catches a `false` return and cannot
              see a token that returns true and pays less. MEASURED before the
              fix, with an injected token: 4e18 owed, 3.96e18 paid, and the
              balance still went to ZERO - the claimant lost the difference and
              had nothing left to claim with. The recipient's balance is now
              measured across the transfer and a shortfall reverts, which
              unwinds the write and leaves the claim to be made again.
              AND THE ESCROW'S OWN OUTFLOW IS BOUNDED TOO, because the
              recipient check protects the wrong person on its own: a token
              that pays the claimant their full 4 and takes 4.04 from the
              escrow passes it, and the 0.04 comes out of what backs OTHER
              people's balances. This is a shared vault; a claim is bounded on
              both sides or one claimant is made whole out of another's money.
              A surplus on the RECIPIENT'S side is not refused - their balance
              can rise for reasons unrelated to this contract. The ceiling is
              on the escrow's side.
              WHAT THE RECIPIENT MEASUREMENT IS NOT. It is a NET GAIN, not a
              delivery: a recipient that forwards the tokens onward from inside
              a transfer callback ends up with less than arrived, and THIS
              CHECK REFUSES THAT CLAIM TOO. The contract cannot tell that apart
              from a short payment, so it pays out under neither. There is a
              test for exactly that case, and what it shows is the REFUSAL -
              not that the recipient could have avoided it. An account whose
              forwarding is in immutable code cannot choose otherwise, and for
              such an address this escrow is unusable rather than merely
              strict. For a launch's creator that is the address they can no
              longer change (C3).
              THOSE ARE INJECTED TOKEN BEHAVIOURS, not observations about
              NVDAc. NVDAc is a precompile and cannot be made to short-pay on
              demand; nothing here says it ever would.
    WHAT IT DOES NOT FIX, AND THE USER SAID SO FIRST
              An escrow moves the delivery problem, it does not remove it. It
              is why a creator who cannot receive NVDAc no longer blocks a
              sweep - and so no longer blocks a graduation, since graduation
              sweeps on its way through. It does NOTHING if NVDAc itself stops
              moving to or from the escrow: the fees are then stuck in a shared
              contract rather than in transit, which is not an improvement.
              And a lost creator key is permanent - there is no administrator
              who could reassign a balance, deliberately, because an address
              that CAN move somebody else's fees is the larger risk. See C3.
    STILL NOT A MAINNET DEPLOYMENT. This closes the escrow and nothing else -
              see A1, which is still open for the policy and for the deployment
              script itself.
    THE PROBLEM AS IT WAS FIRST RECORDED - THE BEFORE STATE, NOT THE WIRING
    NOW. Every graduation sweeps fees on its way through [code:
    PonsV2BondingCurve.sol:543, inside graduate()], and a sweep pays the
    escrow. When this entry was opened that escrow was MockFeeEscrow, whose
    claim() and claimToken() returned 0 with no transfer out; no payout had
    ever been executed, and on that wiring every fee would have been locked in
    a contract with no exit. That is what the CLOSED above answers:
    DeployCurb now deploys the real FeeEscrow, one per quote asset
    [DeployCurb._deployAssetStack, `new FeeEscrow(asset.quote)`], and the mock
    is in no deployment path. (This paragraph used to say, under the CLOSED
    header, that the wired escrow was still the mock.)

        curve could starve its graduation and rescue in the same transaction,
        forcing a creator's fees out as WETH to a contract that takes only
        ETH. The keeper sends noteReady itself when graduate() would revert.
        Proven end to end: after the blocklist a passer-by notes the launch
        ready, rescues the full curve's fees an hour later, the protocol is
        paid directly, and the launch graduates with the escrow never
        touched.

C1  B20 ISSUER RESTRICTIONS - THE MECHANISM IS MEASURED. 2026-09-19.
    SCOPE NOTE, 2026-09-20: measured on NVDAc. Seven assets are live now and
    the other six are argued-identical rather than measured - see F3.
    What was open: what a restricted address does, and whether an interface can
    warn before a trade. Both are answered, on the pinned fork, against the real
    token and the real registry. What is NOT answered is kept at the bottom.

    THE REFUSAL IS A REVERT, WITH A NAMED ERROR AND ITS ARGUMENTS
      error PolicyForbids(bytes32 policyScope, uint64 policyId)   0xa43fec12
    Provoked, not injected: NVDAc names a policy per scope, that policy lives in
    the PolicyRegistry precompile at 0x8453000000000000000000000000000000000002,
    and on a fork its own admin can add an address to the blocklist. The token
    then refuses a transfer to it with PolicyForbids carrying the scope and the
    policy id. Asserted on the raw call rather than through a wrapper: it
    REVERTS - it does not return false and it does not quietly do nothing, which
    is what C1 needed to know. Nothing moves on the refusal.

    AND AN INTERFACE CAN ASK BEFORE THE TRADE, which the first version of this
    entry said was impossible:
      NVDAc.policyId(NVDAc.TRANSFER_RECEIVER_POLICY())  -> 5 at this block
      PolicyRegistry.isAuthorized(5, account)           -> true / false
    Sender, receiver and executor scopes each name a policy; sender and receiver
    both read 5 here. policyExists(5) is true, and its admin is
    0xEC0F05C174e54FBf0Fe16ad930a8AFEbCe612812.

    HOW THE EARLIER "THERE IS NO WAY TO ASK" HAPPENED, because the same mistake
    is easy to repeat. Two of them compounded:
      NVDAc answers an unknown selector by reverting with THE FOUR BYTES OF THE
      CALL. cast prints that as `custom error 0x...`, which reads like the token
      raising an error of its own. C5's "uiMultiplier() reverts" came from that
      misreading too - 0xa60bf13d is keccak("uiMultiplier()")[0:4] - and is

C3  NOBODY CAN POINT A CREATOR'S INCOME AT AN ADDRESS OF THEIR CHOOSING OR

    SHARING, WHICH CAN MOVE THE RECIPIENT ONCE.
    The curve's setCreatorFeeRecipient and rescueFees are onlyFactory [code:
    PonsV2BondingCurve.sol:295, :774]; that role is held by the Graduator.
    THE ONE CALL. The Graduator calls setCreatorFeeRecipient in exactly one
    place, step 7 of _enable [Graduator._enable], and _enable runs at most once
    per launch: the switch it writes is never cleared, and a second one is
    refused (AlreadySharing). It is reached only through enableHolderSharing,
    by the launch record's beneficiary, or enableHolderSharingAtLaunch, by the
    launcher that initialized the launch's real curve and only inside that
    transaction. The address it sets is never one a caller names: it is the

    token, checked there to be a contract that names this token, this quote
    asset and this Graduator, serves no other launch, and is none of the
    launch's own or the deployment's contracts. That distributor pays the
    launch's holders and nobody else, and nothing turns the switch off. After
    graduation the curve has nothing left to pay, and the same switch moves the
    hook's creator leg instead (FeeHook.syncCreator), with the locked
    position's fees following it live (PositionLock.feeRecipient).
    WHAT THIS ENTRY USED TO SAY. "The Graduator never calls
    setCreatorFeeRecipient, so the creator's recipient is permanently fixed in
    this stack." That is no longer true as written: a launch's creator
    recipient can change once, one way, to that launch's holder distributor.
    WHAT IS STILL TRUE, AND IS WHAT THIS ENTRY IS FOR. No address - not the
    creator, not the platform, not a launcher for someone else's launch - can
    point a launch's income at an address of its own choosing, and no address
    can move a balance already earned.
    A LAUNCHER, EVEN ONE THE REGISTRY'S OWNER ADMITS BY MISTAKE, because of
    two checks added after the internal audit of 9 October 2026 (finding 1).
    LaunchRegistry.register accepts a record only for a curve whose token()
    is the record's launch token, whose pairToken() is its quote asset and
    whose factory() is the registering launcher's own Graduator, and refuses
    a curve already registered; and the Graduator's graduate,
    enableHolderSharing and rescueCurveFees refuse a record whose curve does
    not name its launch token. So a record and its curve belong to one
    launch, and a launcher can reach only the launches it made itself.
    WITHOUT THEM THIS LINE WAS FALSE: one addLauncher - a compromised or
    misled policy Safe, or the deployer's key before STEP 4B - let a hostile
    launcher register a live launch's curve under a token of its own, then
    take that curve's whole quote reserve through graduate() (a scratch run
    on the fork took 3.90 of 3.94 WETH and stranded the launch's holders), or
    switch "its" launch's sharing on and point the real launch's creator
    income at a distributor of its own. What an admitted launcher can still
    do is make launches of its own, on its own terms - which is why the
    admission itself stays a Safe decision (SAFE_OPS.txt, Safe A).
    The switch cuts clean where it can [Graduator._enable step 5]: escrow
    credits and the hook's owed stay keyed to the creator and stay the
    creator's to claim. The two collections it makes first are BOTH BEST
    EFFORT: before graduation the curve's pending fees are rescued to the
    creator; after it, the locked position's fees accrued so far are collected
    for the creator, or held in the lock for it. Either call may fail -
    nothing pending, or an issuer blocking the creator or the lock - and the
    switch goes ahead anyway: what it failed to settle stays where it was (in
    the curve, or in the position) and is paid to the holders with what comes
    after. A caller who starves those calls of gas can only hand the creator's
    own unsettled fees to the holders, never to itself. The escrow still has
    no administrator who could reassign a balance.
    rescueFees IS REACHABLE - this entry used to say both were not. The
    Graduator's rescueCurveFees(launchToken) calls it. The curve's
    creator-fee recipient (its deployer(), read live) and the record's

    Graduator.noteReady(launchToken) noted the curve full and ungraduated,
    while it still is [Graduator.rescueCurveFees]. It moves no balance to
    anyone new: it pays the curve's accrued fees straight to the curve's own
    two recipients, the platform's and the creator's - the launch's holder
    distributor once its sharing is on - bypassing the escrow: the exit AUDIT
    C-1 added for an escrow the issuer has blocked.
    THIS IS THE CHOSEN TRADE-OFF, not an unresolved consequence of the wiring.
    An address that CAN move somebody else's fees is a larger risk than a
    balance nobody claims, so no such address exists.
    THE COST, STATED RATHER THAN SOFTENED. A creator who loses their key
    strands every future payout on that launch - the curve's share and, after
    graduation, the pool's creator leg and the locked position's fees. Nobody,
    including us, can move any of it. An account whose forwarding is in
    immutable code cannot claim from the escrow at all (A2). A launch switched
    to its holders no longer depends on the creator's key for that income;

    payouts is set out in HolderDistributorFactory's header.
    NOT AN OPEN ITEM. It is here so the cost is written down where the rest of
    the record is, not because a decision is outstanding.

C9  ACCEPTED AND DOCUMENTED, NOT FIXED - THE OWNER'S DECISION OF 9 OCTOBER
    2026, on two findings of that day's internal audit. Neither takes an
    existing balance; each is a limit of the design, written down here so it
    is not rediscovered as a surprise.
    - TAX-FREE TRADING THROUGH ONE-SIDED RANGE LIQUIDITY (finding 23). The
      hook charges a graduated pool's tax on the swapper [FeeHook]. A holder
      who adds a Uniswap v4 position of launch tokens only, just above the
      price, has it turned into quote as buyers push through it - the buyers
      pay the buy tax, the position's owner pays none - and removes it; the
      mirror case, quote only below the price, buys tax-free. So one side of
      any trade made as a limit order avoids the 1-5% tax, and the creator or
      the launch's holders lose that revenue; the product's "one tax on every
      buy and every sell" is true of swaps, not of liquidity. Refusing it
      would take a hook with the add-liquidity permission, allowing only
      full-range positions or only the pool opener, and the registry's
      LIQUIDITY_BLOCKING_FLAGS check refuses such a hook today. Accepted as a
      Uniswap v4 limitation.
    - HOLDERS' INCOME DEPENDS ON A LIVE PLATFORM PUBLISHER (finding 25). Once
      a launch switches holder sharing on - one way, for good - its creator
      income goes to its distributor, and the distributor pays only against
      roots the platform's publisher proposes through the
      HolderDistributorFactory. If the platform stops - the publisher key
      lost and not rotated by the policy Safe, the keeper shut down - no new
      root is published, and what the distributor receives after the last
      activated root stays in it for good; without the switch, that income
      would have stayed the creator's to claim. No fallback publisher is
      built. Documented: the switch is offered knowing it, and DEPLOY.txt
      decision 5 says it where the roles are chosen.

1. WHO MAY CREATE A LAUNCH  -  ANSWERED BY A5: ANYONE CAN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT THE CODE DID WHEN THIS WAS WRITTEN
    CurbLaunchFactory.launch() was owner-only; anyone else got NotTheOwner.
    The owner was set at construction. NEITHER THE CHECK NOR THE ERROR EXISTS
    TODAY - the function carries "@dev Open to anyone." and no owner test
    [CurbLaunchFactory.launch and its @dev].
    What that owner CANNOT do is worth stating in the same breath, because it
    bounds the decision: the registry is write-once, so a created launch's
    terms - beneficiary, pool configuration, refund address -
    can never be altered, and the owner has no path to anyone's funds. The
    privilege is over what EXISTS, not over what has already happened.

  WHAT A PRODUCT WOULD WANT
    A launchpad, as the word is normally used, is permissionless: a creator
    arrives, pays gas, and a launch exists. That is also what the demo's shape
    implies to anyone using it.

  WHAT HAS TO BE DECIDED
    (a) Permissionless, or curated at the opening?
    (b) If curated: for how long, on what criteria, and who holds the key -
        one key, or a multisig? What happens to launches in flight if that key
        is lost?
    (c) If permissionless: what stops the obvious abuse - a launch pointing its
        beneficiary or refund address somewhere harmful - given that the
        registry validates a configuration at launch and can never revise it?
        The validation that exists today runs at creation precisely because it
        cannot run later, and it would become the only filter.
    (d) Either way: is the owner's ability to create launches on OTHER people's
        behalf - which is what owner-only creation means when a creator address
        is a parameter - intended, and is it surfaced?

  WHY THIS ONE IS FIRST
    It decides whether the fee decision below is about a handful of curated
    launches or about an open stream of them, and those are not the same
    design.

        authority, and on whose signature? This is the question A2 cannot be
        answered without.
    (e) DOES THE ESCROW EARN ITS PLACE AT ALL? The curve credits it because
        PONS did. A direct payment to the recipient is fewer moving parts; the
        escrow buys the ability to keep paying when one recipient address stops
        being deliverable, which is a real property for a permissioned quote
        asset - the vendored code says as much in rescueFees' own comment.
        Decide it on that trade-off rather than by inheritance.
    (f) THE CREATOR'S ADDRESS IS FIXED FOR THE LIFE OF THE LAUNCH (C3). Accept
        it, or design the handoff - and if the handoff exists, it is a new
        authority and belongs in this table.
