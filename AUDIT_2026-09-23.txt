================================================================================
CURB - AUDIT, 23 SEPTEMBER 2026
Findings only. Nothing in this document has been fixed.
================================================================================

SCOPE. Everything changed between 21 and 23 September 2026: the launch fee, the
native-ETH payouts, the launch form and its limits, the paired-asset artwork,
the dev buy ceiling, and the page changes that go with each. Plus the parts of
the older system those changes touch.

STATE AT THE TIME OF WRITING. 61 non-fork tests, 272 fork tests, and the
browser suite, all passing. Every finding below is something the suites do not
catch, which is the point of writing it down.

HOW TO READ THE SEVERITIES.
  A  can produce a wrong number, a wrong claim or a wrong transaction.
  B  the code and what it says about itself disagree.
  C  a gap in what the product does, not a defect in what it does.
  D  a risk taken deliberately, recorded so it is not rediscovered as a bug.
  E  something this audit could not establish.


================================================================================
A. CAN PRODUCE A WRONG NUMBER OR A WRONG CLAIM
================================================================================

A-1  THE DEV BUY IS PARSED AT THE WRONG SCALE, AND UNCHECKED, WHEN THE FACTORY
     CONTEXT HAS NOT LOADED.

     web/app.js:2165
         const devBuy = readDevBuy(ctx ? ctx.quoteDecimals : 8,
                                   ctx ? ctx.threshold : 0n);

     With ctx null, three things happen at once:

       decimals fall back to 8. On an 18-decimal asset - WETH, the pair this
       product calls its primary one - "1" becomes 1e8 wei instead of 1e18. Ten
       orders of magnitude, silently, and the transaction succeeds.

       the threshold falls back to 0, so devBuyCeiling() returns null and the
       75% check does not run; and

       `threshold && v >= threshold` is false, so the older below-threshold
       check does not run either.

     REACHABLE. Nothing gates the Create button on ctx. Buttons are disabled
     only while an action is in flight (web/app.js:2120), so a click before the
     first refresh finishes - or after a factory read that failed - takes this
     path.

     WHAT IT COSTS. A creator asks for 1 WETH of first buy and gets
     0.0000000001, pays the gas, and owns a launch anybody can front-run -
     which is the exact thing the dev buy exists to prevent.

     THIS PARAGRAPH SAID "THIS CANNOT OVERSPEND" AND THAT IS NO LONGER TRUE.
     It was written when every deployed asset had eight or eighteen decimals.
     cbXRP has six. See N-1.

     Not a contract bug. The contract is correct; the form hands it a number
     computed from a guess.


A-2  THE FORM DUPLICATES THE DEV BUY CEILING INSTEAD OF READING IT.

     web/app.js:2301          const MAX_DEV_BUY_BPS = 7500n;
     src/curb/CurbLaunchFactory.sol   uint16 public constant MAX_DEV_BUY_BPS = 7500;

     The constant is public, so it has a getter and costs one call to read -
     the same call the page already makes for the supply, the threshold, the
     lock and the launch fee, all of which it reads rather than repeats.

     WHAT IT COSTS IF THEY DRIFT. In one direction the form refuses a buy the
     factory would have taken. In the other it promises one the factory
     refuses, after the gas. It also prints the figure - "At most 45 NVDAc -
     75% of this launch's graduation threshold" - so a drift is a page stating
     a rule that is not the rule.

     This is the same mistake the threshold comment in this file warns about,
     made one field over.


A-3  THE DESCRIPTION IS SILENTLY TRUNCATED AT 300 CHARACTERS.

     web/app.js:2256     ($("nDesc").value || "").trim().slice(0, 300)
     web/app.html:307    <textarea id="nDesc" rows="3" ...>  - no maxlength,
                         no hint, nothing said anywhere on the page.

     The summary card added on 23 September shows the WHOLE typed string, so a
     creator reads back 500 characters, launches, and 300 are stored - for
     good, since CurbLaunchToken has no setter.

     This is precisely the defect that was just fixed for the name and the
     symbol, in the opposite direction: there the page advertised a limit and
     did not enforce it; here it enforces one and does not advertise it.


A-4  THE PAGE CLAIMS A PAYOUT ARRIVES AS ETHER WITHOUT ASKING ANYTHING.

     web/app.js  payoutSymbol() decides from the page's own WETH address
     constant and the factory's quoteToken. It does not read the escrow, the
     hook, or anything else that would establish the unwrapping actually
     happens.

     Against a factory wired to an escrow or hook deployed before 22 September,
     the card would say "you are paid in: ETH" and the claimant would receive
     WETH. DeployCurb builds a fresh escrow per asset and one hook on every
     run, so this needs the page to be pointed at an older deployment rather
     than a mixed one.

     THAT IS NOT HYPOTHETICAL. On 23 September the page was open on the
     previous day's deployment and showed "launch fee: Gas only" - correct for
     the factory it was reading and wrong about the product. The launch fee is
     read from the chain and was therefore merely stale; this row is computed
     from a constant and would have been confidently false.

     A page may summarise what the contracts do. This one asserts it from a
     string comparison.

================================================================================
C. GAPS IN WHAT THE PRODUCT DOES
================================================================================

C-1  PAYING WITH ETHER ON AN ETH-QUOTED LAUNCH TAKES A POINTLESS ROUND TRIP
     AND CHARGES 0.6% FOR IT.

     CORRECTED 23 SEPTEMBER, AFTER THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS ENTRY WAS WRONG.
     It said the route "throws No v3 route between ETH and WETH could be priced
     at this size", which was read off quoteV3's error message rather than
     traced through v3Candidates. It does not throw. What it does is worse,
     because it is silent.

     WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS. v3Candidates looks for three things: a direct
     WETH -> quote pool, a USDC -> quote pool, and a WETH -> USDC pool. With
     the quote asset being WETH:

       getPool(WETH, WETH, 3000)  -> 0x0, verified on chain. No direct path.
       getPool(WETH, USDC, 3000)  -> 0x6c561B44..., and it is used TWICE.

     So the two-hop branch builds WETH -> USDC -> WETH, and the quoter prices
     it happily. Measured against the deployed QuoterV2 at the pinned block:

       1.000000000000000000 WETH in
       0.994005604549159684 WETH out        -0.5994%

     Two pool fees and the slippage between them, paid to arrive at the token
     the buyer already had. Then the v4 leg into the launch token runs on what
     is left. The same happens in reverse on a sell.

     THE HONEST ROUTE IS ZERO HOPS: wrapEth on the way in, unwrapWeth on the
     way out, and no v3 leg at all. It does not exist.

     THE DEV BUY IS A SEPARATE, HARDER GAP: it takes the quote ERC20 and only
     the quote ERC20, on every pair, so there is no ETH path into a first buy
     anywhere.

     THE DEV BUY. readDevBuy/createLaunchInner take the quote ERC20 and only
     the quote ERC20. There is no ETH path into a first buy on any pair.

     SO, ON THE PAIR THIS PRODUCT PUTS FIRST: a person paying with ether is
     quietly charged 0.6% for a swap that does nothing, and a person wanting to
     make a first buy has to hold the wrapped token and is told nothing.

     WHAT THE REFERENCE DOES. thestonks.exchange accepts ETH for every pair and
     routes ETH -> USDC -> quote with a floor enforced on-chain, executed by
     their launcher rather than an aggregator. Read from a screenshot; see E-2.

     This is the largest single gap in the product today and it is entirely in
     the page and the factory's entry point, not in the curve or the hook.


C-2  THE GRADUATOR RECORDS NEITHER THE LOCK NOR THE BURN.

     It emits both and stores neither, so every client scans logs for what a
     getter could return. That is why the page has a block-window search at
     all - and why an upstream endpoint tightening its eth_getLogs range from
     2,000 to 1,000 blocks broke the lock panel on 23 September.

     The window now fits and narrows on refusal, so the symptom is gone. The
     reason it was possible is not.


C-3  THE THIN-MARKET WARNING IS STRUCTURAL, NOT NOISE.

     Recorded because it has been asked about twice. Our curve must TAKE IN
     graduationThreshold of the quote asset before it graduates, so the depth
     of that asset's own market decides whether a launch can ever fill. The
     reference launchpad's position is single-sided - all coin, no quote - so a
     creator there needs none of the paired asset and depth is irrelevant.

     The warning is correct for the product as built. Removing it means
     changing the shape of the launch, which is an architectural decision and
     not a UI one.

     MEASURED RATHER THAN INFERRED, 23 SEPTEMBER. Their marketing copy says
     single-sided; their chain says it too. A METAc-paired launch of theirs
     with zero swaps, pool 0xcc0c251ef20ea22ceea1dd9dddeea03c45c7b083:

         METAc in the pool         0
         launch token in the pool  999,999,999.99...  (the whole 1e27 supply)

     The creator supplied none of the quote asset. The pool is live the moment
     it is made and there is nothing to fill, so the depth of METAc never
     touches their launch - only whoever trades afterwards.

     AND IT IS NOT A CORNER OF THEIR MARKET. Across their 923 launches
     (/api/coins, same day) the quote assets are:

         WETH 181, NVDAc 120, AAPLc 89, SPCXc 51, COINc 47, TSLAc 42,
         METAc 34, MSFTc 27, AMZNc 26, MSTRc 21, SNDKc 10, cbBTC 8, ...

     More than a third of those are assets this product either warns about or
     cannot price a threshold for at all. Whatever is decided here, it is not
     a rounding error.

     DECIDED 23 SEPTEMBER: LEAVE IT. The warning stays and the thresholds stay
     where they are. Three options were put and this was chosen; the other two
     were per-asset thresholds sized to what each market can supply, and
     moving to a single-sided launch. Revisit with the numbers above rather
     than from scratch.


C-4  THE SUMMARY CARD SHOWS NO LAUNCH WINDOW BECAUSE THERE IS NONE.

     The reference card carries "Launch window - 99% snipe tax, 3s". This
     product has no snipe tax and no launch window, so the row is absent rather
     than empty. Listed here so the absence is a decision on the record.


================================================================================
D. RISKS TAKEN DELIBERATELY
================================================================================

D-1  THE HOOK MAKES AN UNTRUSTED CALL INSIDE afterSwap.

     FeeHook._payNative sends ether to a launch's creator - an address someone
     else chose - while the pool manager is unlocked, bounded to 30,000 gas.
     Reentering the manager costs more than that, and the recipient is not the
     locker, so it cannot take. The price of the bound is that a recipient
     whose receive() does real work is paid WETH instead; that is tested.

D-2  DevBuyTooLarge IS UNREACHABLE TODAY AND IS KEPT.

     The 75% ceiling is tighter at every threshold. Setting MAX_DEV_BUY_BPS to
     10,000 makes it reachable again, and the mutation run confirms it: it is
     load-bearing under a plausible future, not dead weight.

D-3  collected[token] STILL COUNTS THE QUOTE TOKEN AFTER THE UNWRAP.

     The pool was charged in WETH; ether is what left. The counter is
     denominated in the charge.

D-4  SEVEN THIRD-PARTY TRADEMARKS ARE VENDORED INTO THE REPOSITORY.

     web/assets/, with source, date, licence and removal instructions in
     web/assets/README.txt. Used to identify which tokenized equity a launch is
     priced against. Not a technical risk; recorded because it is a risk.

D-5  THE ESCROW PAYS A CLAIMANT WITH ALL AVAILABLE GAS.

     Deliberate, and different from D-1: the recipient is the caller, the
     balance is written down first, and the reentrancy guard is held. A
     claimant whose wallet is a contract is paid in ether like everyone else.

SECOND PASS, SAME DAY - WHAT THE WORK SINCE THE FIRST PASS ADDED
================================================================================

The dev buy ceiling, three new quote assets, the measurement script, and the
page changes that went with them. Findings only; nothing below is fixed.


N-1  A-1 IS WORSE THAN IT WAS WRITTEN, AND cbXRP IS WHY.

     web/app.js:2196  readDevBuy(ctx ? ctx.quoteDecimals : 8, ...)

     The first pass said this under-buys: on WETH, eighteen decimals read as
     eight makes "1" into a ten-billionth of what was typed. cbXRP is SIX
     decimals, and the same fallback runs the other way - "1" becomes 1e8 raw,
     which is ONE HUNDRED cbXRP.

     So the fallback does not fail in one direction. It fails in whichever
     direction the asset happens to sit relative to eight, and the only thing
     bounding the expensive direction is the factory's 75% ceiling.

     Adding an asset with fewer than eight decimals turned a silent underspend
     into a silent overspend, and nothing in the suites noticed.


N-2  THE HALF-LINK WARNING ONLY WATCHES ONE HALF.

     web/app.js:259
         mixedEndpoint = !q.get("rpc") && !!(q.get("factory") || q.get("lens"));

     A link that names an endpoint but no factory takes the factory from the
     last session, reads it at the named endpoint, and says nothing. That is
     the same mixing the warning exists for, from the other side.


N-3  ASSET_VERSION AND ASSET_FILE ARE MAINTAINED BY HAND.

     web/app.js:50, 58

     The version exists because changing the artwork without changing the URL
     left browsers drawing the previous files - white marks on what had become
     a white tile, invisible, with no error anywhere. Forgetting to bump it
     reproduces exactly that, and it fails the same silent way. ASSET_FILE has
     the same shape: a file renamed from .png to .svg and not removed from the
     map asks for a file that is not there.


N-4  THE PAGE CARRIES A SECOND REGISTRY OF WHICH ASSETS ARE EQUITIES.

     web/app.js  EQUITIES, an allow-list of twelve addresses, behind
     isEquity().

     THE DESIGN IS RIGHT AND THE MAINTENANCE IS THE FINDING. A prefix rule
     would have been shorter and would have put cbZEC - which is 0xb2-prefixed
     like the equities - under "Stocks", which is wrong. The cost is that an
     equity deployed without being added to this list appears under "Coins",
     silently, and nothing connects the list to the deployment that decides
     what actually exists.


N-5  PRICE_FLOOR_USD DECIDES WHICH ASSETS CAN EXIST, AND IS A CHOSEN NUMBER.

     script/b20/measure-thresholds.js

     $1,000 on the valuable side of a pool is what separates "priceable" from
     "no market" - and nine of the twelve assets asked for on 23 September
     failed it. The floor is documented and defensible; it is not derived from
     anything, and it is doing more work than any other constant in that file.


N-6  THE THRESHOLDS IN DeployCurb WERE MEASURED ON THREE DIFFERENT DAYS.

     WETH and the equities on 22 September, the three coins on the 23rd. The
     file says so per asset, and the script says to re-run immediately before
     deploying - but nothing enforces it, and SPCXc is the standing proof that
     a threshold measured once goes stale: it cleared the bar when it was
     written and does not now.


================================================================================
THIRD PASS - TWO FINDINGS RAISED FROM OUTSIDE, BOTH CONFIRMED
================================================================================

Raised by the user relaying another model's reading of the ETH payout. Both are
right, both are about code written on 22 September, and both were invisible to
the suites for the same reason: no browser or fork test ever traded on a
WETH-quoted pool until 23 September.


X-1  A FEE RECIPIENT CAN STOP EVERY SWAP IN ITS OWN POOL. CONFIRMED.
     SEVERITY: this is the failure the WETH fallback was written to prevent,
     reached by a path the fallback cannot see.

     WHAT D-1 CLAIMED, AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE. D-1 says "Reentering the
     manager costs more than [30,000 gas], and the recipient is not the locker,
     so it cannot take." The second half is simply wrong:

         lib/v4-core/src/PoolManager.sol:291
         function take(Currency currency, address to, uint256 amount)
             external onlyWhenUnlocked
         { _accountDelta(currency, -(amount.toInt128()), msg.sender); ... }

     onlyWhenUnlocked and nothing else. Anybody may call it while the manager
     is unlocked, and the debt is recorded against msg.sender - the caller, not
     the unlocker.

     AND THE FIRST HALF DOES NOT SAVE IT. 30,000 gas is enough to reach take().

     THE MECHANISM, END TO END:
       1. FeeHook._payNative sends ether to the creator with a 30,000 stipend,
          from inside afterSwap, while the manager is unlocked.
       2. The creator's receive() calls poolManager.take(WETH, itself, 1) and
          RETURNS NORMALLY.
       3. `(bool sent,)` is therefore TRUE. The wrapped-token fallback never
          runs - it cannot, because nothing failed.
       4. The stray delta is never settled, so PoolManager.sol:112 finds
          NonzeroDeltaCount != 0 and reverts the whole transaction.

     Result: one address, chosen by the creator at launch and unchangeable
     afterwards, makes every swap in that pool revert forever.

     PROVEN, NOT REASONED. test_wethPool_aCreatorCanStopEverySwapByReentering-
     TheManager builds exactly that recipient, swaps, and asserts the revert
     carries CurrencyNotSettled rather than merely asserting that something
     failed. The control is already in the file: test_wethPool_aCreatorWhose-
     ReceiveIsTooExpensiveGetsWeth has a contract recipient with an expensive
     receive() and its swap SUCCEEDS, so the difference is the take() and not
     "the recipient is a contract".

     THIS WAS INTRODUCED BY THE 22 SEPTEMBER CHANGE. Before it, _pay did
     poolManager.take(quote, recipient, amount) - an ERC20 transfer, with no
     callback into anybody's code inside afterSwap at all.

     NOT FIXED. The options are a design choice and are for the morning:
       (a) go back to taking the quote token straight to each recipient - safe,
           and gives up ETH payouts on the pool side;
       (b) credit the escrow from the hook and pay ether on CLAIM, where there
           is no lock to re-enter and the reentrancy guard is real;
       (c) cut the stipend to 2,300, which cannot make a cold external call at
           all - and pays every smart-contract wallet in WETH.
     (b) keeps what the change was for. It is the one worth costing first.


X-2  THE PAGE'S RECEIPT CHECK CANNOT RECONCILE AN ETH-PAIRED TRADE. CONFIRMED.

     web/app.js  paidIn() reads Transfer logs whose sender is the POOL MANAGER
     and matches them, one for one, against the two fee legs it computed.

     On a WETH pool the new payout emits none of those:

       poolManager.take(quote, address(this), charged)
                                 ONE transfer, to the HOOK, for the total
       IWETH.withdraw(charged)   a Withdrawal event, no Transfer to a payee
       to.call{value:}           raw ether, NO LOG AT ALL

     So matchPayments finds one payment to an address that is not a payee,
     against two expected payments, and sets reconciles = false. Every trade on
     an ETH-quoted pool reports "this pool paid out 1 time(s) ... and that does
     not match a swap of ...".

     The check is doing its job - the payments really are not there any more.
     What is wrong is that nothing taught it about the unwrap.

     WHY NEITHER SUITE SAW IT. The browser tests trade on the equity pool the
     fixture builds; the new ETH-route tests read the calldata with dispatch()
     and never mine a receipt. A test that presses through on a WETH pool would
     have failed the day the payout changed.

FOURTH PASS - AN INDEPENDENT AUDIT ARRIVED, AND WHAT CHECKING IT FOUND
================================================================================

CODEX_AUDIT_2026-09-23.md, produced separately and without touching product
code. Cross-checked here rather than accepted. Its two High findings are the
two this document already proves (X-1 and A-1/N-1), reached independently,
which is worth as much as the findings themselves.

VERIFIED HERE, AND MORE SERIOUS AT ITS ROOT THAN THE REPORT SAYS:

  M-02, the v4 quote being exact only within one liquidity range. The report
  treats third-party liquidity as possible. It is not merely possible - the
  hook cannot stop it:

      mined flags: BEFORE_INITIALIZE | BEFORE_SWAP | BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA
                   | AFTER_SWAP | AFTER_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA

  There is no BEFORE_ADD_LIQUIDITY bit, and the pool manager calls only the
  hooks whose bit is set in the address. So FeeHook.beforeAddLiquidity - which
  reverts HookNotImplemented, and reads like a refusal - IS NEVER CALLED.
  Anybody may add liquidity in any range to a graduated pool. The same is true
  of beforeRemoveLiquidity. Two functions that look load-bearing are dead.

VERIFIED HERE, EXACTLY AS WRITTEN:

  M-05. preflight-mainnet.sh:112 and :140 both pass
  ${CURB_ALLOW_EOA_RECIPIENT:-true}. DeployCurb refuses a no-code platform
  recipient by default; the preflight flips that default to allow, at the one
  layer whose job is to protect the real deployment.

  M-03. v3PoolsBetween sorts tiers by the token balance sitting in each pool
  and v3Candidates keeps only [0]. Tiers are therefore eliminated before the
  quoter ever sees them. In v3 custody is not executable depth at the current
  price - which is the lesson the header of measure-thresholds.js already
  spells out for PRICE, made again one layer over for ROUTE.

VERIFIED, WITH ONE PART OF THE MECHANISM OVERSTATED:

  M-06. The core is right and it is our code: launch() sends the fee to the
  hook's current platform recipient with an unrestricted-gas call, so a
  recipient that will not take ether stops every new launch. LaunchFeeNotTaken
  was written as "refused rather than stranding the fee" and the availability
  consequence was never stated.

  The report adds that a recipient can "re-enter launch() and recycle the same
  fee to itself until gas exhaustion". A re-entrant launch() must supply its
  own msg.value or fail the exact-amount check, so the attacker is forwarding
  ether they were just given rather than recycling one payment - and the
  outcome is the same revert a plain refusal produces. The recursion is a
  longer road to the DoS that already exists, not a second vulnerability.

NOT RE-DERIVED HERE: M-04 (registry deployer is a permanent, immediate
administrator) and L-03 (unbounded caller-chosen loops in the lens). Both read
correctly against the source; neither was independently reproduced, and this
document does not claim more than it did.

FIFTH PASS - THE SECOND EXTERNAL REPORT, AND THE FINDING THAT OUTRANKS X-1
================================================================================

CODEX_AUDIT_RECHECK_2026-09-23.md. Its H-01 and H-03 are this document's X-1
and A-1/N-1. Its H-02 is new here, and checking it changed the shape of the
whole problem.


Y-1  AN ISSUER BLOCKING THE CREATOR'S FEE ADDRESS STOPS EVERY SWAP IN A
     GRADUATED EQUITY POOL. CONFIRMED, INDEPENDENTLY.

     WHAT THE REPORT PROVED AND WHAT IT DID NOT. It cites two tests already in
     this repository - test_aBlockedRecipientIsRefusedWithPolicyForbids and
     test_oneBlocklistEntryStopsTwoDifferentAssets. Those establish that a B20
     TRANSFER to a blocked receiver is refused. They say nothing about a SWAP.
     The step from one to the other is the claim, so it was tested here:

         test_equityPool_aBlockedCreatorAddressStopsEverySwap

       1. an NVDAc launch graduates with a named creator;
       2. a swap SUCCEEDS - the control, without which this proves only that
          something reverts;
       3. the policy admin adds that creator to the receiver blocklist;
       4. the same swap reverts, and the revert data carries PolicyForbids.

     WHY THIS OUTRANKS X-1. X-1 needs a creator who chose a malicious payout
     address. This needs nobody to misbehave at all: blocking an address is the
     designed purpose of a compliance token, an expected event rather than an
     attack. And it reaches every equity pair, which is most of the product,
     where X-1 reaches only WETH pairs.

     NOT INTRODUCED BY THE ETHER PAYOUT. _pay has taken the quote asset
     straight to cfg.creator since long before 22 September. That change moved
     the WETH case out of this path and put X-1 there instead.

     THE CREATOR ADDRESS CANNOT BE ROTATED. It is written into the hook at
     graduation and there is no setter, so a pool that hits this cannot be
     repaired from inside the product at all.


Y-2  X-1 AND Y-1 ARE ONE FLAW WITH TWO ENTRANCES, AND THAT IS THE THING TO FIX.

     Both say: THE SUCCESS OF A SWAP DEPENDS ON AN EXTERNAL TRANSFER TO AN
     ADDRESS CHOSEN AT LAUNCH AND NEVER CHANGEABLE.

       WETH pairs   - the recipient runs code and can leave an unsettled delta
       equity pairs - the recipient is subject to an issuer policy that can
                      refuse the transfer

     Treating them separately invites two partial fixes. The curve side already
     solved this: fees are CREDITED to an escrow and claimed later, so a
     recipient who cannot take delivery loses their claim rather than stopping
     everybody's trading. The pool side pushes instead, and that is the whole
     difference.

     This raises the priority of the escrow option recorded under X-1 from "the
     one worth costing first" to "the one that closes both".


================================================================================
CLOSING PASS - WHAT WAS FIXED, WHAT WAS NOT, AND WHAT THE FIXING BROKE
================================================================================

Written after the work, against the three suites green: 61 non-fork, 274 fork,
64 browser. Everything below was verified by a test that fails without it.


FIXED
-----

X-1 + Y-1  ONE FLAW, TWO DOORS, CLOSED TOGETHER. A swap's success no longer
     depends on an external transfer to an address chosen at launch. WETH never
     leaves during a swap; every other asset is still tried directly and falls
     back to a debt only when refused. Three tests that proved the holes this
     morning now assert the opposite, and the equity fallback is
     mutation-checked.

A-1  The dev buy is scaled by what the transaction pinned, and refuses when
     nothing is pinned. N-1's overspend on a six-decimal asset goes with it.

M-06 A refused launch fee is held for the platform rather than refusing every
     launch. launch() is guarded against reentry from the fee call.

M-01 The receipt reconciles a fee leg settled to its payee OR to the hook, one
     payment per leg.

M-03 Route candidates are no longer eliminated by custody before the quoter
     sees them.

M-05 The mainnet preflight stops defaulting the Safe-recipient override to
     true.

L-03 The lens caps a page at 50.

A-2, A-3, A-4, B-1, N-2  The ceiling is read rather than repeated; the
     description is capped, advertised and refused rather than cut; the ether
     claim asks the hook; the retry comment matches the code; the half-link
     warning watches both directions.


NOT FIXED, AND WHY

M-02 THE v4 QUOTE DOES NOT MODEL TICK TRAVERSAL. Confirmed worse at its root

WHAT THE FIXING BROKE, AND WHAT THAT SAYS
-----------------------------------------

Six of the failures in this stretch were caused by the fixes rather than found
by them, and every one was caught by a suite rather than by reading:

  pin.hook read in a function that never received pin;
  q read outside loadCfg, where it does not exist;
  quoting a nearly empty tier with a large amount, which QuoterV2 SIMULATES -
    the eth_call effectively never returns, and one such path held up the whole
    quote for a user as well as a test;
  the lock panel's log window turned from a fast 413 into a slow upstream fetch
    that every refresh awaited;
  a fee expectation written as one aggregate payment when the contract takes
    once per leg;
  and the phrase "was expected" in an explanation, which the browser suite's
    press() helper treats as the page reporting a failure.

THE ONE THAT TOOK EIGHT ATTEMPTS was the last of the browser failures, and five
of those attempts were hypotheses that measurement then disproved. What ended it
was not a better guess: it was making the mismatch message name the payments on
BOTH sides instead of only the output side. The message had been describing
numbers that were fine while staying silent about the ones that were not.
