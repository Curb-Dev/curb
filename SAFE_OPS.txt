WHAT THIS IS
  How the keys that control CURB after the deployment are held and used: the
  two Safes and their signers, how a signature is checked before it is
  made, how a signer is replaced, who is reachable during a payout's veto
  hour, the platform recipient's custody, and the accounts that control the
  site, the code and the keeper's secrets. Anyone who can push to main or
  take over the host or the domain controls what users sign; for a
  launchpad that is a more common attack than a contract bug, so the accounts
  get the same care as the keys.
  What to do in an incident is INCIDENT.txt; how the Safes are created is
  DEPLOY.txt decision 3.

BEFORE ANY SIGNATURE
================================================================================
  The Bybit loss of February 2025 was a Safe whose signers approved what
  their screens showed, not what their devices signed. So every signature,
  every time, routine or urgent:

  1. KNOW WHAT YOU EXPECT before you open the Safe: the contract (from
     ADDRESSES.txt), the function, its arguments, value 0. The runbook step
     or the alert you are acting on says which. A transaction nobody can tie
     to a step or an alert is not signed.
  2. READ THE DECODED CALL in the Safe app: the "to" is that contract, the
     function and every argument are the ones expected, and the selector is
     the one in the table below.
  3. OPERATION MUST BE 0 (CALL). An operation of 1 (DELEGATECALL) runs
     someone else's code as the Safe itself and can take the Safe over. No
     call CURB ever needs is a delegatecall.
  4. THE NONCE is the Safe's next one, or the one you are deliberately
     replacing.
  5. COMPARE THE HASHES ON THE DEVICE. A Ledger shows the domain hash and the
     message hash it is signing. Compute both independently from the decoded
     fields (to, value, data, operation, nonce and the rest) on another
     machine - a tool such as safe-tx-hashes-util does this - and check the
     device, the independent result and the Safe app all agree. Any
     difference: stop, sign nothing, tell the other signers.
  6. NEVER SIGN FROM A LINK someone sent you, an "urgent" message, or a page
     you did not open yourself from a bookmark.

================================================================================
REPLACING A SIGNER
================================================================================
  A lost or doubtful device is replaced AT ONCE, before a second one fails:
  a 2-of-3 that loses two keys is locked for good, and with it whatever it
  owns.
  In the Safe app: Settings, Setup, the signer, Replace. Underneath it is the
  Safe calling itself with
      swapOwner(prevOwner, oldOwner, newOwner)
  where prevOwner is the signer listed just before oldOwner in getOwners(),
  or 0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000001 when oldOwner is the first.
  Check the decoded call as above: "to" is the Safe's own address, oldOwner
  is the key being removed, newOwner is a fresh hardware wallet whose
  address its holder read off the device. Then read getOwners() and
  getThreshold() back, and update THE SAFES above in the same day: the
  signers in the private copy, a changed threshold here, committed.
  A changed threshold (changeThreshold) or an added signer
  (addOwnerWithThreshold) goes the same way. A compromised signer is an
  incident (INCIDENT.txt): replace it before anything else is signed.


================================================================================
THE VETO HOUR - WHO IS AWAKE
================================================================================
  Every holder-sharing payout root waits one hour before it pays. The guardian
  vetoes a wrong one within minutes; when it cannot (its RPC down, out of
  ETH, its key not a vetoer), the alert reaches people, and enough signers
  of Safe A or Safe B must sign the ready-made veto INSIDE THAT HOUR - at
  night too, and on holidays.
  So, from the first launch that shares with holders:
    - every signer of Safe B (and of Safe A) receives the PAGE alerts on a
      phone (services/sharer/RUNBOOK.md part 13 lists the sinks);
    - at any hour, at least a threshold of Safe B's signers is reachable with
      their device. Write who covers which hours, and who covers for whom
      when someone travels, in the private copy (Status above):
        private copy: ........
    - once a quarter, one signer runs a dry signature with the others: open
      the Safe, build a veto, check it as above, reject it.
  If nobody can cover the hour, say so in the private copy - it decides how
  much the guardian alone must be trusted.

ACCOUNTS - THE HARDENING CHECKLIST
================================================================================
  Done before PREP 3 of DEPLOY.txt is ticked, and re-checked every quarter.
  The boxes are ticked in the private copy (Status above): an unticked box
  in a public file tells an attacker where to push.

  EVERY ACCOUNT BELOW
  [ ] a hardware security key (or passkey) as its second factor - not SMS,
      not an authenticator app on the same phone as the email
  [ ] its recovery codes printed and stored offline with the phrase backups
  [ ] a unique password, in a password manager
  [ ] the fewest people who need it, each with their own login
  [ ] the email address it recovers to is itself protected this way - the
      mailbox is the master key to all of them

  THE ACCOUNTS
  [ ] the email account(s) behind the others
  [ ] GitHub: the organisation and every member's own account
  [ ] the host (Vercel, or Netlify / Cloudflare Pages)
  [ ] the domain registrar, and the DNS provider if separate
  [ ] Pinata and Filebase (the keeper's pins)
  [ ] the RPC providers and the alert sinks (the Telegram bot's owner, the
      Discord server, the email API)
  [ ] X, the project's account

  THE DOMAIN
  [ ] registrar transfer lock on, and registry lock if the registrar offers it
  [ ] DNSSEC on, if the registrar and the host support it
  [ ] the DNS records the site needs, written in the private copy, so a
      change can be seen:
        private copy: ........

  THE CODE
  [ ] main is protected: no force-push, no deletion, changes through a pull
      request (with a review once there is a second person)
  [ ] write access for as few people as possible; outside collaborators none
  [ ] the GitHub Actions environments (sharing-mainnet, sharing-verify) are
      limited to main, as services/sharer/RUNBOOK.md part 9.1 says; their
      secrets live only there; fine-grained tokens with an expiry, each in a
      calendar
  [ ] no deploy keys, OAuth apps or GitHub Apps that are not needed

  THE SITE
  [ ] the site is published from main only: the host's production branch is
      main, and nobody publishes to production from a laptop
  [ ] preview deployments never answer on the production domain
  [ ] no deploy hooks and no access tokens that are not needed


================================================================================
IF THE SITE IS NOT OURS ANY MORE
================================================================================
  A page that changed without a commit, a DNS record nobody set, an account
  login nobody made - treat it as a takeover until shown otherwise.
  1. TAKE IT DOWN. In the host, remove the production domain or pause the
     project. If the host account is lost, point the domain away at the
     registrar. If the registrar is lost, call its security line.
  2. TELL PEOPLE, on X at once: the site is not safe right now, do not
     connect a wallet or sign anything on it, and revoke any approval given
     on it since <the time>. Name the real contract addresses (docs.md,
     ADDRESSES.txt) so people can tell them from a fake.
  3. GET THE ACCOUNTS BACK: reset passwords, end every session, revoke every
     token (GitHub, the host, the registrar), remove SSH keys, deploy keys
     and apps nobody recognises.
  4. FIND WHAT CHANGED: git log on main, the host's list of deployments and
     which commit is live, the DNS records against the list above (in the
     private copy).
  5. ROTATE WHAT COULD HAVE BEEN READ. A GitHub environment secret an
     attacker could reach is stolen: the publisher key is then an incident
     of its own (services/sharer/RUNBOOK.md part 14), and every other
     secret is replaced (part 12).
  6. REPUBLISH FROM A KNOWN COMMIT on main, after checking web/config.js and
     web/app.js's CURB_ROUTER against ADDRESSES.txt, line by line. Then
     DEPLOY.txt STEP 8's domain checks again, before the domain comes back.
  7. SAY IT IS OVER on X and in the site's banner, and write it up
     (INCIDENT.txt, COMMS).
