================================================================================
CURB - THE ADDRESS BOOK
================================================================================
STATUS: EMPTY. Nothing is deployed. Every line below is a blank to be filled in
        from the deployment's own output, not from anybody's memory.

        Filling this in is step 5 of DEPLOY.txt and is part of A1, not an
        afterthought: an address book written later from recollection is how a
        project ends up unable to say which commit its live code came from.

HOW TO FILL IT
  Take every address from the script's console output - it prints all of them:
  the shared contracts, the hook's salt, the deployer, the recipient, the
  policy owner, the sharing roles, the opt-ins, and a block per asset under "=== each asset's
  stack, read back from its factory ===". Cross-check them against
  deployments/base-mainnet/run.json, the committed copy of the broadcast
  record (DEPLOY.txt step 5), which records what was actually sent. Where the
  two disagree, that record is the truth.
  NOT broadcast/DeployCurb.s.sol/8453/run-latest.json directly: broadcast/ is
  gitignored, and the next forge run against any node serving chain 8453
  overwrites it. Copy it first, then read the copy.
  Then let the chain answer: script/b20/check-deployment.sh reads the RECORD
  (deployments/base-mainnet/run.json) back from the chain (DEPLOY.txt STEP
  5, and again with --accepted after STEP 4B) - it never reads this file -
  so compare every address written here with the record and the script's
  output before copying any into web/config.js or docs.md. Nothing else
  does: a LaunchLens or factory mistyped here and copied the same way into
  config.js passes both checks. script/b20/check-publish-set.sh --addresses
  ADDRESSES.txt then holds web/config.js to the LaunchRegistry, LaunchLens
  and CurbLaunchFactory lines below - so keep those three names at the start
  of their lines, as written here.

WHAT TO CHECK ONCE, ON CHAIN, BEFORE ANYONE LAUNCHES
================================================================================
  The script asserts hook.owner() == CURB_POLICY_OWNER, refuses an owner
  equal to the recipient unless CURB_ALLOW_SAME_RECIPIENT_AND_OWNER was set,
  and refuses an owner that is one key or a contract that is not Safe-shaped
  unless CURB_ALLOW_EOA_POLICY_OWNER or CURB_ALLOW_NON_SAFE_POLICY_OWNER was.
  Everything else here holds because the wiring calls succeeded, and is read
  back here only: wired(), requiredHook(), poolOpener(), the policy pair, the
  counts and pendingOwner(). Reading it all back is cheap and catches a run
  that reported success into a void.
  THE COMMAND THAT READS IT, from the deployment's commit, in the STEP 2
  shell with its CURB_* settings still exported - in a new shell, export the
  same CURB_* values again first, as STEP 2 writes them (this file records
  them):
    bash script/b20/check-deployment.sh <a Base RPC> deployments/base-mainnet/run.json
  It names every failure and exits 1 on any, or ends "check-deployment: ok".
  Run it at DEPLOY.txt STEP 5 - where a registry still waiting for the Safe
  is printed as a PENDING line and passes - and again after STEP 4B with
  --accepted at the end, which fails while the deployer still owns the
  registry and must end ok. An asset added later is read by passing its
  DeployAssetStack record after the first (DEPLOY.txt, ADDING A QUOTE ASSET
  AFTER LAUNCH). Use the keyed endpoint: some four hundred reads, and a
  refused one counts as a failure. It ends by printing the size-gate.sh
  --deployed command for every escrow, policy and vault, addresses filled
  in (THE CODE THAT LANDED, below). Tick the lines below from its output,
  not from memory.

  [ ] registry.wired() is true, and registry.launcherCount() equals the number
      of assets the run reported - not a number written here. It was 3 in this
      file while the deployment made 11.
  [ ] registry.requiredHook() is the FeeHook above
  [ ] hook.poolOpener() is the Graduator
  [ ] hook.platformRecipient() == each factory's protocolFeeRecipient() - true
      at deployment only. The factory's copy is the recipient AT DEPLOYMENT; a
      launch is stamped from the hook, which currentPlatformPolicy() returns.
  [ ] hook.platformFeeBps() is the share you intend to launch with, and each
      factory's currentPlatformPolicy() returns the same pair (check-deployment.sh
      reads it per asset: "<asset>: factory.currentPlatformPolicy()")
      This used to read "== 50 == factory.PLATFORM_FEE_BPS()". That constant is
      the FLOOR the tax band is checked against, not the share a launch is built
      from - launch() reads the share off the hook and freezes it into the
      record. The two were equal and were never the same thing.
  [ ] each factory's quoteToken() is the asset it is listed under
  [ ] each escrow's quoteToken() matches its factory's
  [ ] registry.launchCount() was 1 when the run ended - $CURB, and nothing
      else, unless a launch landed in the one receipt before $CURB's - and
      registry.launchAt(0) is $CURB (the section above says what if not). It
      said "is 0" until $CURB moved into the run; later it is whatever has
      launched since, and only launchAt(0) is fixed.
  [ ] each factory's launchFee() is 0 - launching costs gas only, the figure
      DEPLOY.txt records - immutable per factory
  [ ] hook.owner() is the CURB_POLICY_OWNER you chose - and is NOT the platform
      recipient. The deploy script defaults it to the deployer when the
      variable is unset - which then needed CURB_ALLOW_EOA_POLICY_OWNER=true,
      since a deployer is one key - and every other line here still passes if
      it was.
  [ ] hook.pendingOwner() is the zero address: the hook is born owned by the
      policy owner, so nothing is handed over and nothing may be pending (an
      offer there would be a move nobody planned - the keeper pages it)

  WHO OWNS WHAT, ONCE THE SAFE HAS ACCEPTED (DEPLOY.txt STEP 4B)
  [ ] registry.owner() is the policy Safe, and registry.pendingOwner() is the
      zero address. Before STEP 4B: owner() is the deployer and
      pendingOwner() the Safe - the deployer's one key can then still admit
      launchers, so this line is not ticked until the Safe has executed
      acceptOwnership().
  [ ] hook.owner(), catalog.owner() and factory.owner() (the
      HolderDistributorFactory) are the policy Safe, and pendingOwner() is the
      zero address on all three - they are born owned by it, so nothing was

  [ ] every PonsV2BuybackVault's owner() is the deployer - permanent and
      worthless (DEPLOY.txt decision 2), and the one owner left on the key.

  [ ] graduator.sharingCatalog() is the SharingCatalog above
  [ ] catalog.owner() is the policy owner, catalog.bootstrapper() the deployer,

  [ ] catalog.isApproved(<HolderDistributorFactory>) is true and

  [ ] lens.graduator() is the Graduator
