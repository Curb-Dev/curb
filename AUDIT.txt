A-1  HIGH - A TICKER CHOSEN BY A LINK REACHED innerHTML.   FIXED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT. app.html reads `factory` and `lens` off the query string, so a link
  decides which contracts the page talks to. The quote asset's symbol() is read
  from whatever factory that is, and it reached innerHTML UNESCAPED in fifteen
  places - every fee row, the reserve line, the balance line, both receipts.

  WHY IT IS THE WORST ONE HERE. This is not a defaced page. app.html is where
  the wallet connects and where transactions are built, so script running there
  can compose a transaction and put it in front of somebody already expecting
  to sign one. Delivery is a link; it needs no compromise of anything we run.

  IT WAS PARTLY SELF-INFLICTED. Some of the fifteen were older; four were added
  hours earlier while making the page multi-asset. That is how a sink count
  grows without anybody deciding to grow it.

  FIX. Not fifteen escapes - the sixteenth is whatever somebody adds next. The
  symbol is checked ONCE where it is read [web/app.js symbolOf]: a ticker has a
  small known alphabet and a bounded length, and a string outside it falls back
  to the truncated address, which is already this function's answer for an
  asset that will not respond. A thing that is not a symbol is not shown as one.

  WHAT THE FIX COSTS, because it is not free. The alphabet is
  [A-Za-z0-9 ._+-], capped at 16. A LEGITIMATE asset whose ticker uses anything
  else - non-ASCII letters, a currency sign - will be displayed as its address
  rather than its name. Every B20 equity measured is plain ASCII so nothing
  enabled today is affected, and the failure is visible rather than silent. It
  is still a real restriction on what this page can display, and widening the
  alphabet later means re-checking it against the sinks.

  PROVEN, NOT ASSERTED. Two browser tests: one makes the chain answer with a
  DIFFERENT valid ticker and requires the receipt to carry it (mutation-checked
  - putting one hardcoded name back fails that test and nothing else), and one
  makes it answer with an <img onerror> payload and requires that no dialog
  fires, no element enters the DOM, and the page falls back to the address.

  NOT CLAIMED: that no other chain-derived string reaches a sink unescaped. The
  ones found were fixed. The review was a read, not a proof.

A-2  LOW - UNBOUNDED LAUNCH METADATA ON A SHARED LIST.   FIXED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT. launch() takes name, symbol and tokenUri as free strings and ANYONE may
  launch [CurbLaunchFactory.sol:322], so both are attacker-chosen. browse.html
  writes them with textContent - so there is no injection, and that is not luck,
  the page uses no innerHTML at all - but nothing bounded their LENGTH. One
  launch with a hundred-kilobyte name pushes every other row off a list that
  strangers share.

  FIX. Capped where they are decoded [web/browse.js labelFor], with an ellipsis
  so a reader can tell a cut from a short name.

A-3  INFORMATIONAL - approve() WITHOUT A RESET, NOW MEASURED ON EVERY ENABLED
     ASSET.   NO CODE CHANGE; A TEST ADDED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT. Graduator._mint approves Permit2 without zeroing first, and the
  allowance it sets is a CEILING the mint usually does not spend to the last
  unit. A stale non-zero allowance therefore survives every graduation, and the
  next graduation on the same quote asset writes non-zero over non-zero. In the
  USDT family that reverts, and every graduation after the first would fail -
  discovered with a curve already full.

  IT WAS MEASURED, FOR ONE ASSET. test_forceApproveOverwritesANonZeroAllowance-
  Directly proved it for NVDAc. This deployment enables three. That all thirteen
  equities report one policy and one admin is an ARGUMENT that they share an
  implementation, not a measurement that they do.

  MEASURED FOR ALL THREE, 2026-09-20: non-zero over non-zero succeeds and
  returns true on NVDAc, SPCXc and GOOGLc
  [test_nonZeroOverNonZeroApproveWorksOnEveryEnabledAsset].

  STANDING REQUIREMENT: the measurement is per asset, not per standard. A
  fourth asset needs the same line before it is enabled, and the test is
  written so that adding one is a one-line change.

A-4  LOW - THE REMAINDER PATHS SWEPT A BALANCE RATHER THAN AN AMOUNT.
     FIXED 2026-09-21.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT IT WAS. Graduator._refund and _burnRemainder acted on
  `IERC20(token).balanceOf(address(this))` - everything the contract held -
  rather than on the remainder that graduation had computed.

  WHAT IT IS NOT. The POOL is seeded correctly regardless: held0/held1 come
  from a measured balance delta across curve.graduate(), which already excludes
  anything sitting there beforehand. That part is careful and stays careful.

  WHAT IT WAS. Anyone may send the quote asset to the Graduator, and the next
  graduation forwarded it to THAT launch's refundTo. Nothing of ours was lost;
  somebody else's stray transfer was delivered to an address they did not pick.
  The same shape let a donated launch token be burned.

  AND WHY IT IS NOT MERELY COSMETIC: one Graduator now serves seven factories.
  The sweep is correct only while two graduations cannot overlap, which today
  rests on no token in the path handing control back - true of B20 and of our
  own token, and an assumption rather than a bound.

  FIXED, AND THE REASON THE FIRST ANSWER WAS WRONG. This originally said "not
  changed here - changing a stack immediately after reading it is the wrong
  order", and recommended it for a future revision. There is no future revision:
  nothing in this system is upgradeable, the registry is write-once, and a
  Graduator deployed at A1 is the Graduator every launch under it will ever
  have. "Next time" and "never" are the same sentence here.

  WHAT IT DOES NOW. Both paths take what this contract held when the graduation
  STARTED and act only on what arrived since. `quoteBefore` and `launchBefore`
  were already being measured for the pool's own sizing, so the fix reuses them
  and adds no new read. _burnRemainder's final check became "nothing of THIS
  graduation's is left" rather than "nothing at all".

  AND WHAT A DONATION NOW DOES: it stays in the Graduator, unreachable, because
  there is no rescue path. That is not a new condition - it was unreachable the
  moment it arrived and the donor loses it either way. What changes is that it
  is no longer attributed to a launch it was never part of, in that launch's
  supply change and its RemainderBurned event.

  PROVEN: test_aDonationToTheGraduatorIsNotSweptIntoALaunch sends 5 NVDAc to the
  Graduator before a launch fills, then graduates it. With the old behaviour the
  test fails naming the exact amount - "the donation was forwarded to this
  launch's refund address: 500000000 != 0".

A-5  LOW - CONSTRUCTORS TOOK ADDRESSES WITHOUT REJECTING ZERO.
     FIXED 2026-09-21.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Graduator's constructor checked none of its four arguments; FeeHook's checked
  none of its four. CurbLaunchFactory, by contrast, checks all seven of its own
  and says why - the inconsistency was as much the finding as the gap.

  WHAT IS ALREADY CAUGHT, and it is most of it: LaunchRegistry.wire() proves the
  loop closes, so a zero graduator, a zero registry behind it and a zero
  poolOpener on the hook are all refused before any launch exists.

  WHAT IS NOT. platformRecipient is checked nowhere on chain. Zero there makes
  every pool trade revert inside poolManager.take - loud, not silent, and found
  on the first trade rather than lost quietly. The deploy script does guard it,
  and more thoroughly than a constructor could: it refuses a code-less address
  unless explicitly told otherwise and prints getThreshold/getOwners when there
  is code [script/DeployCurb.s.sol platformRecipient]. The defence exists; it is
  in the script rather than in the contract.

  A SECOND ONE: wire() proves hook and graduator AGREE on a PoolManager, and two
  zeroes agree. Graduation would then fail after the sale - which is the one
  failure this codebase is written to avoid, since the money is already in.

  FIXED. Both constructors now reject a zero in any of their four arguments,
  each with its own ZeroAddress error. The script's guard stays: it checks
  things a constructor cannot, like whether the recipient has code and what it
  answers. Having both is the point - a constructor argument is the last place
  a contract can refuse, and after A1 there is no other place at all.

  PROVEN: test_theGraduatorRefusesAZeroAddressArgument and
  test_theHookRefusesAZeroAddressArgument, each passing a zero in ONE position
  at a time - four calls each, not one call with four zeroes, because a single
  check in the wrong place would satisfy that and leave three unguarded. Both
  go red with the guards removed.

A-6  RESOLVED ON READING - THE ECONOMIC TERMS ARE VALIDATED, ONE LAYER LATER.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The factory constructor validates supply, pool fee, tick spacing (and, until
  4 October 2026, a lock duration - there is none now), the tax band and the quote's decimals, but NOT phantomQuote or
  graduationThreshold - which looked like a hole in a constructor whose stated
  purpose is that "a factory that cannot produce a single launch" cannot be
  deployed at all.

  IT IS NOT A SAFETY GAP. PonsV2BondingCurve.initialize computes
  reserved = supply * phantom / (phantom + threshold) and refuses
  `reserved == 0 || reserved >= supply` with InvalidLaunchEconomics. A zero
  threshold gives reserved == supply; a zero phantom gives reserved == 0. Both
  are refused inside launch(), on the first attempt, before any money is in a
  curve.

  WHAT REMAINS is a consistency gap: every other unusable term is refused at
  deployment and these two are refused at the first launch. Worth one line
  whenever that constructor is next touched; worth nothing today.

  RECORDED BECAUSE THE FIRST READING WAS WRONG. It was written up as an
  unvalidated immutable and the code says otherwise. A review that records only
  what survived reading hides how much of it was pattern-matching.

A-7  INFORMATIONAL - launchSlice PANICKED ON A LARGE COUNT. FIXED 2026-09-21.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  `uint256 end = start + count` overflowed and panicked for a caller asking for
  "everything from here" with type(uint256).max - against a comment in the same
  function promising a short read rather than a revert. A view function: no
  state, no loss, but the promise held for every count except the natural way
  to say "the rest".

  FIXED by counting from the room left instead of adding to `start`, which

  PROVEN: test_browsingTheRestOfTheListDoesNotOverflow asks for the rest from
  the middle, from the very end, and from zero. With the old arithmetic it fails
  with panic 0x11 - the overflow itself, not an incidental assertion.

A-9  INFORMATIONAL - TWO SURFACES THAT LOOK LIVE AND ARE NOT, AND ONE OWNER
     KEY THAT IS REAL.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FeeHook.collected is a public counter no payout path reads; the fees go
  straight out through poolManager.take. It is accounting, not a balance, and
  nothing can be claimed from it.

  PonsV2BuybackVault is constructed and passed to every curve, and buyback is
  off by construction [CurbLaunchFactory.sol:357] with no path to turn it on -
  PRE_MAINNET E1. That is 376 lines of deployed, unreachable code.

  AND IT IS OWNED. FOUND ON THE SECOND PASS, after the first one wrote "no
  owner anywhere in src/curb" - which is true as scoped and would let a reader
  conclude the deployment has no owner at all. It does:

    PonsV2BuybackVault is Ownable2Step [src/vendor/PonsV2BuybackVault.sol:55],
    constructed with `vaultOwner` [script/DeployCurb.s.sol:832], and the script
    passes the DEPLOYER for it [DeployCurb.s.sol:672]. One vault per asset, and
    the deployment lists fourteen assets since 2026-10-15 (eleven when this was
    written) [DeployCurb.quoteAssets], so the deployer address is the permanent
    owner of fourteen contracts.

  WHAT THAT OWNER CAN DO, bounded precisely rather than waved away:
    - setFactory(address), and ONLY that. It is write-once - a second call
      reverts AlreadyInitialized [:118].
    - nothing else. There is no withdraw, no sweep and no rescue in the
      contract; the single value-in path is a safeTransferFrom that requires
      the factory role, and `factory` is the zero address.
    - it cannot give the role up: renounceOwnership() is overridden to revert
      OwnershipCannotBeRenounced [:129]. Ownership is permanent by design.

  AND setFactory IS NEVER CALLED - not by the deploy script, not by any test,
  nowhere in the repository. So each vault ships unwired, which is what makes
  its lock path unreachable in the first place.

  NET: no value is at risk and nothing here is exploitable. What exists is a
  permanent, non-renounceable owner role held by the deployer over seven
  contracts that hold nothing and are wired to nothing. Worth writing down
  because "this protocol has no owner" is a claim somebody will want to make,
  and it would be false as stated.

C-1  HIGH - A BLOCKLISTED ESCROW STRANDED EVERY LAUNCH, WITH THE ONE EXIT
     SEALED BY OUR OWN WIRING.   FIXED 2026-09-21.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW IT WAS FOUND. The curve ships a function called rescueFees(), and its own
  comment says what it is for - a permissioned quote asset that "can stop
  delivering to that one address while still permitting transfers between
  traders and this curve", after which "the launch would be stuck on its curve
  forever". PONS wrote that for a hypothetical. OUR QUOTE ASSET IS A
  PERMISSIONED ASSET, and "that one address" is our FeeEscrow.

  WHY THE FEES BLOCK A GRADUATION AT ALL, which is the part that makes it
  serious rather than annoying: graduate() SWEEPS BEFORE it hands over the
  reserves, the sweep credits the escrow, and fees accrue from the very first
  trade - so by the time a launch is full the sweep is never a no-op. A payment
  the escrow cannot receive therefore takes the whole graduation down.

  PROVOKED FOR REAL, not argued. The issuer's own policy admin adds ONE entry
  to the PolicyRegistry precompile against ONE address - the escrow - and:
    trading carries on normally, which is the shape of it: nothing looks wrong
      until the launch tries to finish
    the crossing buy succeeds and its automatic graduation fails into the
      curve's own try/catch, leaving the launch full and ready
    graduate() reverts with PolicyForbids
    buy() reverts, the allocation is exhausted
    sell() reverts, the curve is ready
    every door at once, permanently

  AND THE SCOPE THAT FIRES IS THE EXECUTOR'S, which had to be read off the
  chain rather than guessed. The first version of the test named the RECEIVER
  scope - the escrow is being paid, after all - and the chain answered with the
  executor's instead: FeeEscrow.creditToken PULLS, so it is the executor of its
  own payment and is refused there before the receiver check is reached.

  WHY THE HATCH WAS SEALED. rescueFees() is onlyFactory. In our deployment the
  curve's "factory" role is held by the GRADUATOR - deliberately, because that
  role also gates graduate() and graduate() is what moves value. The Graduator
  exposed initializeCurve and graduate and nothing else. So the mitigation
  existed, we held the only key to it, and there was no lock to put the key in.

  FIX. Graduator.rescueCurveFees(launchToken) forwards to the curve's own
  rescueFees. The curve's creator-fee recipient (its deployer(), read live, so
  the distributor once holder sharing is on) and the record's platform
  recipient may call it AT ONCE. ANYONE ELSE ONE HOUR (RESCUE_DELAY) AFTER
  Graduator.noteReady(launchToken), and only while the curve is still full
  and ungraduated: noteReady is permissionless, records readySince once, and
  only for a registered launch whose own curve is ready and has not
  graduated. Open to strangers, for the same reason graduate() is: a caller
  chooses nothing - the curve pays its own immutable protocolFeeRecipient and
  its own creator (the launch's holder distributor, once its holder sharing
  is on: core v2) - and the only argument must already have a registry
  record whose curve names its token (CurveIsAnotherLaunchs otherwise), so
  this cannot be aimed at an arbitrary contract. A full curve is the only one
  whose pending fees block anything - graduation - and closing the rescue to
  everyone but its two payees would put a stranded launch's recovery behind
  them, which is the mistake graduate() records having already made once.
  NOT THE MOMENT THE CURVE IS FULL (RescueNotOpenYet(caller, opensAt); the
  internal audit of 9 October 2026, finding 12): the rescue pays the
  creator's tax as raw quote where the escrow pays a WETH credit as ETH, and
  a buyer who fills the curve can starve its automatic graduation (the 63/64
  rule) and rescue in the same transaction - forcing a creator's fees out as
  WETH to a contract that takes only ETH. The hour closes that: graduate()
  stays open to everybody for all of it, and the keeper completes the move
  of any launch that is not truly stranded (and, when graduate() would
  revert, sends noteReady itself, so the hour starts without a person).

  PROVEN END TO END: after the blocklist, on the full curve, a passer-by is
  refused (RescueNotOpenYet), notes the launch ready, and an hour later calls
  rescueCurveFees; the protocol is paid directly, and the launch then
  graduates with the escrow never touched
  [test_blocklistingTheEscrowBlocksGraduationUntilRescueCurveFees, which was
  named ...StrandsALaunchWithNoWayOut before the hatch existed - the old name
  is spelled without its prefix so the name check does not look for a test
  that was renamed on purpose].

  WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT FIX, and this is 6.3's own honest requirement rather
  than a gap: if the issuer blocks the PROTOCOL'S recipient instead of the
  escrow, the rescue pays it directly and reverts for the same reason. The
  hatch routes around the escrow, not around the issuer. Nothing can route
  around the issuer, and 6.3 says so.

C-2  THE CURVE MATH IS CLEAN, AND IT IS THE BEST-COVERED FILE HERE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  106 lines, a faithful Uniswap v2 constant product. getAmountIn's trailing
  "+1" rounds in the POOL's favour, which is the correct direction, and the
  guards are in the right places.

  OVERFLOW IS NOT CLOSE. The widest product our parameters can reach is a sell
  of the whole supply: amountIn 1e27, scaled by 1e4, against a quote reserve
  around 1e10 - about 1e41, against a uint256 ceiling near 1.15e77. Solidity
  0.8 would revert rather than wrap in any case.

  ONE INCONSISTENCY, and it is unreachable for us. A fee at or above 100% is a
  named revert in getAmountIn, a zero return in quoteAmountOut, and an unnamed
  PANIC in getAmountOut, where BASIS_POINTS - feeBps underflows. Three answers
  to one question.

  AND THE PARAMETER IS DEAD IN THIS DEPLOYMENT, which is the thing worth
  writing down. All four of the curve's call sites pass feeBps = 0, because the
  curve computes its fee and tax and deducts them BEFORE calling the library.

WHAT ELSE THE READ FOUND, AND DID NOT TURN INTO FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE CURVE ALREADY DOES WHAT A-4 HAD TO FIX IN OURS. graduate() hands over
  `trackedQuote` and `trackedTokens` - its own accounting - and its comment
  says why: "Any quote asset or launch token force-sent to this curve is
  deliberately left stranded here rather than folded into the graduated pool's
  seed, so a donation cannot move the price the pool opens at." Our Graduator
  swept its whole balance. The vendored code had the discipline and ours did
  not, which is worth recording as an argument for reading what you vendor.

  TRACKED BALANCES, NOT LIVE ONES, on both sides and for the stated reason: a

  threshold. The token side gets the same treatment as the quote side.

  THE FEE BUCKETS CANNOT EXCEED WHAT BACKS THEM. quoteFeeBalance and
  creatorTaxBalance accrue as a bounded fraction of amounts already added to
  trackedQuote, and MAX_TOTAL_TRADE_FEE_BPS caps the pair at 20% - which the
  constructor enforces for itself rather than trusting the factory to have. So
  getReserves()'s subtraction and _sweepFees's `trackedQuote -= ...` cannot
  underflow at our parameters, where the total is at most 5%.

  THE BUYBACK EARMARK IS BUCKETED AT ACCRUAL, so toggling the flag decides how
  the NEXT trade's fee splits and never re-routes one already charged. Dead
  code for us - buyback is off by construction - but it is the kind of thing
  that is wrong far more often than it is right.

  THE ROLES WE HOLD, AND THE ONE USE OF ONE OF THEM, confirmed by reading
  rather than assumed: setCreatorFeeRecipient and setBuybackEnabled are both
  onlyFactory, and the Graduator holds that role. The Graduator never calls
  setBuybackEnabled; that is what makes PRE_MAINNET E1 true. Since core v2
  (2026-10-02) it calls setCreatorFeeRecipient in exactly one place - step 7
  of _enable, at most once per launch, one way, and only to that launch's
  catalog-approved holder distributor, which pays the launch's holders. This
  entry used to say the Graduator calls neither; that was true of the code it

  ONE THING THE CURVE IS HONEST ABOUT AND WE INHERIT: _tryAutoGraduate catches

D-3  WHAT ELSE WAS CHECKED AND FOUND SOUND, listed so the absence of findings
     is not mistaken for the absence of looking.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE CAP IS CHECKED AT ANNOUNCEMENT AND NOT AGAIN AT EXECUTION. Sound because
  it derives from MIN_TOTAL_TAX_BPS, a constant, on a registry that is
  immutable on the hook - so it cannot move in the 24 hours between. Nothing
  can ever be pending that could not be applied, which the test asserts by
  checking the pending slot is empty after a refused announcement.

  RE-ANNOUNCING RESETS THE CLOCK rather than shortening it, so the delay cannot
  be walked down by repetition.

  THE TWO PENDING SLOTS ARE INDEPENDENT. A rate change and a recipient change
  do not interfere, and executing one leaves the other pending.

  ZERO IS A LEGAL RATE and is not confused with "nothing pending" - readyAt is
  the marker, not the value.

  A SWAP CANNOT SEE TWO DIFFERENT RATES. beforeSwap and afterSwap run in one
  transaction and read the same storage, so the partial-fill check in afterSwap
  recomputes the same legs beforeSwap charged.

  uint64 TIMESTAMPS do not overflow in any horizon that matters - the ceiling
  is around 584 billion years.

  AND RENOUNCING WITH SOMETHING PENDING leaves it pending and unexecutable,
  because execute is owner-gated and there is no owner. Stuck, not dangerous,
  and the policy is frozen either way - which is what renouncing means here.

E. ECONOMIC ATTACKS - MEASURED, 2026-09-21

  Twelve attacks, each written with its criterion BEFORE it measured anything,

E-1  BUY, GRADUATE, DUMP - IN ONE SEQUENCE, BY ONE ACTOR.        NO PROFIT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  else's timing is involved, and sells into the pool they just created.
  MEASURED: they end 2,306,213,896 raw NVDAc DOWN - about 23 NVDAc, the fee
  legs plus the price they moved against themselves.
  [test_attack_buyGraduateAndDumpCannotProfit]

  NEARLY A FALSE HIGH. The first version of this test reported a 4.29e9 PROFIT

  "profit" was that transfer. A balance comparison is a measurement only if

E-2  THE GAP E-1 WOULD HAVE EXPLOITED, MEASURED DIRECTLY.        NONE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The pool is seeded with what the curve could physically transfer, which is
  SHORT of what it priced against by the phantom reserve. Opened at the ratio
  of what arrived, it would open below the curve's last price and the first
  seller would sell into a gap the holders paid for.
  MEASURED: opened at sqrtPrice 388137142868843132408; at the held ratio it
  would have been 274454405775801996156. Those are square roots, so the size
  of the gap depends on which quantity you name it in, and the first version
  of this line named it wrong - "41% lower", which is neither: 41.42% is how
  far the correct price sits ABOVE the other one, the opposite direction.
  Stated in all three, since only the last one is the trader's money:
    the sqrtPrice would be 29.29% lower,
    the correct one is 41.42% higher than it,

  Exactly half is not a coincidence: this launch's phantom reserve equals its

E-3  SANDWICHING AN UNBOUNDED CURVE BUY.          THIS ONE WORKS. +3.01 NVDAc.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Front-run a victim's buy, let it fill, sell into the price it made.
  MEASURED: the attacker nets 300,987,389 raw NVDAc - 3.01 NVDAc - against a
  victim who passed minTokensOut = 0. With a bound set at the quote the victim
  would have got alone, the same attack reverts SlippageExceeded, and the test
  pins the FULL revert data: the victim's fill drops from 118569417719643804-
  619268222 to 72661516229194359258815607, 38.7% worse.
  [test_attack_aSandwichWorksWithoutABoundAndFailsWithOne]

  THIS IS NOT A CONTRACT BUG AND IT IS NOT FIXED. It is the ordinary MEV of
  any constant-product AMM, and the curve cannot know what the trader intended.
  What it changes is the STATUS OF THE FRONT END'S SLIPPAGE BOUND: it is not a
  nicety, it is the thing standing between a trader and this, and it is now
  load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

  WHAT THE TEST PROVES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. It proves the SEQUENCE is
  profitable when the three transactions land in that order - front-run, the
  victim, back-run. It says nothing about how an attacker would get that
  ordering, and the test simply imposes it. Stating it the other way round
  would be the overstatement: an unbounded trade is EXPOSED to this, not
  doomed to it.

  AND ORDERING IS WHERE BASE DIFFERS FROM THE ASSUMPTION. Base runs a single
  sequencer with a private mempool - pending transactions are not gossiped -
  so the classic watch-the-mempool-and-wrap-it attack is not available to an
  outsider the way it is on Ethereum L1. That is a real mitigation and it is
  worth the whole difference between "will happen" and "can happen".

  IT IS ALSO NOT OURS. It is Base's operational policy, not a property of our
  contracts: it changes if the sequencer decentralises, if a public mempool is
  added, or for anyone who gets privileged ordering - the sequencer included.
  We should not spend a mitigation we do not control. The bound is the part we
  own, which is why it is the part written down as load-bearing.

  WHAT THE PAGE ACTUALLY DOES, read rather than assumed: the slippage field
  defaults to 1, is required - blank is refused - and the resulting minimum is
  passed as the second argument to both buy() and sell(), computed as
  out * (10000 - bps) / 10000 [web/app.js:921, web/app.js:1128, web/app.html:260].
  So the default path is bounded.

  IT ALSO ACCEPTS 100, which makes the minimum exactly zero and reproduces the
  unbounded victim above - and when this was first written, nothing on the page
  said so.

  CLOSED 2026-09-21. The quote panel now warns, on all three of them - curve
  buy, curve sell and the pool swap [web/app.js:1675]. It does NOT block the
  trade: the bound is the trader's to set and there are reasons to widen it.
  Saying nothing was the problem.

  KEYED ON THE MINIMUM BEING ZERO, NOT ON THE FIELD READING 100, because zero
  is the state that removes the protection and it is also reachable from below
  - a small enough amount at a wide enough bound floors to zero too. A second,
  quieter line appears at 10% and above, since a 99% bound is nearly as bad and
  would otherwise have passed in silence.

  AND THE TEST READS THE FLOOR THE BUTTON WOULD SEND rather than the percentage

E-13 AND WHAT E-12 FOUND ON ITS WAY - 24 TESTS WITH THE WRONG LAUNCHER.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  E-12 went red on its first run for a reason that had nothing to do with the
  attack: the lock's beneficiary was neither the creator nor the attacker but
  0x7FA9385b..., the Foundry TEST CONTRACT.

  THE CAUSE. Twenty-four sites in the fork suite were written as

      vm.prank(creator);
      factory.launch(_config(true));

  and Solidity evaluates the argument first. _config reaches _saltForSideOf,
  which calls fac.supply() - a genuine external call, and a genuine external
  call CONSUMES A PRANK where a cheatcode does not. So launch() ran as the
  test contract and every one of those launches was created by it.

  NOTHING IN THE PRODUCTION CODE WAS WRONG. The factory takes the creator from
  msg.sender exactly as documented; it was handed a different msg.sender than
  the tests claimed. Two of the twenty-four actually asserted something about
  the launcher - E-12, and test_aStrangersLaunchUsesTheFactorysTerms, whose
  whole subject is a STRANGER launching and where no stranger ever did. Those
  two were void. The other twenty-two do not depend on who launched, so they
  were true for a reason they did not state.

  FIXED AT ALL 24 CALL SITES by hoisting the config to its own line, and the
  trap is now documented in _saltForSideOf itself - the call-site shape is
  what stays correct when a helper grows another external call later, which is
  exactly how this one broke. The full suite is green after the change, which
  is the evidence that the other twenty-two were indeed indifferent.

  WORTH KEEPING IN MIND: this was found because ONE test asked a question no
  other test asked. Twenty-four tests had been passing for weeks on a premise
  none of them checked.

E-14 DO THESE TESTS HAVE TEETH. FIVE MUTATIONS, FOUR CAUGHT, ONE CORRECTLY NOT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A test that cannot go red proves nothing, and twelve green tests are exactly
  the shape of a suite that has stopped checking. So each load-bearing new
  assertion was put against a mutation of the code it is about.

    CAUGHT  reserves read balanceOf instead of trackedTokens      -> E-7 red
    CAUGHT  sell() no longer closes the graduation window         -> E-9 red
    CAUGHT  buys may eat into the reserved allocation             -> E-11 red
    CAUGHT  the lock goes to whoever triggered graduation         -> E-12 red
    NOT     the clamped gross-up rounds Floor instead of Ceil     -> E-8 green

  THE FIFTH IS THE INTERESTING ONE. E-8 stayed green, and the first reading -
  that the test was weak where its own prose claimed a "CEILING division" -
  was wrong. Both sides were measured rather than reasoned about:

              charged        credited to reserve    the fill was priced at
    Ceil      2185567012     2120000002             2120000001
    Floor     2185567011     2120000001             2120000001

  FLOOR STILL COVERS THE FILL EXACTLY. The gross-up rounds down by one unit
  and the two fee legs, each floored, hand that unit straight back. So the
  ceiling is not what keeps the curve solvent on its last buy; it is a margin
  of ONE RAW UNIT - 0.00000001 NVDAc - taken in the protocol's favour.

  SO THE TEST IS RIGHT TO ALLOW BOTH. It asserts the reserve is credited with
  at least what the fill was priced at and at most one unit more, which is the
  property. A test that went red here would be pinning which rounding mode the
  vendored code happens to use - an implementation choice, not a promise - and
  would go red on a refactor that changed nothing anyone holds.

  WHAT THE ASSERTION WAS BEFORE, and why this was worth doing at all: it
  checked only that the curve's balance rose by what left the buyer's wallet.
  That is true under EITHER rounding, because a consistent undercharge is
  still consistent. The mutation is what exposed that, and E-8 now checks the
  direction as well as the consistency.

  AND THE LIMIT OF ALL FIVE: one mutation per test, chosen by the person who
  wrote the test, at one fixture's numbers. It shows these assertions are
  connected to the code. It does not show they are complete.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MEASURED: after each of two graduations the Graduator holds exactly what it
  held before, and neither refund address received anything - including from
  the other launch.
  [test_cross_neitherGraduationLeavesResidueForTheNext]

  WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT GUARD, stated because the mutation run made the
  difference visible. Disabling _refund entirely leaves this test GREEN, and
  correctly: the Graduator's own comment says the quote side binds and the
  remainder "is measured to be zero, but it is not guaranteed to be". Nothing
  flows through that path, so nothing can be guarded by watching it. What the
  test now pins is the CLAIM - the refund address receiving exactly nothing is
  asserted rather than merely permitted - so the day the quote stops binding
  exactly, a launch starts leaving dust for the next one and this goes red.

F-4  TWO LAUNCHES, TWO TAXES, AN IDENTICAL POOL EITHER WAY.      BY DESIGN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MEASURED, and this test was wrong twice before it was right:

      tax        opening sqrtPrice           liquidity              paid in
      100 bps    388137142868843132408       1224744871711329658    6060606062
      500 bps    identical                   identical              6315789475

  IT FIRST ASSERTED THE PRICES WOULD DIFFER - red, identical. Then that the
  5% launch would be seeded thinner - red, identical to the wei. Neither was a
  bug; both were the assumption that the tax reaches the pool.

  IT DOES NOT. The graduation threshold is measured on realQuoteReserve(),
  which already excludes both fee buckets, so the curve fills until the quote
  NET OF TAX reaches the threshold whatever the tax was, and hands the pool
  that same number every time against the same reserved allocation.

  SO: EVERY LAUNCH FROM ONE FACTORY OPENS THE SAME POOL, at the same price
  and the same depth, and the tax is paid by the buyers on the way up and by
  nobody else. A 5% launch costs its buyers 4.21% more to fill than a 1% one
  and opens exactly the pool the 1% launch opens. That is a product statement,
  it had never been written down anywhere, and it is now three assertions.

  "FROM ONE FACTORY" IS NOT A HEDGE, IT IS THE SCOPE. phantomQuote,
  graduationThreshold, the supply and quoteToken are all IMMUTABLE on the
  factory [src/curb/CurbLaunchFactory.sol:188, :195, :196], so there is one
  factory per quote asset and its terms are fixed at deployment. Every launch
  it makes opens identically; a second factory for a second B20 asset opens
  identically to ITSELF, and matches this one only if it is deployed with the
  same terms. Nothing here measures two factories - this was measured at two
  taxes on one - and stating it as "every CURB launch" would have been a
  universal drawn from a single deployment.
  [test_cross_eachPoolOpensAtItsOwnCurvesPrice]

  AND SINCE PRICE AND DEPTH CAN NO LONGER TELL THE TWO LAUNCHES APART, what
  proves the records stayed separate is that each locked position belongs to
  its own launch's creator.

F-5  ONE HOOK, TWO TAXES.                                        EACH ITS OWN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The sharpest seam in the system: one FeeHook serves every pool, keeping a
  frozen config per pool id and a fee total per TOKEN - one separated on
  purpose, one shared on purpose. A swap reading the wrong pool's config would
  pay the wrong creator at the wrong rate and every number involved would
  still look plausible.
  MEASURED with an identical 0.1 NVDAc swap into each pool: 50,000 raw to the
  100 bps launch's creator, 450,000 to the 500 bps launch's, neither creator
  paid by the other's pool, the platform paid both legs, and the hook's
  token-wide counter ending at exactly the two pools added together.
  [test_cross_oneHookChargesEachPoolItsOwnTax]

F-6  THE SAME CONFIGURATION LAUNCHED TWICE.                      TWO LAUNCHES.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Identical calldata, two different callers, and no collision: the token's
  CREATE2 address folds in the curve address, and the curve is made with
  CREATE at the factory's CURRENT NONCE, so the second launch derives a
  different address from the same salt. Both registry records are intact and
  each names its own launcher.
  [test_cross_relaunchingTheSameConfigCannotOverwriteTheFirst]

  PINNED BECAUSE IT IS NOT OBVIOUS. A reader who sees a caller-supplied salt
  reasonably expects reusing it to collide, and a future change that made the
  token address depend on the salt alone would turn that expectation into a
  way to grief a launch.

F-8  TWO GRADUATIONS IN ONE TRANSACTION.        INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM TWO.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  F's own closing note said every graduation above lands in a transaction of
  its own, so nothing that lives for the length of a TRANSACTION rather than a
  call had ever had a second graduation land on top of it. Uniswap v4 keeps
  its currency deltas and its unlock flag in transient storage, which is
  exactly that, and both graduations here settle the same currency.

  THE ASSERTION IS A COMPARISON, not a list of properties. A reference launch
  is graduated alone, in its own transaction, and the two sharing a
  transaction must come out identical to it. MEASURED: same opening price,
  same liquidity, each lock to its own creator, two distinct locks, nothing
  left on the Graduator and nothing paid to the caller for triggering it.
  [test_cross_twoGraduationsInOneTransactionMatchTwoSeparateOnes]

F-9  THE SAME THING WITHOUT ANYONE MEANING IT.  ALSO INDISTINGUISHABLE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Nobody sets out to graduate two launches at once. One transaction buys the
  last slice of two curves and each crossing buy graduates its own launch on
  the way out - an aggregator, a batcher or a bot filling two pending buys
  does this without intending to.

  AND THE GRADUATION IS NESTED HERE, which F-8's is not: it runs from inside
  buy()'s own reentrancy-guarded scope through _tryAutoGraduate, which is why
  graduate() is deliberately not nonReentrant. MEASURED against the same
  reference: both launches graduated, both pools at the reference price and
  depth, and the batching contract kept no quote and no launch token.
  [test_cross_twoCrossingBuysInOneTransactionGraduateBothCleanly]

  WHAT MAKES THESE TWO WORTH HAVING SEPARATELY: _tryAutoGraduate SWALLOWS a
  failed graduation so a problem at the pool cannot take an honest buyer's
  trade down with it. So F-8 catches a broken second graduation loudly, by
  reverting, and F-9 catches it SILENTLY - the launch simply does not
  graduate and the buy stands. The mutation below produced exactly that split.

  WHAT THEY CANNOT DO, said plainly. They verify an ABSENCE: that nothing
  couples two graduations inside one transaction. The only state the Graduator
  keeps between calls is `hasGraduated`, a mapping keyed by launch token, and
  every other value in graduate() is a local - so our own code has no
  transaction-scoped state for a mutation to break. These are therefore a
  TRIPWIRE rather than a proof: they go red the day someone adds caching to
  the Graduator or the hook, or the day v4's transient handling changes under
  us. They do not demonstrate that such a coupling would be caught in general.

F-10 THREE LAUNCHES, THREE CREATORS, ONE ESCROW.                 APART.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY THREE RATHER THAN TWO. With two launches, "A was not disturbed by B" is
  also what you would see if credits were landing in the wrong slot in a way
  that happened to hit B's. Three gives the mistake somewhere else to land,
  and the platform - which is SUPPOSED to accumulate from all of them - is the
  fourth party that tells accumulation apart from leakage.

  MEASURED at three different taxes, so no assertion can be passing because
  two numbers coincide: 30,303,030 raw at 100 bps, 154,639,175 at 300 and
  284,210,526 at 500. Each creator's credit checked after EACH of the other
  two graduations rather than once at the end; the escrow's balance equal to
  all four credits summed; and a claim by the MIDDLE creator reaching neither
  neighbour nor the platform.
  [test_cross_threeLaunchesKeepTheirCreditsApart]

F-11 THREE GRADUATIONS, ONE TRANSACTION, OUT OF REGISTRATION ORDER.  CLEAN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Launched A, B, C and graduated C, A, B, all in one transaction, against a
  reference launch graduated alone. MEASURED: every pool opened at its own
  price and matched the reference in price and depth, every lock went to its
  own creator, all three locks distinct, and nothing left on the Graduator.
  [test_cross_threeGraduationsOutOfOrderInOneTransaction]

  WHY THE ORDER IS THE WHOLE POINT. F-8 already put two graduations in one
  transaction, but two can only be done in one order or its reverse, so
  nothing there separates "handled independently" from "handled in the order
  they were registered". Three out of order is the case a lookup keyed on
  POSITION rather than on identity gets wrong - and the registry does keep an
  ordered _all array beside its mapping, so that is a plausible thing for
  someone to write later.

  SAME LIMIT AS F-8 AND F-9, and for the same reason: nothing in our code is
  keyed on position today, so this is a tripwire for a change that would
  introduce it rather than a demonstration that one would be caught. What it
  does establish is that graduation order is not load-bearing now.

  NOT RETESTED HERE: launchSlice, the one place that genuinely does read by
  position. It already has paging, type(uint256).max and out-of-range covered,
  and a third launch would have been repetition rather than coverage.

F-7  DO THESE HAVE TEETH. FOUR MUTATIONS, THREE CAUGHT, ONE CORRECTLY NOT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
    CAUGHT  the escrow overwrites a credit instead of adding to it  -> F-1 red
    CAUGHT  the burn takes the whole balance again (pre-A-4)        -> F-2 red
    CAUGHT  the hook charges one fixed tax, not the pool's own      -> F-5 red
    NOT     the quote refund never happens                          -> F-3 green

  The fourth is explained in F-3 and is the right outcome. The mutation run
  also caught a test rather than a bug: F-2's first version, which could not
  have gone red under any mutation at all, and was rewritten rather than kept.

  AND ONE MORE FOR F-8 AND F-9: the graduation flag keyed on a constant
  instead of on the launch token - the "state not keyed per launch" mistake -
  takes both of them red, in the two different ways the design makes possible.
  F-8 reverts AlreadyGraduated. F-9 reports that curve A simply did not
  graduate, because _tryAutoGraduate swallowed it, which is the failure that
  would be missed in production and is the reason F-9 exists as well as F-8.

  IT IS NOT A MUTATION ABOUT TRANSACTIONS, though, and should not be read as
  one: it breaks the two-transaction case just as thoroughly. See the last
  paragraph of F-9 for what these two tests do and do not establish.

G. CROSS-LAUNCH FUZZING - 2026-09-21
================================================================================
  Section F's closing note said what it left: "the orders tested are the ones
  I chose rather than any the machine found". This is the machine looking.

  THREE LAUNCHES, THREE CREATORS, THREE TAXES, ONE REAL ESCROW. Not the mock
  the single-curve invariants use - the whole question is whether several
  launches paying into one (recipient, token) ledger can reach each other's
  balances, and a mock that records what it is told cannot answer it.
  [test/CrossLaunchInvariants.t.sol]

  FOUR INVARIANTS, each 256 runs and 128,000 calls, all green:

    the escrow holds exactly what it owes - its balance equals the sum of
      every credit, so a mistake that credits the wrong recipient keeps the
      sum right but one that credits the wrong AMOUNT cannot;
    a credit only ever comes from the creator's own launch - checked against a
      flag the HANDLER sets, not against anything derived from the escrow;
    every curve's books balance, all three at once, including curves the
      fuzzer left untouched for a hundred calls while it worked on another;
    no curve breaches its reserved allocation, under any interleaving.

  NOTHING FAILED. Which is worth less than it sounds, and G-1 is why.

G-1  THE FUZZER HAD NEVER GRADUATED ANYTHING. IN EITHER SUITE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The new suite's coverage guard asked for one graduation and got ZERO out of
  128,000 calls. The same question put to the EXISTING single-curve suite - in
  place since before this review - returned zero as well, across all six of
  its campaigns.

  SO invariant_afterGraduationOnlyDonationsRemain HAS BEEN VACUOUS SINCE IT
  WAS WRITTEN. It asserts an identity about a state its campaign never
  reaches, and it has been reporting PASS for that reason.

  THE POST-GRADUATION REGIME IS NOT UNTESTED, and this would be a much worse
  finding if it were: test_postGraduationRegimeIsReachedAndHolds drives there
  deterministically and holds. What was missing is the FUZZED version - random
  sequences arriving at graduation - which is the only one that could find an
  ordering nobody thought of.

  WHY IT HAPPENED, and it is not a careless bound. buy() is capped at 1e7
  against a 10e8 threshold, so roughly a hundred NET buys are needed, and
  sell() gives the walk somewhere to go back to; a balanced random walk does
  not drift a hundred steps up. The cap is deliberate - the handler's own
  comment records that an earlier 5e8 version exhausted the allocation in two
  or three calls and left the sell path almost unreachable.

  THE FIX IS A SEPARATE ACTION, NOT A WIDER BOUND. fill() can finish a launch;
  buy() stays small; both are in the selector set. And fill() WAITS - it does
  nothing until the campaign has made three sells and one fee-paying sweep,
  because graduation closes both of those paths for good:
    gating on nothing        -> zero sells in every campaign;
    gating on sells alone    -> one campaign in six with zero sweeps;
    gating on both           -> every path exercised, and graduation reached.
  Each of those three lines was measured, in that order.

  AND BOTH SUITES NOW GUARD IT. A campaign that graduates nothing fails
  afterInvariant rather than passing quietly.

G-2  WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The fuzzed launches are CURVES, not full launches: no Graduator, no pool, no
  hook, no lock. It runs without a fork, which is what lets it do 128,000
  calls, and the price is that F-4's pool identities and F-8's shared
  transaction are out of its reach. The escrow, the books and the allocation
  are what it can see, and they are what it checks.

  THREE LAUNCHES IS STILL NOT MANY. Tens of launches are untested, and so is
  any launch count that changes as a campaign runs - every curve here exists
  before the first call.
