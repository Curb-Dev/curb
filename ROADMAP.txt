1. THESIS
================================================================================

Tokenized US equities are live on Base and doing real volume, but there is no
consumer destination. Everything that exists is infrastructure: DEXes,
aggregators, lending, vaults. There is no front end.

We close that gap in two phases:

  PHASE 1  Launchpad    - new tokens launch priced in B20 equities
  PHASE 2  Destination  - the existing 13 B20 stocks trade in the same venue

Phase 1 is the customer acquisition engine. Phase 2 is the retention and
monetization layer. Building Phase 1 on a venue we control makes Phase 2
possible without starting over - but not automatic and not free. Phase 2 is
gated on Phase 1 showing repeat use and net revenue against numbers frozen in
Phase 0.

[SHELVED 2026-10-18] Phase 2 is shelved by the owner's decision. Section 10,
under PHASE 2, says why and what replaces it; the thesis above is kept as it
was written.

4. WHY LAUNCHPAD FIRST
================================================================================

The cold start problem.

  If the trading destination opens first: nobody has a reason to move from
  Aerodrome or 1inch for 13 existing tokens. You compete on execution quality
  against aggregators. A losing game for a small team.

  If the launchpad opens first: the new token exists only on your venue.
  Exclusive inventory. And every launch is a marketing event - the launchpad
  itself is the content engine that feeds the live feed.

This is the pump.fun -> PumpSwap sequence: launchpad first, then your own
venue so you stop handing graduated volume to someone else.

5. GRADUATION: WHERE DOES THE TOKEN LAND
================================================================================

When the curve completes, where does the token graduate?

  OPTION A - Graduate to Aerodrome
    We hand the volume to Aerodrome and earn nothing from it afterwards.

  OPTION B - Graduate into a venue we control
    Graduated volume lands somewhere we capture fees.

[DECISION] Direction: Option B. Graduated volume should land somewhere we
capture fees, not on a venue we do not control.

[OPEN] The implementation. Three routes must be compared in Phase 0 E before
a line of contract code is written:
    (a) reuse existing infrastructure (Doppler, an existing hook)
    (b) adapt or fork an existing hook
    (c) write a custom hook
Cost, audit surface and time differ enormously between these. Do not assume
(c).

IMPORTANT CAVEAT: owning a hook does NOT guarantee order flow. A hook earns
fees on trades that hit THAT pool, and aggregators route to the best price.
For a freshly graduated memecoin our pool is usually the only pool, so capture
is real. For an established asset like NVDAc, Aerodrome already has the depth
and owning a hook buys us nothing. This is precisely why Phase 2 is a separate
product with its own liquidity problem, not a free extension.

Ref: https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/concepts/hooks

[BUILT] The graduation transaction itself, whichever route (a)/(b)/(c) the hook
question lands on. src/curb/Graduator.sol, src/curb/LaunchRegistry.sol,
test/fork/B20Graduation.t.sol, 10 passing against the REAL vendored bonding
curve driven to its threshold by real NVDAc buys on the pinned fork.

  SCOPE. Graduation moves the token the launch ALREADY created, plus the
  curve's reserves, into a pool. It does not create a token.

  ONE TRANSACTION: reserves leave the curve -> pool opens at the curve's final
  price -> LP position minted through the official PositionManager -> position
  locked for the creator -> remainder refunded. Between first and last the
  token is quotable by neither the curve nor the pool; splitting the steps
  would give that window a length.

  THE PRICE IS NOT THE RATIO OF WHAT THE CURVE HANDS OVER. This is the finding
  that matters most in this section. The curve PRICES against
      phantomQuote + trackedQuote - fees
  and can only TRANSFER trackedQuote. Measured on the fork:
      priced quote reserve   35.00000002 NVDAc
      transferable quote      5.00000002 NVDAc      difference = phantomQuote
      curve final price      40833333356
      pool opening price     40833333356            exact
      naive "ratio of what moved" price  5833333356
  A pool seeded at the transferable ratio would have opened at ONE SEVENTH of
  the curve's last price - an arbitrage gap handed to whoever is watching,
  paid for by everyone who bought on the curve. The opening price is therefore
  read from the curve's own getReserves() BEFORE graduation and the handed-over
  amounts deposited at that price.
    Asserted in BOTH currency orderings, launch token placed either side of
    NVDAc by CREATE2 on purpose, and the test checks that skipping the ordering
    step changes the answer - so it demonstrably depends on it.

  AUTHORISATION - A HOLE THAT WAS REAL. For graduation to move a curve's
  reserves the curve must authorise the graduator, and an ERC-20 allowance
  authorises the CONTRACT, not a caller of it. An earlier version took the
  beneficiary, refund address and lock duration as call ARGUMENTS, so a
  stranger could aim a graduation at someone else's funded curve and lock the
  LP position for themselves. Now every economic term comes from a write-once
  LaunchRegistry record and graduate() takes only the token address - there is
  nothing left for a caller to choose. The caller must also be the registry's
  launcher, which is defence in depth and costs liveness: see the contract.

  THE POOL NOBODY ELSE CAN OPEN. Refusing a pre-opened pool at the wrong price
  stops an attacker CHOOSING our opening price but not BLOCKING us - the key is
  taken permanently, a pool cannot be re-initialized, and the hook is part of
  the key. So FeeHook now holds BEFORE_INITIALIZE and only the graduator may
  open a pool carrying it. The attack stops being possible instead of becoming
  a permanent block, and the test shows graduation completing after an attempt.

  ROLLBACK, TESTED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. Atomicity is the EVM's; that the code
  strands nothing on the way is not. Two deliberate failures at different
  depths - a mint refused by a hostile hook, and a lock that cannot be
  constructed - both leave the curve's reserves untouched, no NFT, no pool.

  REACHED WITH PRODUCT CONTRACTS, NOT WITH vm.prank. An earlier version set up
  its starting state by impersonating the curve's factory role in the test.
  That made graduation testable from a state no product contract could produce.
  src/curb/CurbLaunchFactory.sol now creates the curve, the token, the curve's
  initialization and the permanent registry record in one real transaction, and
  every graduation test starts from its output.

  TWO DIFFERENT FAILURE KINDS, WHICH AN EARLIER VERSION CONFUSED
    A permanently broken configuration is NOT a retryable failure: the registry
    record cannot be rewritten, so a launch registered with terms that cannot
    graduate would take people's money and be stuck forever. Those are now
    refused at LAUNCH, before a single buy - a hook holding any liquidity
    permission, an unbounded lock duration, an illegal tick spacing.
    A TRANSIENT failure is the retryable one, and the test shows the same
    record graduating once the condition clears, with hasGraduated confirmed
    not to have survived the failed attempt.

  [OPEN] THE SUPPLY SPLIT IS A CURVE PARAMETER, NOT A PROPERTY OF GRADUATION
    An earlier revision reported "73.46% remainder" as if it were a fact about
    the design. It is what 30 NVDAc of phantom against a 5 NVDAc threshold
    produces, and those were chosen so the fork fixture would fit the NVDAc
    balance available to it. Fixture constraints are not product economics.

    MEASURED on the real curve, one real launch per row
    (test/fork/B20Graduation.t.sol):

      phantom/thr    r     price x   sold     LP    remainder  outside pool
        30 / 5     0.857    1.36x   14.28%  12.24%   73.46%      87.74%
        18 / 6     0.75     1.78x   25.00%  18.75%   56.24%      81.24%
         9 / 9     0.50     4.00x   50.00%  25.00%   24.99%      74.99%
         3 / 9     0.25    16.00x   75.00%  18.75%    6.24%      81.24%
         1 / 9     0.10   100.00x   90.00%   9.00%    0.99%      90.99%

    With r = phantom / (phantom + threshold):
      sold = supply(1-r)   LP = supply*r(1-r)   remainder = supply*r^2
      price multiplier across the curve = 1/r^2
    The last column is NOT "free float": it is what sits outside the pool, and
    only while the remainder goes to an ordinary address. The pool's own tokens
    are purchasable - locking the LP NFT locks the POSITION, not the tokens
    inside it.

    r IS NOT A FREE CHOICE. Shrinking the remainder means widening the gap
    between the first and last buyer on the curve: the multiplier is 1/r^2, so
    3/9's 6% remainder costs a 16x run where 9/9's 25% remainder costs 4x.

    THE THRESHOLD IS THE OTHER LEVER, AND IT IS NEARLY INDEPENDENT. The pool is
    seeded with what buyers actually paid, so LP depth in quote terms IS the
    threshold. Both candidates below were measured at a 9 NVDAc threshold and
    produced IDENTICAL price impact - depth, not the split, is what a trader
    feels.

[SUPERSEDED 2026-10-03 - THE CURVE IN THE DEPLOYMENT NOW]
  A launch opens at a market cap of about $4,000 and graduates at about
  $55,000 (the owner's call). Each asset has its own phantom V and threshold T,
  sized to those dollar values [DeployCurb.quoteAssets] - WETH 1.46 / 3.94,
  NVDAc 18.1 / 49.1 - so r = V/(V+T) is about 0.27: about 73% of the supply
  sells on the curve, about 20% opens the pool and about 7% is burned, and the
  curve's price runs about 13.7x from the first buyer to the last. The pool's
  quote depth is still the threshold. Dev buy capped at 20% of the supply; LP
  locked forever and no launch fee (2026-10-04). Everything below in this
  block is the record of the r = 0.5 shape it replaced.

[CANDIDATE - DEFAULT TEST PARAMETERS, NOT A LAUNCH DECISION]
  phantom = threshold = 60 NVDAc, i.e. r = 0.5.

  Labelled DECIDED in an earlier revision. It was not: it is the candidate the
  comparison converged on and the value the test suite now runs at. Running the
  tests at these numbers and shipping a launch at them are different acts, and
  only the first has happened.

  WHAT IT BUYS, MEASURED, NOT ARGUED
    supply       50% sold on the curve / 25% into the LP / 25% remainder
    curve price  runs 4x from the first buyer to the last
    pool depth   60 NVDAc of quote
    trade cost   a 1 NVDAc buy costs 299 bps against the opening pool, inside
                 the 3% target and asserted in the test suite

  WHAT THE TARGET IS AND IS NOT. 3% is an OPENING-STATE EXECUTION cost target:
  against the pool's spot price immediately before the trade, including the
  0.30% LP fee and the 1% hook fee. It is not a price guarantee for any later
  moment, and it excludes gas, a USDC->NVDAc conversion on the way in, and the
  price moving while a transaction waits.

  THE DEFAULT TEST FIXTURE IS NOW THESE PARAMETERS. It used to be 30/5, chosen
  because that fitted the small NVDAc holder - which let a fixture constraint
  masquerade as product economics in every number the suite reported. It needs
  a larger holder, and that is the right way round.

  HOW BIG DOES THE POOL HAVE TO BE - THE SAME TRADES AT DIFFERENT THRESHOLDS
    A trade sized as a PERCENTAGE of depth costs the same percentage wherever
    depth is set. That is why the earlier table showed two candidates with
    identical impact and why it could not answer this question. A user does not
    scale their order to our pool; they buy some amount. Measured, r = 0.5,
    effective cost in bps (includes the 0.30% LP fee and the 1% hook fee):

      pool depth     0.05 NVDAc      0.25 NVDAc       1.00 NVDAc
                    buy    sell     buy    sell      buy     sell
       2 NVDAc      383     369     1394   1223      5181    3413
       4 NVDAc      257     251      762    708      2656    2099
       8 NVDAc      194     190      447    427      1394    1223

    Doubling depth roughly HALVES the excess over the fee floor. About 130 bps
    of every figure is the two fees and does not fall with depth at all.

    THE CLOSED FORM, WHICH THE MEASUREMENTS MATCH EXACTLY
        cost  =  1/(0.997 x 0.99) - 1  +  T / (0.99 x depth)
      The first term is the two fees: 131.404 bps, and it does not fall with
      depth. Measured against the chain at 60 NVDAc depth and a 1 NVDAc trade:
      299 bps predicted, 299 bps measured. At 28 NVDAc: 492 and 492.

      SOLVED FOR DEPTH, AND THE 0.99 BELONGS IN IT:
          depth  >=  T * 10,000 / (0.99 * (C - 131.40431395...))
      with C the limit in bps. Dropping that 0.99 - which one summary of this
      work did - returns 59.31 for a 3% limit instead of 59.91, and so breaks
      the limit it is solving for. For a 1 NVDAc trade:
          <= 3%  needs 59.9126 NVDAc        <= 5%  needs 27.4040 NVDAc
      Scaling: T = 0.5 NVDAc at 3% needs 29.96; T = 2 NVDAc at 3% needs 119.83.

      ROUNDING THOSE DOWN BREAKS THE LIMIT, and an earlier revision of this
      document did exactly that by quoting 59 and 27. Measured: 59 NVDAc costs
      302 bps and 27 NVDAc costs 505 bps. The test candidates are 60 and 28,
      and both limits are ASSERTED in test/fork/B20Graduation.t.sol rather than
      reported - together with two tests confirming 59 and 27 miss, so the
      assertions are load-bearing.

      AND THE LIMIT IS CHECKED BEFORE ROUNDING. Comparing a cost already
      rounded to whole basis points lets anything under limit+1 bps through:
      59.9 NVDAc of depth costs 300.0355 bps, reports as 300, and passed a
      300 bps assertion. The check now cross-multiplies the unrounded ratio,
      and that 59.9 case is a regression test - it must still REPORT 300 and
      still be rejected.

    WHAT "COST" COVERS, NARROWLY. Execution against the pool's spot price
    immediately before the trade, including the 0.30% LP fee and the 1% hook
    fee. NOT gas, NOT a USDC->NVDAc conversion on the way in, NOT the price
    moving while the transaction waits. A user arriving with USDC pays more
    than this and that total has not been measured.

    SO THE QUESTION IS NOT "9 OR 3". It is: what is the typical trade, and what
    cost is acceptable for it? The threshold follows from those two, and r
    stays free to be chosen for the supply split.

    AND THESE ARE TWO SEPARATE DECISIONS, NOT ONE.
      (1) the threshold, i.e. what a typical trade may cost
      (2) what happens to the remainder
    Choosing (1) does not approve (2). 60 and 28 are TEST CANDIDATES; neither
    is a chosen product parameter, and the remainder policy is still open.

  WHAT THE CANDIDATE COMPARISON DID AND DID NOT SHOW
    9/9 and 3/9 produced identical trade costs. That is narrower than "the same
    tradeability": it was measured at the pool's OPENING state, at those sizes,
    through a direct PoolManager call. The two leave very different amounts in
    buyers' hands - 50% of supply against 75% - so the selling pressure that
    can arrive afterwards is not the same, and nothing here measures that.

  [APPROVED - THE REMAINDER IS BURNED]  Approved by the project owner on
  13 September 2026. Implemented in src/curb/Graduator.sol.

    WHY BURN AND NOT THE OTHER TWO. Creator vesting and protocol inventory both
    create an address someone has to be trusted with, for a quarter of the
    supply. Burning creates none, and the float buyers were told about is the
    float that exists.

    IT IS NOT A PARAMETER. No per-launch setting routes the remainder anywhere
    else, so no launch can be configured wrong and nothing has to be validated
    at registration. The QUOTE remainder - usually zero - still goes to the
    record's refund address, because NVDAc is not ours to destroy.

    THE BURN IS REAL, AND CHECKED ON totalSupply. CurbLaunchToken.burn()
    destroys the caller's own tokens through ERC20._burn. Sending to address(0)
    or a dead address does not reduce totalSupply and would leave every reader
    of the supply quoting a float that does not exist.
      The graduator checks totalSupply fell by EXACTLY the amount burned. An
      earlier version only checked its own balance was empty afterwards - which
      a burn() that merely transferred the tokens elsewhere satisfies just as
      well, i.e. precisely the case the check existed to catch.
      TESTED WITH A BURN THAT ACTUALLY MOVES THE TOKENS, by etching a
      storage-layout-compatible implementation over the real launch token: the
      graduator is emptied exactly as an honest burn would empty it, and only
      totalSupply separates the two. Verified load-bearing by deleting the
      supply check and watching that test fail.
      A first attempt mocked burn() into a no-op, which does NOT express this
      adversary - the graduator is left holding the tokens, so the old
      balance-only check caught it by accident and the test proved nothing. It
      is kept as a second, weaker case, and it still passes with the supply
      check removed.

    MEASURED at the candidate parameters:
      supply at launch                1,000,000,000
      sold on the curve to buyers       500,000,000   50.00%
      into the LP position              250,000,000   25.00%
      BURNED                            250,000,000   25.00%
      supply after the burn             750,000,000   75.00% of launch supply
        held by buyers                                66.66% of what remains
        in the pool                                   33.33% of what remains

    AND IT UNWINDS. The burn is ordered LAST, after the quote refund. An
    earlier version burned first and the claim "nothing internal can fail after
    the burn" was then simply false - the refund was right there. Reordering
    makes the claim structural rather than an argument about which remaining
    step can revert.
      So a failure after the burn has to come from outside the call: a batch, a
      multicall, any caller that throws later.
      test_aFailureAfterTheBurnUnwindsTheBurn runs a complete graduation and
      then reverts, and the harness reports the supply either side of the burn
      in a custom error the test matches in full - an earlier version only
      asserted the outer call failed, which a graduation that reverted long
      before the burn would have satisfied while proving nothing. Everything
      comes back: totalSupply, the curve's allocation, the reserves, the closed
      flag, the NFT, hasGraduated. The same launch graduates afterwards.

    WHAT BURNING IS NOT. A supply change, not a price guarantee and not a
    "clean chart". The pool's own tokens stay purchasable - locking the LP
    position does not lock them out of the market.

6. CONTRACT ARCHITECTURE (draft)
================================================================================

6.1  LAYERING - what is in the critical path and what is not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This separation is load-bearing. It keeps oracle and router failures out of
the core.

  CORE - deterministic, no oracle, no swap
    BondingCurve and graduation operate in RAW B20 UNITS only.
    Per the PONS V2 design, the curve collects the quote asset of the pool it
    will graduate into from the start, so graduation requires neither a swap
    nor a price feed. VERIFY THIS AGAINST THE V2 SOURCE in Phase 0 A before
    depending on it.

  EDGE - convenience, outside the critical path
    QuoteRouter converts USDC/ETH in and out for users who do not already
    hold the B20 asset. If the router is unavailable, a user who already holds
    NVDAc can still trade directly against the curve.

  DISPLAY AND GUARDS - not consensus-critical
    PriceAdapter provides USD display and defined trade protections.

6.2  COMPONENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LaunchFactory      CREATE2, deterministic addresses. Freezes launch
                     parameters (quote asset, curve shape, fees, graduation
                     threshold). Immutable per launch.

  BondingCurve       Quote = a B20 equity token. Raw-unit accounting.
                     Full supply on the curve until the graduation threshold.

  Graduation         Into the venue chosen in Phase 0 E. Opens the position,
                     locks LP, enforces fee policy.

  QuoteRouter        Edge layer. USDC/ETH <-> B20 with a depth guard. See 7.1.

  FeeRouter          Protocol/creator split. One-way, no generic call.

  PriceAdapter       Chainlink feed + DEX TWAP. Display and guards. See 7.2.

6.3  SECURITY REVIEW SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Curve accounting: rounding, precision, raw-unit handling
  - Graduation: the transition itself, AND failed graduation
  - The venue integration: fee logic, LP lock, upgrade and authority surface
  - Fee distribution
  - QuoteRouter and its depth guard
  - Mass-sell / bank-run scenarios against both the curve and the graduated pool
  - B20 transfer restrictions - see the honest requirement below

  B20 TRANSFER RESTRICTIONS - what we can and cannot promise
    isAuthorized() and allow/blocklist capability exist in the standard, and
    the issuer can pause or restrict transfers.

    WE CANNOT GUARANTEE AN EXIT IN ALL CASES. If the issuer blocks the pool's
    B20 transfers, no contract of ours routes around that. Saying otherwise
    in the product or its marketing would be a promise we cannot keep.

    The honest requirement is:
      (a) a failed transfer must not corrupt accounting
      (b) retryable states must be supported
      (c) issuer-caused blockage must be surfaced explicitly to the user

  Ref: https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/security
  Ref: https://docs.base.org/build-on-base/integrate-defi/list-tokenized-stocks

6.4  GRADUATION FAILURE - designed in, not bolted on
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Graduation is the one moment where funds move between two systems. Its
failure path is a v1 requirement, not error handling to be added after
something goes wrong in production.

  [x] Pool creation must be RETRYABLE. Tested at three depths in
      test/fork/B20Graduation.t.sol: a transient failure before anything moves
      (the curve not yet at its threshold), an injected mint failure after the
      reserves have left the curve AND the pool has been initialized, and a
      revert from outside the call after the burn. In each case everything
      unwinds - reserves, the curve's closed flag, the pool, the NFT, the
      hasGraduated flag, the burn - and the SAME launch, on the same
      never-rewritten record, graduates afterwards.
      A PERMANENTLY broken configuration is a different thing and is refused at
      LAUNCH instead, before anyone can buy: there is no retry that fixes a
      record that cannot work, because the record cannot be rewritten.
  [x] SINGLE-SHOT PER LAUNCH, PROTECTED AGAINST DUPLICATE CREATION. Called
      "idempotent" in an earlier revision, which it is not: a second call
      REVERTS rather than returning the first result. hasGraduated refuses it;
      a pool already open at OUR price is accepted rather than duplicated, and
      one at any other price is refused; and the hook's beforeInitialize means
      nobody but the graduator can open the pool at all, so the racing case
      cannot start.
  [x] Where the funds sit is explicit at every step OF THE GRADUATION CALL,
      which is one transaction: the test asserts every unit that left the curve
      is in the PoolManager, refunded, or destroyed, with the graduator, the
      curve and the creator holding none of it.
      THAT IS NOT THE SAME AS "there is no transition", which an earlier
      revision claimed. See the state list below: the launch itself can sit
      between "ready" and "graduated".
  [ ] What the user can and cannot do mid-transition must be defined and
      shown: can they still sell on the curve, is the position frozen, for
      how long.
  [~] A graduation that cannot complete - for example an issuer-side B20
      restriction per 6.3 - must surface the reason rather than stall silently.
      TESTED AGAINST INJECTED FAILURES ONLY. No B20 issuer restriction was
      triggered on chain: we have no restricted address, so the live behaviour

  [x] THE STATES BETWEEN READY AND GRADUATED, tested in
      test/fork/B20Graduation.t.sol.

      NORMALLY THERE IS NO GAP. PonsV2BondingCurve calls factory.graduate() the
      instant a buy exhausts the allocation, so the crossing trade carries the
      migration: the pool opens, the position is minted and locked and the
      remainder burned, all inside that buy.

      THE GAP IS REAL WHEN THE AUTOMATIC ATTEMPT FAILS. The crossing buyer sets
      their own gas limit and can leave it too little under the 63/64 rule; the
      curve catches the failure, emits AutoGraduationFailed and lets the buy
      stand. The event carries no reason - the catch takes no data - so the
      event and the receipt say an attempt failed and nothing more. A trace is
      where a reason would have to come from. The launch is then READY BUT NOT
      GRADUATED, and in that state BOTH sides of the curve are shut - sell()
      reverts once readyToGraduate() is true, and there is no allocation left
      to buy. A holder can reach neither the curve nor a pool that does not
      exist yet.

      WHICH IS WHY GRADUATION IS PERMISSIONLESS. An earlier version restricted
      it to the registry's launcher. That bought nothing the write-once record
      did not already provide - graduate() takes only the token, so no caller
      can redirect anything - and it broke the curve's own design: its call was
      refused, and the party who most needs the state unstuck is a holder, not
      an operator. Tested: a stranger can end the wait, and the position still
      locks for the registered creator.

      THREE STATES, AND ONE EVENT THAT IS NOT A STATE.
        selling      readyToGraduate() false, graduated() false
        waiting      readyToGraduate() true,  graduated() false  <- name this
        graduated    graduated() true, hasGraduated[token] true

        AutoGraduationFailed is an EVENT, not a fourth state. An earlier
        revision listed it as one. A launch that graduated perfectly well on a
        later attempt still carries that event in its history, so an interface
        reading the log to decide what to show would report a healthy launch as
        failed. Current state comes from the two booleans; the event only says
        an attempt once failed.

7. THREE REAL B20-SPECIFIC PROBLEMS
================================================================================

7.1  QUOTE-ASSET LIQUIDITY  -- the hard scaling ceiling
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
     The user arrives with USDC; the curve wants NVDAc. Every buy through the
     router is two hops. About $3M of liquidity was seeded across all pairs at
     launch. NVDAc turns over $10.6M/day, but that is VOLUME, not depth.

     Risk: one popular launch routes $500K through USDC->NVDAc and moves the
     STOCK TOKEN ITSELF. NVDAc drifts off real NVDA. That is both an arbitrage
     opportunity and a reputational bomb. Our scaling limit is the quote
     asset's depth, not our curve.

     TWO SEPARATE GUARDS - do not merge these under "slippage guard"
     They protect against different things and fail in different conditions.

       1. EXECUTION GUARD (minimum output)
          Protects the user against the trade filling worse than quoted.
          Needs no external price: minimum received + deadline, checked
          against the actual fill. Works on weekends and with no oracle.

       2. REFERENCE-DEVIATION GUARD
          Protects against the B20 token itself being pushed off the real
          stock. Needs a verifiable reference price. This is the one that
          depends on the oracle, and the one that closes per 7.2 A.

     APPROACH - v1 keeps this simple:
       - Reference-deviation guard: if a buy would move NVDAc more than X bps
         off reference, REJECT it. No splitting, no time-slicing in v1.
       - Execution guard: always show minimum received, deadline and total
         cost before signing
       - Explicit warning on large buys - never silent slippage
       - MEASURE IN PHASE 0 B, both directions

     DEFERRED past v1: splitting or time-slicing large orders, multi-venue
     routing across Aerodrome + Uniswap + 1inch. Rejecting an oversized order
     is an acceptable v1 behaviour; silently executing it is not.

7.2  WEEKENDS, AFTER HOURS, AND ORACLE DEGRADATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
     Chainlink feeds run 24/5. They hold the last close through weekends and
     holidays, and freeze during corporate actions. Updates on 0.5% deviation
     or a 24h heartbeat. But the pool trades 24/7.

     On Saturday, which "NVDA price" do you show the user?
       Show Chainlink  -> you are lying, the market moved
       Show the DEX    -> honest, but it diverges from the "stock price" they expect

     As far as we can see nobody has solved this - a judgement from the
     venues we surveyed, not a verified fact. The problem itself is
     structural and Base-specific. Get it right and it is differentiation.
     Ignore it and you lose trust the first weekend.

     UI APPROACH:
       - Show both prices, labelled: "Last close" / "Onchain now"
       - Show the basis between them openly. Do not hide it.
       - Keep the after-hours state visible at all times

     DEGRADATION - define per layer, never globally
     "Halt trading" is too blunt. Each layer behaves differently:

     Two different failures get confused constantly. Keep them apart:
     an ORACLE stall is a price event; a B20 TRANSFER restriction is a token
     event. They have different blast radii.

     A. ORACLE STALL - stale feed, weekend, or a corporate action
        Chainlink stops updating the feed during a corporate action. That is
        a stale-price condition, not a token restriction, and it is handled
        exactly like any other stale feed.

       Price display      Show the last value with an explicit staleness
                          label and timestamp. Never present a stale price
                          as live.
       QuoteRouter        Needs a price reference for its slippage guard.
                          In v1, if a fresh reference cannot be verified:
                          CLOSE the router path. Do NOT widen the guard and
                          ask the user to confirm - consent is not a
                          substitute for a technical control. Closing an edge
                          layer does not stop the core.
       Direct B20 trades  A user holding NVDAc trading against the curve in
                          raw units needs NO oracle. This path stays open.

        PRODUCT CONSEQUENCE - face this, do not bury it. If the only
        reference is Chainlink, and Chainlink not updating at the weekend is
        normal and expected behaviour, then the USDC in/out path CLOSES
        EVERY WEEKEND. Direct B20 trading staying open does not solve this
        for a user who arrived with USDC and holds no NVDAc.

        [OPEN] PHASE 0 EXIT ITEM - two questions, one answer:
          (a) What verifiable price source does the reference-deviation
              guard use at the weekend? A DEX TWAP across venues? A widened
              band anchored to the last close? Nothing at all?
          (b) If no acceptable source exists, what exactly is offered to a
              USDC user on a Saturday - a clear "conversion paused until
              Monday", or a path with a different guard?
        Whatever the answer, the weekend behaviour must be a designed state
        with its own screen, not an error the user discovers.

     B. B20 TRANSFER RESTRICTION - a token-level event, far wider blast radius
        The issuer can pause or restrict transfers. This reaches the CORE -
        curve and graduation included - because no path can move the asset.
        Our contracts cannot override it.

       All layers         Surface the reason, mark the affected market, halt
                          new launches on that asset (Phase 1C), and never
                          claim an exit. See 6.3.

8. THE MULTIPLIER IS NOT THE PROBLEM IT LOOKS LIKE
================================================================================

B20 handles corporate actions through an onchain multiplier (ERC-8056
uiMultiplier): the raw balance stays fixed and the multiplier adjusts the
shares-per-token ratio. Total-return logic.

  4:1 split  -> multiplier goes to 4x, per-share price falls to 1/4
             -> TOKEN PRICE STAYS CONTINUOUS
  Dividend   -> multiplier rises, token price stays continuous

So AMM math and curve accounting DO NOT break. Base designed it this way
("positions already deployed in DeFi do not break").

The remaining issues are DISPLAY and one arithmetic trap:
  - NEVER write "1 NVDAc = 1 NVDA share" in the UI. It is a total-return wrapper.
  - Transfers emit both raw and UI-adjusted values (TransferWithScaledUI)
  - Keep raw-unit accounting in the core; the multiplier is a display concern

WHEN TO APPLY THE MULTIPLIER - three cases, and the middle one is the trap

  1. Computing a token price from a RAW SHARE price  -> APPLY the multiplier
  2. Using the Coinbase B20 Chainlink feed           -> DO NOT APPLY IT
     That feed already reports the multiplier-adjusted token price.
     Multiplying it again produces a wrong number that looks plausible.
  3. Computing represented SHARES from a token balance -> APPLY it

"Apply the multiplier in the display layer" is not a sufficient rule on its
own, because case 2 lives in the display layer too. Write the source of each
price into the code and pick the rule from the source, not from the layer.

MEASURED (11 Sep 2026, NVDAc on Base mainnet):
    multiplier()    -> 1000000000000000000   (1e18, currently 1.0)
    uiMultiplier()  -> reverts, 0xa60bf13d

The accessor is multiplier(). uiMultiplier() IS declared in the current
base-std IB20Asset interface but the live network does not answer it - a
source-version vs deployed-version mismatch. Use multiplier(), and pin which
interface version matches the deployment before depending on either.

The total-return logic above is unaffected; only the function name was wrong.

Small distinction, but it matters legally.

     hide a divergence between them. Note the mock also differs in code

     How base-std actually chooses the mode (verified against current source):
       - setUp() runs _detectLivePrecompiles() FIRST - a STATICCALL to
         ActivationRegistry.admin(). A live precompile returns a 32-byte
         address; stock forge returns zero-length returndata.
       - If live precompiles are detected it returns BEFORE any vm.etch.
         Nothing is overwritten.
       - Only in reference mode does it etch the mocks at their addresses.
       - LIVE_PRECOMPILES=true forces live mode and bypasses detection. It
         is an escape hatch for when auto-detection fails, not the only path.

     THE FLAG ALONE IS NOT ENOUGH. Base's own guide is explicit that it "does
     NOT deploy precompiles - it only signals the test suite to expect them."
     The precompiles must already exist in the EVM: base-forge hosting them
     in-process (needs base-anvil, a patched foundry with --base), or a node
     RUNNING base-anvil --base with the required features activated.
     Install is a prebuilt-binary script; the lld / mold linker requirement
     in Base's guide applies to BUILDING base-anvil from source, and is not
     a general prerequisite.

     "An anvil node" is not precise enough and would waste a day: stock
     Anvil does not host Base precompiles. It must be base-anvil, started
     with --base, with the feature IDs registered. Setting the flag against
     a plain fork or a stock Anvil proves nothing.

11. FEE MODEL - BENCHMARKS AND PHASE 0 EXIT ITEM
================================================================================

  CORRECTION - "1% protocol fee" was wrong. Verified against
  PonsV2BondingCurve.sol on 11 Sep 2026:

    feeBps is the BASE TRADE FEE, and the protocol gets only a share of it:
      uint256 protocolAmount = (pending * protocolFeeShareBps) / BASIS_POINTS;
      uint256 creatorBucket  = pending - protocolAmount;
    The remainder goes to buyback (if enabled) and then the creator:
      uint256 buybackAmount = executeBuyback ? min(buybackQuoteBalance, creatorBucket) : 0;
      uint256 creatorAmount = creatorBucket - buybackAmount + tax;

    creatorTaxBps is SEPARATE and LAYERED. From the source comment: "Kept
    entirely separate from feeBps: it is layered on top of the base trade
    fee, not part of the protocol/buyback/creator split, and is paid to the
    creator in full."

  SO: "a 1% base trade fee" and "the platform earns 1%" are NOT the same
  thing. Both figures are in basis points, so BOTH need converting:

    gross protocol revenue from this fee line
      = fee base x (feeBps / 10,000) x (protocolFeeShareBps / 10,000)

    Worked example: a 1% base fee (feeBps = 100) with a 20% protocol share
    (protocolFeeShareBps = 2,000) leaves the protocol 0.20% of the trade.
    On a $1,000,000 fee base that is $2,000 gross - before any costs.

  Model revenue on that formula, not on the headline rate. Treat every rate
  below as a REFERENCE VALUE to be re-verified against live deployment
  settings, not as fact.

  SECOND-ORDER CONSEQUENCE: the Phase 2 entry gate requires "positive revenue
  after costs." Since revenue is a fraction of a fraction of volume, the
  volume needed to clear that gate is proportionally higher than a headline
  rate suggests. Set the Phase 0 numeric targets from this formula.

  REFERENCE RATES (verify against live deployments before relying on them)
  PONS V2   : base trade fee feeBps, split by protocolFeeShareBps;
              creatorTaxBps 0-300 layered on top and paid to the creator
  Clanker   : creator picks a 1-3% pool fee, Clanker takes 20% of LP fees
  Flaunch   : launch fee of 0.1% of market cap, default 80/20 creator/buyback

  PONS model (explicit per-trade fee) or Clanker model (a cut of LP fees)?
  The first is transparent and predictable; the second is invisible to the
  user but produces ongoing revenue from graduated volume.

  This is a PHASE 0 EXIT ITEM. Decide the split, and compute expected
  platform revenue from the split - not from the headline rate.

  WHAT THE FEE IS DENOMINATED IN - OPEN, AND NOT A DETAIL
  Measured 11 Sep 2026, test/fork/B20FeeHook.t.sol. A hook that charges on the
  OUTPUT side charges in a DIFFERENT TOKEN depending on trade direction:

    buy  (NVDAc -> MEME)   fee arrives as MEME
    sell (MEME  -> NVDAc)  fee arrives as NVDAc

  Back to back through one pool, the platform ended the test holding 9,871 raw
  MEME and 10,068 raw NVDAc. Those do not add up to anything. Summing them into
  one revenue figure would be an arithmetic error dressed as a metric.

  AND COLLECTING MEME IS NOT EARNING SPENDABLE REVENUE. The memecoin side of
  every pair is the token we just launched: thin, volatile, and priced by the
  very curve we operate. A fee taken in it is a claim whose value depends on
  being able to sell it, into liquidity we also provide. The NVDAc side is the
  part that behaves like revenue.

  THREE OPTIONS, NONE DECIDED
    a. charge on the output regardless of side   - simplest, what is built now,
                                                   leaves the platform long the
                                                   memecoin on every buy
    b. always charge in the quote asset (NVDAc)  - real revenue on both sides;
                                                   needs the fee taken from the
                                                   INPUT on a buy, which is a
                                                   different hook
    c. charge on output, sell the memecoin       - keeps the hook simple and
       side on a schedule                          moves the problem to
                                                   treasury operations, with
                                                   its own price impact
  DECIDE THIS BEFORE ANY REVENUE NUMBER IS QUOTED, including in Phase 2's
  "positive revenue after costs" gate. Per-token accounting is already in the
  hook so the two streams can never be silently merged.

  WHAT THE FEE HOOK TEST DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
    ESTABLISHED  the collection MECHANISM works: the fee leaves the pool, lands
                 in the recipient's real balance, is taken from the gross
                 output, rounds down in the trader's favour, is bounded by the
                 trader's minimum-output check on the NET, and survives nothing
                 when the swap reverts. Exact-output swaps are refused rather
                 than charged on the wrong side.
    NOT ESTABLISHED  volume, the realisable value of what is collected, and
                 whether any of it covers costs. One pool, one block, 1e6 raw
                 per swap, on a fork. No claim about economics follows from it.

GOVERNING RULE - DO NOT INVENT WHAT ALREADY WORKS
  We are building our own launchpad; PONS V2 is the reference we read before
  writing. Reuse is decided file by file in REUSE_ANALYSIS.txt, not by a
  blanket fork or a blanket rewrite.

  Where we do diverge from PONS, the reason gets written down. "We would do
  it differently" is not a reason. Do NOT redesign the curve, graduation and
  fee system at the same time - change one thing, keep the others known.

  NOTE: PONS ships no build or test infrastructure (no foundry.toml, no
  remappings.txt, no *.t.sol). "Run its existing flow first" is not
  available; a compiling workspace and tests we write are step one.

ORDER OF WORK
  1. Run the PONS V2 flow as-is and understand it end to end
  2. Produce the Base/B20 adaptation diff - what must change, and why
  3. Build the complete lifecycle on ONE quote asset:
       launch -> buy -> sell -> graduation -> trade in pool -> fee claim
  4. Only then add further stocks
  5. Minimal interface, with the discovery screen as its core
  6. Public launch with three names total and a prepared calendar

WHAT MUST STAY INDEPENDENT OF DOLLAR PRICE
  The graduation THRESHOLD and all accounting run on raw B20 units. If the
  threshold is denominated in dollars, the oracle becomes a core dependency,
  and a moving stock price or a stalled feed starts affecting the mechanism
  itself. Dollars belong in the interface only. This is the practical
  consequence of the layering in 6.1.

FIRST TECHNICAL MILESTONE - acceptance criteria, before the full lifecycle
  One narrower thing has to be demonstrably true first: token approval and
  transfer working correctly against a REAL B20 asset. Show all five.

  [ ] Pinned source and tool versions - PONS commit, base-anvil / base-forge,
      foundry, solc - plus the exact command and the runtime environment
  [ ] The live-precompile mode log line from setup
  [ ] The measured codesize at the B20 address under test. Mainnet says 1
      (0xef). This doubles as an environment sanity check: if your test
      environment reports 0 or a large number, it is not serving real B20.
  [ ] BEHAVIOURAL results at that same address, asserted on state - not on
      "it did not revert":
        - decimals() returns the expected value
        - after approve(), allowance() reflects it
        - after transfer(), BOTH sender and recipient balances moved by the
          expected amounts
  [ ] setPairTokenApproved() ACCEPTS a real B20 address on the UNMODIFIED
      contract.

      DO NOT assume a revert here means the environment is broken. Read from
      the source at debbc21f - the function has FIVE distinct failure modes:

        1. onlyOwner                                    (caller)
        2. pairToken == address(0)  -> PairTokenValidationFailed
        3. pairToken.code.length == 0 -> PairTokenValidationFailed
        4. economics.phantomQuote == 0 || graduationThreshold == 0
                                    -> PairTokenEconomicsInvalid
        5. _requireDecimals mismatch -> PairTokenDecimalsMismatch(exp, actual)
           or, if decimals() is unreadable -> PairTokenDecimalsUnavailable

      Two consequences that will bite:
        - PairTokenEconomics MUST be configured BEFORE approval. A naive
          setPairTokenApproved(NVDAc, true) reverts with
          PairTokenEconomicsInvalid and has nothing to do with B20.
        - NVDAc has decimals = 8, not 18 (measured). Set economics.decimals
          to 8 or _requireDecimals reverts with PairTokenDecimalsMismatch.
          This is CONFIGURATION, not an algorithm change: the maths works in
          raw token units and PairTokenEconomics already carries a per-asset
          decimals field. Configure NVDAc amounts at the 10^8 scale. The
          18-vs-8 gap is 10^10; the "twelve-order-of-magnitude mispricing"
          in the source comment is that comment's own example, not our case.
          The mis-scaling risk is real - the conclusion that the existing
          maths is broken does not follow.

      Diagnose the actual error before concluding anything about the
      environment or about B20.

  [ ] It STILL REJECTS an EOA and the zero address. Whatever we change later
      must keep rejecting what should be rejected - a suite that only proves
      acceptance would also pass if the check were simply deleted.
  [ ] Any revert asserted by SELECTOR, never by "it reverted".
      CAVEAT: cases 2 and 3 share the selector PairTokenValidationFailed, so
      the selector alone does not tell you which branch fired. To exercise
      the code-length branch specifically you need a non-zero address with
      no code, and you still cannot distinguish it by selector - distinguish
      it by the input you chose.

  THE LOG LINE IS NOT SELF-VALIDATING. LIVE_PRECOMPILES=true bypasses
  detection and prints the same line, so the log can claim live mode in an
  environment that has no precompiles. These criteria only prove anything
  TOGETHER: the log, plus the measured codesize, plus real calls returning
  correct state. Any one alone is circular or insufficient.

FIRST DELIVERABLE - the definition of done for this stage
  Our launchpad running the complete lifecycle on a single B20 quote asset
  in a test environment, with a minimal interface over it - built from
  whichever PONS parts REUSE_ANALYSIS.txt justifies plus our own code.

  Not a bigger document. Not three quote assets. Not the onramp.
  One asset, one working loop, something you can click.

  Every subsequent decision gets made against that running version.
