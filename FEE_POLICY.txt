================================================================================
CURB - THE FEE POLICY, DECIDED
================================================================================

THE DECISION, AS THE CODE NOW HOLDS IT
  Every buy and every sell carries ONE tax, between 1% and 5%, and the CREATOR
  picks the number for their own launch. Of that tax the platform takes a share
  P of the trade base; everything above it goes to the creator. Raising the tax
  does not raise the platform's share - it only raises the creator's.

    tax T, chosen per launch, 100 <= T <= 500 bps
    platform  =  P bps of the base, frozen per launch - 50 at deployment
    creator   =  T - P bps

  P IS NOT FIXED ACROSS LAUNCHES. This decision was first written as "a FIXED
  0.5%", "50 bps of the base, always", and that is not what was built. P is
  the fee hook's platformFeeBps. Its owner (the policy owner, DEPLOY.txt
  decision 3) can change it FOR FUTURE LAUNCHES ONLY: announce, wait
  POLICY_CHANGE_DELAY (24 hours), execute inside POLICY_CHANGE_WINDOW (7 days),
  anywhere from 0 up to maxPlatformFeeBps() = 80 bps, a cap that keeps the
  creator at no less than a fifth of the lowest tax [FeeHook.sol,
  announcePlatformFeeBps / executePlatformFeeBps]. launch() reads P once and
  freezes it, with the recipient, into the launch's record; the curve and the
  pool both charge that copy, so a launch's P never moves after it is created
  [CurbLaunchFactory.launch, the policy.platformFeeBps() read].

  Charged in THE LAUNCH'S QUOTE ASSET on both sides - WETH, USDC, a
  Coinbase-wrapped coin, VIRTUAL or AERO, or a tokenized stock: fourteen since
  2026-10-15 [DeployCurb.quoteAssets]. NVDAc, in the figures below, was the
  first and is one of fourteen:
    on a BUY   the base is the quote INPUT
    on a SELL  the base is the GROSS quote OUTPUT
  The same policy applies on the curve and in the graduated pool, so a trader's
  cost does not change shape at graduation.

  The earlier 0.05% and 0.005% additional cuts are DROPPED. They do not exist
  in this policy.

  THE LP FEE IS NOT PART OF THE TAX and is never folded into it. It is the
  pool's own fee tier, it exists only after graduation, and it is shown on its
  own line everywhere a cost is displayed.

WHAT THIS FILE IS
  The current fee behaviour read out of the contracts, the proposed behaviour
  beside it, and the implementation and test scope that follows. No contract is
  changed by this file. Line references were checked against the files while
  writing; the ones that drifted are replaced by the name of what they point
  at, which does not drift.
  WHERE A LATER SECTION SAYS 50 BPS, OR "FIXED", read it as P at its starting
  value: sections 2 to 7 were written while P was a constant, and THE DECISION
  above says what it is now.

3. HOW IT MAPS ONTO THE CURVE - NO NEW CURVE CODE
================================================================================
The curve already has the exact shape this policy needs. It is a
CONFIGURATION, not a rewrite:

    feeBps               = P           the platform's share, frozen per
                                       launch (50 at deployment)
    protocolFeeShareBps  = 10000       all of it to the protocol
    creatorTaxBps        = T - P       the creator's share, chosen per launch

  WHY THAT LANDS EXACTLY RIGHT
    - the base fee and the creator tax are charged on the same base and summed
      independently, so the total is T
    - with the share at 10000 the creator's slice of the BASE fee is zero, and
      the creator is paid entirely through the tax - which is the leg that is
      already "paid to the creator in full" and never enters the split
    - the buyback earmark is computed from the creator's slice of the base fee,
      which is now zero; and buyback is already off by construction
      [CurbLaunchFactory.launch, the literal `false` it passes the curve
      for buybackEnabled, and see PRE_MAINNET E1], so that path stays
      dead rather than newly dead
    - both caps allow it: protocolFeeShareBps is rejected only ABOVE 10000
      [PonsV2BondingCurve.sol:206], and feeBps + creatorTaxBps is capped at
      2000 bps [:215], well above our 500

  DONE. The factory no longer takes a rate at all: it takes ONE number,
  totalTaxBps, and derives the two legs from it -
      feeBps        = the hook's platformFeeBps AT LAUNCH (50 at deployment)
      creatorTaxBps = totalTaxBps - that share
      the snapshot   built inside launch(), with the share at 10000 and the
                     recipient = the hook's platformRecipient AT LAUNCH
  (SUPERSEDED WORDING: this block said feeBps was the constant 50 and the
  recipient the factory's own immutable. Both have been read off the hook, per
  launch, since the fee policy became settable.)
  and it refuses anything outside 100..500 with TaxOutOfBand. The band itself
  is defined in the REGISTRY, read by the factory at construction so the two
  cannot drift, and enforced again when the record is written - a future
  launcher does not get to widen it.
    The registry's error is TaxOutOfRange and the factory's is TaxOutOfBand,
    deliberately different names. Identical names with identical argument types
    share a selector, a test asserting on one is satisfied by the other, and
    deleting the factory's check left the suite green. A mutation run found
    that; the rename is the fix.

4. HOW IT MAPS ONTO THE POOL - BUILT
================================================================================
DONE: a new FeeHook at a new mined address, and the graduator binds each pool to
its launch in the transaction that opens it. What follows is what the plan said,
with DONE notes for what it turned into and what it taught.

The hook could not express this policy as it stood, for two independent reasons.

  A. IT CHARGES THE WRONG SIDE ON A BUY
     The policy says the base is NVDAc on both sides. In the pool:
       sell  MEME -> NVDAc   NVDAc is the OUTPUT - the hook already does this
       buy   NVDAc -> MEME   NVDAc is the INPUT - the hook cannot reach it
     afterSwap can only return a delta on the UNSPECIFIED currency, and for an
     exact-input swap the unspecified side is the output. Charging the input
     needs beforeSwap with a beforeSwapDelta on the specified currency, which
     means the BEFORE_SWAP and BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA permissions the hook
     did not have then [DeployCurb mined BEFORE_INITIALIZE, AFTER_SWAP and
     AFTER_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA; it now mines BEFORE_SWAP and
     BEFORE_SWAP_RETURNS_DELTA too - the `flags` in DeployCurb.run()].
     A permission change changes the hook's ADDRESS, because v4 encodes
     permissions in the address. So this is a new hook deployment and a new
     requiredHook in the registry - not an edit to a live one.

  B. THE RATE IS ONE IMMUTABLE NUMBER FOR EVERY LAUNCH
     feeBps is set once in the hook's constructor. T is per launch. The hook
     needs per-pool configuration, and the honest way to get it is for the
     graduator to write the launch's T into the hook keyed by pool id at
     graduation, from the registry record that already fixed it - one write,
     then one cold read per swap. The alternative, having the hook call out to
     the registry inside every swap, puts an external call in the swap path for
     a value that never changes.

     DONE, AND NARROWER THAN PLANNED. configureLaunch takes a token and a KEY
     and reads the rate and the creator out of the REGISTRY itself, so the
     caller supplies neither and has nothing to get wrong or to lie about. Only
     the graduator may call it; the key must be that launch's own pool; and a
     second call with the same record is a no-op, because a write-once record
     cannot produce different values. If graduation reverts afterwards the
     binding reverts with it - which is what lets the same launch be completed
     by a later attempt, and there is a test for exactly that.

  C. THINGS THAT MUST BE DECIDED WHILE DESIGNING IT, NOT DISCOVERED AFTER
     - EXACT-OUTPUT SWAPS. The hook refuses them today rather than mis-charge
       [FeeHook.beforeSwap and afterSwap, error
       ExactOutputNotSupported]. Keep refusing, or handle
       them? Anyone can swap against
       the pool; our own interface only sends exact-input.
     - SKIMMING THE INPUT CHANGES THE PRICE THE TRADER GETS. Take T off the
       NVDAc first and the swap prices the remainder, so the trader receives
       less MEME than an untaxed swap - which is correct and is what "the base
       is the NVDAc input" means, but the quote on screen must be built the
       same way or it will be wrong by T.
     - THE ETH ROUTE CHARGES ON THE NVDAc LEG. ETH->MEME is wrap, a V3
       WETH/NVDAc swap that is not ours, then NVDAc->MEME on our pool. The tax
       lands on the NVDAc that reaches our pool, which is the right base - and
       the interface must show it as a NVDAc figure rather than implying a
       percentage of the ETH sent.
     - A POOL FROM A LAUNCH CREATED BEFORE THIS CHANGE would have no T written
       for it. Since nothing is deployed to mainnet this is a fork-only
       concern today, but the hook must fail closed rather than charge zero.
       DONE - PoolNotConfigured, in beforeSwap AND afterSwap.

  D. DECIDED WHILE BUILDING, AND WORTH KEEPING
     - EXACT-OUTPUT IS STILL REFUSED, by its own error, in both entry points.
     - EACH LEG IS AN INDEPENDENT FLOOR of the base, never a split of a
       combined total. That is precisely what keeps the platform's P
       identical at every tax - splitting a total would let rounding move it -
       and the trader keeps both remainders, so the two together can only ever
       be at or below base x T.
     - WHAT A RECEIPT CANNOT SETTLE, AND THE PAGE NOW SAYS SO. When the
       platform recipient and the creator are the SAME address - which they are
       on a launch whose creator is the deployer - the chain records two
       payments to one payee, and matching them cannot tell 50/250 from
       250/50. The TOTAL is checkable; the split between the legs is not.
       Found by a mutation that lied about the platform's rate and was NOT
       caught, which is the only reason anyone would know. The page prints the
       limit instead of implying it verified something it could not, and the
       test lies about the total instead.
     - THIS HOOK DOES NOT SUPPORT PARTIAL FILLS ON A BUY, and reverts the
       whole transaction rather than charge for input that was not spent. The
       overcharge it replaces was real, not hypothetical: beforeSwap charges on
       the amount the trader ASKS to spend, and at that moment nobody knows
       whether the pool will take all of it. Measured on the fork with a limit
       a tenth of a percent away: 1,000,000 NVDAc requested, 100,301 actually
       taken, 869,699 returned - and the old code taxed the whole million.
         A CUSTOM PRICE LIMIT IS NOT THE ONLY WAY TO GET A PARTIAL FILL. Our
         own interface supplies none, and that does NOT guarantee a full fill:
         a trade large enough to run the price to the range's own bound stops
         there too, with no limit involved, and is refused the same way. The
         pool is open to anyone besides.
         WHY REFUSE RATHER THAN REFUND, SCOPED TO THIS DESIGN. The fee is taken
         in beforeSwap; afterSwap is the first point a partial fill is visible,
         and it may only return a delta on the UNSPECIFIED currency, which for
         an exact-input swap is the output. Handing input back would mean
         taking the fee somewhere else - a different arrangement, not a missing
         line. It is not a claim that no hook could ever refund.
         A SELL NEEDS NO SUCH GUARD - its tax is taken in afterSwap from the
         NVDAc that actually came out, so a partial fill is already charged on
         exactly what happened. There is a test for that too.
     - A CLAIM I MADE ABOUT THESE TESTS WAS WRONG, AND THE EXPERIMENT THAT
       SETTLED IT IS script/b20/zerofee-experiment.sh.
         Splitting _legs() out of _pay() dropped `charged = platformAmount +
         creatorAmount`, so _pay returned zero and THE POOL CHARGED NOTHING.
         The commit that fixed it said "every fee test still passed" while that
         was true of the code. That was an INFERENCE, never an observation: the
         bug and the fill guard arrived in the same patch, so the only run that
         ever existed had both, and the failures it produced all came from the
         guard - which is what I read.
         RUN APART, the answer is the opposite. With the summing line removed
         AND the fill guard removed, ALL NINE fee tests fail, each naming the
         money: "the platform's leg is wrong: 0 != 5000", "the hook took
         nothing", "not a fixed 50 bps: 0 != 50000". With the guard left in,
         eight of them fail through its revert instead and one -
         aSellIsChargedOnTheGrossOutput, which never touches the buy-side
         guard - still fails on the money.
         So the tests were never blind to a zero fee. What happened is that two
         changes landed together and I attributed the failures to the newer
         one. A sequence is not a cause, and this file should not have said it
         was.
     - THE BUY'S BASE IS MEASURED FROM THE RECEIPT, not taken from the form.
       On the ETH route the v4 leg's input is whatever the v3 leg produced, and
       passing the amount the person typed put an ETH-scale number into an
       8-decimal calculation - a tax five thousand times too large. It was
       caught because the payments then did not reconcile, which is what that
       check is there for.

5. IMPLEMENTATION SCOPE
================================================================================
In order. Nothing starts before the line above it is done.

  5.1  FACTORY: ENFORCE THE BAND - DONE
       Validate at creation, revert with a named error carrying the offending
       value. Three checks: the platform rate is the one the caller was shown
       (it was "the fixed platform rate" while it was a constant), the 1%-5%
       band on the total, and the snapshot's share. Smallest change, largest immediate
       effect, and it is what makes every later test meaningful.

  5.2  A NEW FEE HOOK - DONE
       beforeSwap + beforeSwapDelta for the buy side, afterSwap for the sell
       side, per-pool T written by the graduator at graduation, fail closed on
       an unconfigured pool. New permissions mean a new mined address; the
       registry's requiredHook moves with it.

  5.3  THE GRADUATOR WRITES T - DONE
       One call at graduation, from the registry record. It must be part of the
       same transaction that opens the pool, so a pool can never exist in a
       state where the hook does not know its rate.

  5.4  THE INTERFACE - DONE
       Three lines wherever a cost is shown - platform, creator, LP - never one
       merged number, on the curve quote, the pool quote and the ETH route. The
       receipt-based reporting that already exists reads what actually moved,
       so these become three reconciled figures rather than three predictions.

  5.6  THE POLICY IS REAL TOO, AND THE DEPLOY PATH HAS NO TEST CODE IN IT
       CLOSED. Nothing in sections 1 to 6 of this file is outstanding: the
       curve and the pool both charge the decided tax, the escrow pays it out,
       the policy publishes it, and the deploy path builds all of it without
       test code. What follows this section is what was never in it.
       THE ONE THING THIS WORK HANDED FORWARD is the address the platform is
       paid at. It is required rather than inferred, and each launch freezes
       the one in force when it is created - so choosing it is an OPERATIONAL
       decision, not a fee one, and
       it lives in PRE_MAINNET A6 with the Safe multisig question rather than
       here.
       src/curb/FeePolicy.sol. feeSweepOperator() is a CONSTANT zero - no
       setter, no stored value - because this product has no such role: a
       creator sweeps their own launch and buyback is off. Its other getters
       are not decoration either: they return the terms a deployment chose -
       the recipient being the one AT DEPLOYMENT, since launches take theirs
       from the hook.
       FOUR OF THEM ARE CONSTANTS RATHER THAN ARGUMENTS, and that replaced a
       check that did not cover them. protocolFeeShareBps, buybackBurnBps,
       hookFeeBps and feeSweepOperator were all constructor inputs, and the
       deployment compared the first and the last and NOT the middle two - so a
       policy could have published a buyback burn or a hook rate that no launch
       uses, and every assertion would still have passed. A value that cannot
       be set cannot be set wrong; that is why they moved rather than gaining
       two more comparisons. What a deployment still decides - the recipient,
       the escrow, the price-impact ceiling - is compared against the factory
       both in the script and in a test.
       THE PLATFORM RECIPIENT IS REQUIRED, NOT INFERRED. It used to default to
       the deployer. It is the hook's starting recipient and every launch
       created before a change is stamped with it for good, so a deployment
       that guessed it leaves those launches paying the wrong address forever.
       (The hook's own copy can be changed behind the 24-hour announcement;
       this paragraph used to call it immutable.) The local runners set it explicitly to anvil's account 0, which is
       test configuration where a reader can see it.

  5.4b NO LAUNCH FEE (owner's decision, 4 October 2026). DeployCurb sets
       launchFee 0 for every factory, immutable per factory: launching costs
       gas only. The factory can still carry a fee - it goes to the platform
       recipient in force at launch, and a refused one is held in
       unclaimedLaunchFees until withdrawn (AUDIT M-06) - but no CURB factory
       charges one. The platform's share of the trading tax is unchanged.

  5.5  WHERE THE FEES GO - DONE SEPARATELY, AND NOT BY THE WORK ABOVE
       The fee work made the AMOUNTS right and said, correctly at the time,
       that it did not make them withdrawable. src/curb/FeeEscrow.sol does:
       balances per recipient, a claim that pays only the caller from only the
       caller's balance, nothing privileged anywhere in it. PRE_MAINNET A2
       carries the evidence and the limits.
       BOTH DIRECTIONS ARE MEASURED, AND A CLAIM IS BOUNDED ON BOTH SIDES.
       A credit is what arrived. A claim must pay the claimant at least what
       they asked for AND take no more than that out of the escrow. Each half
       was missing at some point and each was found by reading, not by a test:
         - safeTransfer catches a `false` return and cannot see a token that
           returns true and pays less. Measured: a 4e18 balance paid 3.96e18
           and still went to zero.
         - the recipient check alone protects the wrong person. A token paying
           the claimant 4 and taking 4.04 from the escrow satisfies it, and the
           surplus comes out of what backs everyone else.
       The claimant-side figure is a NET GAIN rather than a delivery, so a
       recipient that forwards its tokens mid-callback is refused as well. That
       is deliberate and documented rather than discovered - and it is a LIMIT,
       not a rule everyone can comply with: an account whose forwarding is in
       immutable code cannot stop, and for it the escrow is unusable.
       THE CREATOR'S ADDRESS IS STILL FIXED for the life of the launch - C3.
       Under core v2 the launch's FUTURE creator income can be switched once,
       one way, to the launch's own holder distributor; nobody can point it at
       an address of their choosing, and the switch moves no balance this
       escrow already holds. The escrow makes the cost of a fixed address
       concrete rather than theoretical: a lost key leaves a balance nobody can
       ever move, and there is deliberately no administrator who could.

6. TEST SCOPE
================================================================================
What would have to pass for this to be believed. Every figure asserted as an
exact integer against a balance or a receipt, never as "about right".

  THE BAND, AT ITS EDGES
    - T = 100 and T = 500 are accepted; 99 and 501 are refused, each with its
      own error and the offending value in it
    - a platform rate other than the one shown is refused: launch() takes
      the share the caller saw (expectedPlatformBps) and refuses if the hook's
      moved in between. A rate other than 50 is NOT refused - the policy owner
      may set 0..80 for later launches. (This line said it was, from the days
      the rate was a constant.)
    - a snapshot whose share is not 10000 is refused
    - MUTATION: remove each check in turn and the matching test must fail, for
      the right reason. A band that no test can break is not enforced.

  THE SPLIT IS EXACT, AND FROZEN PER LAUNCH
    - at T = 100, 300 and 500, on a buy and on a sell: the platform receives
      exactly P of the base and the creator exactly T-P, asserted on balances
      before and after (P = 50 in these tests, its starting value)
    - THE PLATFORM'S SHARE DOES NOT MOVE WITH T. The same trade size at three
      different T values pays the platform the same integer. This is the
      decision's whole point and it deserves its own test.
    - the trader's cost equals T of the base exactly, both sides

  THE BASE IS THE RIGHT ONE
    - buy: the base is the NVDAc SENT, not what reached the curve
    - sell: the base is the gross NVDAc the trade produced, before the tax
    - a clamped partial fill on the curve charges on what was actually SPENT,
      not on what was offered - the refunded part is not taxed

  THE POOL MATCHES THE CURVE
    - the same trade size at the same T costs the same on the curve and in the
      pool, to the integer, with the LP fee excluded from the comparison
    - the LP fee is separately visible and goes to the position, not to us
    - a swap sent by somebody who is not our interface pays the same
    - an exact-output swap is refused, ASSERTED ON ITS OWN SELECTOR rather
      than on "it reverted". A bare expectRevert here passed even with both
      fail-closed guards deleted, because an unconfigured pool has a zero tax
      and the creator's leg underflows instead - the test was reporting
      arithmetic as if it were a guard
    - a pool whose T was never written FAILS rather than charging zero
    - A REVERT IS ASSERTED BY DECODING v4's WRAPPER, not by looking for four
      bytes somewhere in the blob. CustomRevert.WrappedError carries the
      failing contract, the callback it failed in, and the inner error: all
      three are checked, and PoolNotConfigured's pool id with them. The scan it
      replaced could have matched those bytes inside an address or an amount -
      and it could not tell beforeSwap's refusal from afterSwap's, which is
      exactly what one of the mutations does

  ROUNDING AND DUST
    - a trade small enough that 50 bps rounds to zero: what happens, and is it
      in the trader's favour
    - the sum of the parts never exceeds the base, at every T, over a fuzz run

  THE ETH ROUTE
    - the tax appears as a NVDAc figure on the NVDAc leg, and the ETH reported
      to the user still reconciles with the receipt, gas kept separate

  AND WHAT NO TEST HERE CAN SHOW
    None of this proves anyone can WITHDRAW what was collected. That is A2, it
    is not in this scope, and no test in this section should be read as
    covering it.

7. STILL OPEN, AND TOUCHED BY THIS DECISION
================================================================================
  - WHO PICKS T - SETTLED. launch() is open to anyone. The creator is the
    caller UNLESS the caller names another address in creatorWallet
    [CurbLaunchFactory.LaunchConfig, and launch()'s `creator` line], so a
    launch can be made on someone else's behalf, and the tax it chooses is
