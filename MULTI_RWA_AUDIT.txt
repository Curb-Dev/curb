CURB - MULTI-RWA: WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHAT THE AUDIT OF IT FOUND
================================================================================
Date  : 19 September 2026
Status: built and verified ON A LOCAL FORK. Nothing is deployed to mainnet.
        The per-asset thresholds are PROPOSALS and remain a product decision.

WHAT THIS FILE IS
  The build that follows MULTI_RWA.txt's section 3 design, and an audit of that
  build. The audit is the part worth reading: it found three defects, one of
  them serious enough that six of the seven assets would have been listed in
  the interface and unable to create a single launch. Section 5 then closed
  the gap the audit had named, by running the lifecycle against each asset.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE, STATED FIRST
  - No mainnet deployment. Every figure here is from a fork at block 51171034.
    This is the only bullet in the original list that is still true.

  AND THREE THAT WERE TRUE WHEN THIS WAS WRITTEN AND ARE NOT NOW, 2026-09-20.
  They are kept rather than deleted because the audit's value is the gap it
  named, and a reader should be able to see the gap and its closing together.

  - "The per-asset lifecycle IS verified, for all six. What is still missing
    there is the ETH route and the issuer refusal path."
      BOTH BUILT. PRE_MAINNET F2 and F3.
  - "The two-hop ETH route is not built. Twelve of thirteen equities have no
    WETH pool, so ETH entry does not work for them."
      BUILT AND MEASURED. ETH -> WETH -> USDC -> asset is one UniversalRouter
      command with a longer packed path; 0.05 ETH delivered 18998507 raw METAc
      on the fork, and the minimum binds on what arrives rather than on the
      first leg [test/fork/B20EthRoute.t.sol].
  - "The asset list and the thresholds are proposals."
      DECIDED. Three assets - NVDAc 60, SPCXc 88, GOOGLc 39 - sized from one
      target value at each asset's own rate, with the bar that the largest
      measured pool must hold at least one threshold [PRE_MAINNET F1]. The
      sentence after it still holds: change them in DeployCurb.s.sol's
      quoteAssets() and nothing else moves.


================================================================================
1. WHAT WAS BUILT
================================================================================

  CONTRACTS
    LaunchRegistry   wire() still fixes the hook and the first launcher, once.
                     addLauncher() admits more, ADD-ONLY: no removal, no
                     overwrite, the hook is not a parameter, and the SAME loop
                     check runs for every one. isLauncher/launcherCount/
                     launcherAt expose the set.
    Graduator        initializeCurve() now asks the registry who a launcher is
                     instead of keeping its own narrower answer. See 2.1.

  DEPLOYMENT
    DeployCurb       one shared registry, graduator, hook and lens; then one
                     escrow + policy + vault + factory PER ASSET, each with its
                     own immutable quote token and threshold. The cross-checks
                     that used to run once now run per asset, plus two new ones:
                     the escrow must be for that factory's asset, and the asset
                     must answer decimals() before a stack is built around it.

  INTERFACE
    app.html/app.js  a quote-asset picker filled FROM THE REGISTRY - it reads
                     launcherCount()/launcherAt(), asks each factory its
                     quoteToken() and each asset its symbol(). Nothing is
                     configured in the page. Every display label and the
                     threshold come from the chain.
    browse.js        each card reads ITS OWN launch's quote asset out of the
                     lens view, and asks that asset for its symbol and decimals.

  TOOLING
    script/b20/verify-assets.js   reads a finished deployment back and checks
                     what it actually stood up, rather than what the deploying
                     transaction believed.


================================================================================
2. WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND
================================================================================

2.1 F1 - THE GRADUATOR RECOGNISED ONLY THE FIRST FACTORY.  SERIOUS. FIXED.

    Graduator.initializeCurve() read `registry.launcher()`, which is the FIRST
    launcher and nothing more. Every launch initializes its curve through that
    function, so a second factory could be deployed, admitted to the registry,
    verified by every check, listed in the interface - and create ZERO launches.

    HOW IT GOT PAST EVERYTHING ELSE. The registry work has eight tests and all
    eight passed with this defect in place, because not one of them called
    launch() on the second factory. Admitting a factory and that factory being
    able to work are different claims, and only the first was being tested.

    PROVEN TWICE, BEFORE THE FIX:
      - on a real deployment: a cast send of launch() to the deployed METAc
        factory reverted NotTheLauncher(0x312B02…), the factory's own address.
      - in a test: W9 (below) failed with the same error.
    PROVEN AFTER: the same cast send returned status 1, and W9 passes.

    FIX: `if (!registry.isLauncher(msg.sender))`. The registry is the one place
    that decides who a launcher is; the Graduator asks it.

2.2 F2 - THE DEPLOY OUTPUT DROPPED A LINE FOUR THINGS PARSE.  FIXED.

    Replacing the single "CurbLaunchFactory <addr>" console line with a
    per-asset list removed a line that demo-app.sh and demo-waiting.sh read by
    name (`field "CurbLaunchFactory"`), and the browser suite's global setup
    runs demo-waiting.sh. The whole browser suite failed at setup with "could
    not preparing a waiting launch" - a failure that looks like the node and is
    not.
    FIX: the line is back, naming the first factory, with the list beside it.

2.3 F3 - DISCOVERY HARD-CODED ONE ASSET'S DECIMALS AND NAME.  FIXED.

    browse.js divided every reserve by 1e8 and wrote "NVDAc" after it, while
    the number came from whatever asset that launch priced against. All seven
    assets carry 8 decimals today, so the figure would have been right and the
    NAME wrong - until the first asset that does not, where the figure would be
    wrong by orders of magnitude and nothing would say so.
    FIX: quoteToken is decoded from the lens view and each asset is asked for
    its symbol and decimals, once per asset per page.

2.4 F4 - THE FLOW PAGE NAMED ONE ASSET IN TWENTY PLACES.  FIXED.

    app.js printed "NVDAc" beside numbers it had read from the factory's asset.
    Decimals were already read correctly; only the label lied.
    FIX: symbol() is read the same way decimals() already was, and every label
    uses it. The prose in app.html no longer names an asset either, and the
    threshold it used to state as "60 NVDAc" is read from the factory - it is
    per asset now, so a number written into the page would be wrong for six of
    the seven.

2.5 F5 - A MISTYPED ASSET ADDRESS WOULD HAVE DEPLOYED A STACK AROUND NOTHING.
    ADDED DEFENCE, not a defect found in operation.

    quoteAssets() is a hand-written table of addresses. A wrong one would
    deploy an escrow, a policy, a vault and a factory around a token that does
    not exist, and the first sign of it would be a launch nobody can buy into.
    DeployCurb now requires the address to answer decimals() and the threshold
    to be non-zero before it builds anything.

3. THE NEW GATES ARE LOAD-BEARING - MUTATION EVIDENCE
================================================================================

  A test that passes proves the code does something; it does not prove the test
  would notice if the code stopped. Two mutations were injected into the new
  gates and the suite was run against each.

    M1  addLauncher() skips _checkLoopCloses()
        EXPECTED: W6 fails.  OBSERVED: W6 failed
        ("next call did not revert as expected").

    M2  register() stops checking isLauncher
        EXPECTED: W2 fails.  OBSERVED: W2 failed (same message).

    The Graduator fix carries the same evidence without a separate mutation:
    W9 was RED with the old code and GREEN with the new, which is what a
    mutation of that line would have shown.

  BOTH MUTATIONS WERE REMOVED and the sources checked for any remaining marker
  before the final run. ONE EARLIER FULL-SUITE RUN IS VOID and is not counted:
  it was started before M2 was injected and compiled against the mutated
  source, reporting 2 failures that were the mutation, not the code.

5. THE PER-ASSET LIFECYCLE - CLOSED 20 September 2026
================================================================================

  This section used to say seven assets were stood up and one was exercised.
  That is no longer where it stands. test/fork/B20MultiAsset.t.sol runs the
  curve-to-pool lifecycle against EACH of the six, and all six pass.

  SIX NAMED TESTS, NOT ONE LOOP. A loop stops at the first failure and says
  nothing about the other five.

  FUNDED BY IMPERSONATING A REAL HOLDER PER ASSET, because deal() writes
  storage slots and a B20 is a precompile with none. The holders were found by
  script/b20/find-holders.js, which scans Transfer logs and then EXCLUDES the
  v4 PoolManager and every v3/Aerodrome pool: an address whose balance is
  somebody else's accounting is not a funding source, and the existing NVDAc
  fixture already refuses the PoolManager for that reason.

  WHAT EACH ASSET NOW PASSES, criteria written before measuring:
    L1  a launch is created and its record carries THAT asset
    L2  a buy accrues exactly 50 bps to the platform and T-50 to the creator,
        in that asset
    L3  a sell accrues both legs on the GROSS output
    L4  the curve's books close - what it holds equals what it owes plus what
        is tradeable, counted outside the contract
    L5  the threshold is crossed and the launch graduates
    L6  the pool opens on that asset paired with the launch token, with our
        hook, our tier and our tick spacing, and reserves arrive
    L7  the LP position is held by a lock that pays the creator and runs the
        full 180 days
    L8  the sweep credits both sides in the escrow FOR THAT ASSET and each
        side claims its own, with the money arriving
    E1  the one-hop ETH route does NOT exist - measured, not assumed

  TWO CORRECTIONS THIS WORK FORCED, both in the test rather than the code:
    - E1 first asserted the WETH pool holds ZERO. GOOGLc failed it: its
      WETH/1% pool exists and holds ONE RAW UNIT. MULTI_RWA.txt 2.2 had
      recorded exactly that and called it empty; the assertion did not. The
      criterion is now "less than one threshold", which is tied to a real
      quantity - a pool that cannot supply one threshold cannot fill a launch.
    - E1 then passed for five assets WITHOUT MEASURING ANYTHING, because they
      have no WETH pool at all and the assertion lived inside the loop body.
      It counts the pools it finds now and asserts on the count, so the absence
      is recorded rather than implied. Measured: 0 WETH pools for AAPLc,
      AMZNc, METAc, MSFTc and SPCXc; 1 for GOOGLc, holding dust.

  WHAT IS STILL NOT COVERED FOR THESE SIX
    - THE ETH ROUTE ITSELF. It cannot be covered: there is no WETH pool to
      route through. Building the two-hop ETH -> WETH -> USDC -> asset path is
      real work and is not done.
    - THE ISSUER REFUSAL PATH. All thirteen equities share policy 5 with one
      admin, which is an argument that C1's mechanism is identical and NOT a
      measurement that it is.
    - Trading in the graduated pool through the official router. L6 proves the
      pool opened correctly and holds reserves; it does not swap in it.


================================================================================
5b. THE ORIGINAL GAP, KEPT FOR THE RECORD
================================================================================

  *** THIS IS WHAT THE GAP WAS ON 19 SEPTEMBER, NOT WHAT IT IS. *** Section 5
  above supersedes every "NOT DONE" below except the ETH route and the refusal
  path. It is kept because the shape of the gap - a stack deployed before it
  was exercised - is the thing worth remembering, and deleting it would make
  this file look as though the work had always been finished.

  MULTI_RWA.txt 4.2 lists sixteen steps an asset must pass before it is
  offered. For the six assets other than NVDAc, THREE of them were done:

    done      the factory exists with the right asset and threshold (verify)
    done      a launch can be created (proven for METAc, by construction for
              the rest - same factory code, same registry, same graduator)
    done      the record carries that asset

    NOT DONE  a buy on the curve, and the fee split in that asset
    NOT DONE  a sell, and the gross-output side
    NOT DONE  the books closing independently
    NOT DONE  crossing the threshold and graduating
    NOT DONE  the pool opening on the right pair at the right tier
    NOT DONE  the LP lock and the burn
    NOT DONE  buying and selling in the graduated pool
    NOT DONE  the sweep, and both claims out of the escrow
    NOT DONE  the ETH route - and for these six it CANNOT pass: there is no
              WETH pool, which is why the two-hop route is the real work
    NOT DONE  the policy refusal surfaced in the interface

  WHY THEY ARE NOT DONE. Every one of them needs a funded balance of that asset
  on the fork, which means a holder to impersonate per asset - the NVDAc tests
  use one named whale. That is mechanical work, not a design question, and it
  is the next thing to do if these assets are to be offered rather than merely
  deployed.

  AND THE ROADMAP ALREADY SAID SO. Rev 3 fixed the build order as "one quote
  asset end to end before adding more" [ROADMAP.txt:1890]. NVDAc is end to end;
  the other six are not. This build is therefore AHEAD of that rule, not
  compliant with it - the mechanism and the deployment exist before the
  lifecycle evidence does. That is a defensible order only if the six are
  treated as NOT OFFERABLE until section 5's list is closed for each, and it
  stops being defensible the moment one of them is shown to a user as though
  it were finished.

  UNTIL THEN, THE HONEST STATEMENT IS: seven assets are STOOD UP and one is
  EXERCISED. "It is a B20 like NVDAc" is not evidence - MULTI_RWA.txt 2.3 is
  the counter-example, where assets sharing every readable property share no
  route at all.

6. STILL A PRODUCT DECISION
================================================================================

  - the asset list. Seven of thirteen, because six have no pool or only dust.
  - every threshold. Set to the same share of each asset's largest measured
    pool as the decided 60 NVDAc is of its own (41.38%), rounded down, which
    keeps fillability equal and lets the dollar size range from about $1,100 to
    $15,900. The alternative - equal dollars - makes AAPLc unfillable.
  - whether a dollar entry is offered, which section 2.2 of MULTI_RWA.txt shows
    is the shorter route for every asset including NVDAc.
  - A6, the platform recipient, unchanged and still shared across all assets.
