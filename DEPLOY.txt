BEFORE YOU RUN ANYTHING - SIX DECISIONS
================================================================================

  1. THE PLATFORM RECIPIENT.   CURB_PLATFORM_RECIPIENT
     The address the hook starts with. EACH LAUNCH copies the recipient in
     force when it is created into its own record, and that copy is permanent:
     the curve and the pool both pay it for as long as the launch exists. The
     hook's own recipient is NOT permanent - the policy owner (3) can announce
     a new one and execute it 24 hours later, and launches created after that
     are stamped with the new address. So a wrong address here is correctable
     for the future and never for the past: every launch made before the
     correction pays the old address forever.
     This paragraph used to call the recipient "immutable on the hook". It has
     been settable since the announce/execute policy landed.
     RECORDED CHOICE (A6): 0x52483b53c8C08Ece00932A65DaE70487dcCD21E0, an EOA.
     It has no code, no balance and no nonce on Base - it has never been used.
     An EOA means ONE KEY: whoever holds it takes every fee the protocol earns,
     and losing it loses them all. The script refuses a code-less address
     unless you also set CURB_ALLOW_EOA_RECIPIENT=true, which is deliberate:
     accepting it has to be an act, not a default.
     A SAFE IS CHEAPEST NOW. Afterwards it is a policy change - announce, wait
     24 hours, execute - and it reaches only launches created after it.
     CUSTODY IS WRITTEN DOWN EITHER WAY. SAFE_OPS.txt, PLATFORM RECIPIENT,
     holds the choice - keep the recorded EOA with its custody written out
     (device, seed backups, who claims), or switch to a treasury Safe before
     STEP 2. The owner ticks one in SAFE_OPS.txt, with the Safe's address if
     that is the choice, and commits it before STEP 0; the custody goes in
     SAFE_OPS.txt's private copy, kept outside the repository (its Status
     says why: the repository is public, and an uncommitted edit stops STEP
     0). Nothing in this runbook decides it.

  NO LAUNCH FEE. DeployCurb sets launchFee 0 for every factory: launching
  costs gas only, and a launch that sends ether is refused. Immutable per
  factory - charging one would be a new factory. Read it back (ADDRESSES.txt).
  The platform's income is its share of the trading tax, unchanged.

  THE LP LOCK IS FOREVER. A graduated launch's position goes into a
  PositionLock that has no unlock time and no withdrawal - nobody, the
  creator or the platform included, can ever take the liquidity out. Its LP
  fees still go to the creator wallet, or to the launch's holders once its
  sharing is on. There is no lock duration to choose: the factory terms have
  none, and the registry refuses a record whose old lock-duration word
  (reservedZero) is not zero.

  2. THE DEPLOYER.   whatever key you sign with
     It becomes:
       - UNTIL THE SAFE ACCEPTS AT STEP 4B, the registry's owner (deployer()
         returns owner()), the only address that may wire() once and
         addLauncher() afterwards. A launcher it admits cannot touch an
         EXISTING launch - its record, its curve's reserve, its creator's or
         holders' income: LaunchRegistry.register accepts a record only for a
         curve whose token() is the record's launch token, whose pairToken()
         is its quote asset and whose factory() is that launcher's own
         Graduator, and each curve only once; and the Graduator refuses to
         graduate, switch holder sharing on or rescue fees for a record whose
         curve does not name its launch token. Before those checks (the
         internal audit of 9 October 2026, finding 1) this line said the same
         and was false: an admitted launcher could register a live launch's
         curve under a token of its own, then drain that curve's whole
         reserve through graduate() or point its creator income at a
         distributor of its own. What an admitted launcher still can do is
         make launches of its own, on its own terms, which the site would
         list. And admitting is not harmless either: a launcher it never
         admits can never exist, and every admitted launcher - none can be
         removed - adds gas to every launch on every factory and to every
         platform-recipient change. The list is capped at MAX_LAUNCHERS = 32 (fourteen used
         today; measured on the fork on 15 October, each real launcher costs
         a launch about 6,000 gas - 2,839,518 for a launch with eleven
         admitted, 2,857,539 with fourteen, DeployCurbRun's
         test_threeMoreLaunchersCostEachLaunchLittle_fourteenAgainstEleven),
         and the cap bounds a list of junk launchers at about 2M gas per launch; within the cap,
         a stolen key can still fill the remaining slots for good and leave
         no room for a real asset. So THE REGISTRY GOES TO THE POLICY SAFE.
         DeployCurb starts the handover itself: when CURB_POLICY_OWNER is not
         the deployer, the last thing the run does is
         registry.transferOwnership(<CURB_POLICY_OWNER>), and it reads
         pendingOwner() back. The registry is Ownable2Step, so NOTHING HAS
         MOVED until the Safe calls registry.acceptOwnership() - STEP 4B,
         with the exact payload. Until then the deployer's one key can still
         admit launchers, at once and for good. Later assets are admitted by
         the Safe (script/DeployAssetStack.s.sol prints its call).
       - the sender of $CURB's launch, which is made inside the same run
         (decision 6) - but NOT its creator: $CURB's creator wallet is
         CURB_FIRST_CREATOR, which the script refuses to be zero or the
         deployer.
       - PERMANENTLY, the owner of every PonsV2BuybackVault, one per quote
         asset - fourteen today, from the run's own list [AUDIT A-9]. That role has
         one write-once setter which nothing ever calls, on contracts that hold
         nothing, and renounceOwnership is disabled - so it is permanent and
         worth nothing. Named because "this protocol has no owner" would be
         false.
     IT DOES NOT HAVE TO BE THE PLATFORM RECIPIENT and there is an argument it
     should not be: the recipient holds money, the deployer holds a wiring
     privilege, and they have different reasons to be kept separate.

  3. THE POLICY OWNER.   CURB_POLICY_OWNER   (defaults to the deployer, which
                                             is one key - refused unless said)
     Added 2026-09-21 with the settable fee policy. This key can change the
     platform's rate and recipient FOR LAUNCHES CREATED AFTER THE CHANGE, and
     nothing else: each launch's share and recipient are frozen into its
     record when it is created, and the pool reads that record, not the hook.
     It cannot touch an existing launch, a creator's share, a registry record,
     a locked position or anybody's balance. (This used to say it could
     redirect the recipient on pools that already exist - true until the
     per-launch snapshot, and not since.)
     AND IT IS BOUNDED, not just owned: a change must be ANNOUNCED on chain,
     waits 24 hours, then executes inside a seven-day window, and can never
     take a launch's creator below a fifth of their own tax. thestonks'
     arrangement, adopted deliberately over PONS's one-transaction setters.
     THE KEY ITSELF CAN BE HANDED ON - ownership transfers in two steps, and
     renouncing works, which freezes the policy for good. That is also the
     argument
     for making it a Safe now rather than later: it is cheap either way, and it
     is the key that can move money for every future launch.
     It should NOT be the same address as the platform recipient. One holds
     revenue, the other decides where revenue goes.

     THE SCRIPT CHECKS WHAT KIND OF ADDRESS IT IS, as it does the recipient's
     [checkPolicyOwner in script/DeployCurb.s.sol]. One key - no code, or only
     an EIP-7702 delegation - is refused unless CURB_ALLOW_EOA_POLICY_OWNER=true,
     and a contract that does not answer getOwners/getThreshold is refused
     unless CURB_ALLOW_NON_SAFE_POLICY_OWNER=true. A Safe needs neither. This
     used to be checked only for zero, so a mistyped owner - a code-less address
     nobody controls - became the hook's owner silently, and the platform's
     share and recipient could never be moved again
     [test_theDeploymentsPolicyOwnerCheckKnowsAKeyFromASafe].

     MAKE IT A SAFE, AND MAKE IT BEFORE STEP 2 - the preflight simulates with
     it and prints it into the STEP 4 command. The whole path is measured on
     the fork and works - a real 2-of-3 from Base's own factory takes ownership,
     accepts it, announces, cancels, waits and executes, each step relayed by
     somebody who is not an owner
     [test_aSafeCanTakeOverAndOperateTheFeePolicy]. What that test also shows is
     the step people miss:

       OWNABLE2STEP DOES NOT HAND OVER UNTIL THE NEW OWNER ACCEPTS. A Safe
       cannot simply call acceptOwnership - it has to clear its own threshold to
       do it. A transfer to a Safe that never accepts leaves the OLD key
       holding the policy while everybody believes it moved.

     TWO WAYS TO GET ONE, and the first is the one to use:
       a) app.safe.global, "Create new Safe", network Base. Pick the signers and
          the threshold, sign the creation with one of them, note the address.
          2-of-3 across separately-held keys is what A6 suggests; a 1-of-1 Safe
          is a plain account with extra steps.
       b) the same factory the test uses, if you would rather not use a UI:
          SafeProxyFactory 0x4e1DCf7AD4e460CfD30791CCC4F9c8a4f820ec67
          singleton        0x29fcB43b46531BcA003ddC8FCB67FFE91900C762
          createProxyWithNonce(singleton, setup(...), saltNonce)

     THEN EITHER:
       pass it as CURB_POLICY_OWNER at STEP 2, so the hook is born owned by it
       - simplest, and nothing has to be accepted afterwards; or
       deploy with a plain key - which the script refuses unless
       CURB_ALLOW_EOA_POLICY_OWNER=true is set - then transferOwnership(safe)
       and have the SAFE execute acceptOwnership(). Do not stop after the
       transfer.

     I CANNOT CREATE THIS FOR YOU. It needs a funded key to sign with and a
     decision about who the signers are, and neither is mine to make.

  4. WHETHER TO CREATE THE DEMO LAUNCH.   CURB_DEMO_LAUNCH
     LEAVE IT UNSET. A demo launch on mainnet is written into the registry as
     launch number one, permanently - the record is write-once and the list is
     append-only, so there is no removing or renaming it. The flag exists for
     the fork, where the runner scripts need it.

  5. THE HOLDER SHARING ROLES.   CURB_SHARING_PUBLISHER
                                 CURB_SHARING_KEEPER_PAYEE
                                 CURB_SHARING_VETOERS
     A launch whose creator switches holder sharing on pays its creator income
     to a distributor of its own, and the distributor pays its holders against
     roots the HolderDistributorFactory relays. These three are that factory's
     roles from the moment it is deployed:
       - the PUBLISHER proposes each payout root. A hot key: it can propose
         one root per distributor at a time, inside the distributor's on-chain
         caps (never more than it received, keeper fee at most 2%), and
         nothing else. A root waits an hour before it pays.
       - the KEEPER PAYEE receives the keeper's gas fee out of each payout.
         A cold address, or the account that pays the keeper's gas.
       - the VETOERS, comma-separated, besides the policy owner (3), which is
         always one: each can stop a pending root inside its hour, and freeze
         the factory. At least one, held apart from the publisher - none may
         be the publisher, and the policy owner is not listed here.
     The policy owner owns the factory and the SharingCatalog. At once it can
     only stop things: revoke the publisher, freeze, add a vetoer. Everything
     that could let a payout through is announced and waits 48 hours: a new
     publisher, a new payee, removing a vetoer, ending a freeze. A removal
     also waits until no root published in the last hour can still be vetoed,
     so a vetoer keeps its veto over every root published while it is one. A
     freeze holds every pending root - none becomes payable, each can be
     vetoed whatever its hour - while active roots keep paying, and holds a
     publisher rotation too. The owner cannot publish, pay, or change a cap.
     WHY THE SEPARATE VETOER MATTERS. A root decides how much each holder is
     owed; the distributor checks its totals, not each holder's line. A vetoer
     held apart from the publisher is what stops a wrong split, and the owner
     can take such a vetoer away only in public, 48 hours ahead - in which
     time holders can claim what the active root already gives them.
     Removing a vetoer needs an hour with no root published: pause the keeper
     for that hour when you execute one.
     HOLDERS DEPEND ON THE PUBLISHER FOR GOOD. A launch that switches sharing
     on is paid out only against the publisher's roots; if the platform ever
     stops publishing - the key lost and not rotated, the keeper shut down -
     what its distributor receives after the last activated root stays there
     for good, where without the switch it would have stayed the creator's.
     No fallback publisher exists, by the owner's decision of 9 October 2026
     (PRE_MAINNET C9): keeping the publisher alive, and rotating it through
     the Safe when needed, is part of running the platform.
     The deployer admits the factory into the catalog in the same run - the
     catalog's one-day bootstrap - so launches can switch sharing on from the
     start. A later version waits out the catalog's seven days in public.
     The script refuses a real run without all three, a vetoer that is the
     publisher, the policy owner in the list and a vetoer named twice
     [checkSharingRoles in script/DeployCurb.s.sol]; the preflight asks the
     same before the suites run.
     The keys behind these roles are made ahead of the day, as
     services/sharer/RUNBOOK.md part 3 says (PREP 3).

  6. $CURB, THE FIRST LAUNCH.   CURB_FIRST_LAUNCH=1
                                CURB_FIRST_NAME, CURB_FIRST_SYMBOL,
                                CURB_FIRST_URI, CURB_FIRST_TAX_BPS,
                                CURB_FIRST_CREATOR, CURB_FIRST_DEV_BUY,
                                CURB_FIRST_SHARE
     WHY IT IS INSIDE THE RUN. The Launches page's hero features the
     registry's first launch paired with ETH, and the registry is append-only:
     whichever ETH launch is first stays first. launch() on a factory is open
     to anyone from the receipt of that factory's admission - so a $CURB made
     "straight after the run" by hand could be beaten, and the front page
     would feature somebody else's coin for good. With CURB_FIRST_LAUNCH=1,
     DeployCurb builds EVERY stack before it admits ANY factory, wraps and
     approves the dev buy, admits the WETH factory FIRST (the registry's
     wire()), and launches $CURB on it in the very next transaction - after
     the catalog has admitted its first distributor version, so it may share
     with holders, or the launch would revert FactoryNotApproved. Every other
     factory is admitted only after $CURB: no launch on any of them can come
     before it, however slow the run or wherever a broadcast died.
     ONE RECEIPT IS LEFT OPEN, NOT NONE, AND ONLY ON THE WETH FACTORY.
     Between its admission and $CURB's launch - the time the signer takes to
     send one more transaction under --slow - somebody watching the chain
     could launch on the WETH factory first. The run used to wire the WETH
     factory first with some fifty-five transactions after it, and then to
     admit it last while every factory before it stood open for those same
     transactions; one receipt is as low as a run of separate transactions
     goes. The script asserts that $CURB is registry.launchAt(0) in its
     simulation only. On chain, check-deployment.sh (STEP 5) reads which
     launch $CURB is - the token its launch's receipt names, not the
     simulation's address, which a launch landing first would move - fails
     if it is not launch 0, and fails if the record admitted any factory but
     the WETH one before $CURB's launch. IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG, $CURB IS
     NOT LAUNCH ZERO says what then.
     WHAT EACH SETTING IS - all permanent once the run lands:
       CURB_FIRST_NAME, CURB_FIRST_SYMBOL   the token's name and symbol
       CURB_FIRST_URI      the token URI in the launch form's own format:
                           data:application/json;base64, then the base64 of
                           {name, symbol, description, image, website,
                           twitter, telegram}, the image itself a small data:
                           URL. Keep it in a file and pass
                           CURB_FIRST_URI="$(cat <the file>)". It is written
                           into the token and can never change.
       CURB_FIRST_TAX_BPS  the total tax, 100 to 500
       CURB_FIRST_CREATOR  the creator wallet: it receives the creator fees and
                           the dev buy's tokens and owns the locked position.
                           REQUIRED, never zero (zero would make the sender -
                           the deployer, a hot key - $CURB's beneficiary for
                           good) and never the deployer. A treasury Safe; who
                           holds it follows the $CURB treasury policy. ONE
                           KEY - no code, or only an EIP-7702 delegation - is
                           refused unless CURB_ALLOW_EOA_CREATOR=true is set
                           too: a code-less address is also what a mistyped
                           one looks like, and the dev buy's tokens, the
                           refund, the locked position and its LP fees would
                           go to it for good.
       CURB_FIRST_DEV_BUY  the dev buy in wei of ETH, paid by the deployer
                           (0 for none), which must hold it before its first
                           STEP 2: the preflight's simulation spends it from
                           the deployer's real balance (STEP 2 says why).
                           The tokens go to CURB_FIRST_CREATOR.
                           The usual limits hold: below the threshold, and at
                           most 20% of the supply in tokens received.
       CURB_FIRST_SHARE    1 switches holder sharing on inside the launch (the
                           creator share then goes to $CURB's holders, for
                           good); 0 leaves it off, and the creator wallet can
                           switch it on later, once.
     REHEARSE IT ONCE with the same values on a node of your own (never the
     suite's pinned node; see the header of script/DeployCurb.s.sol), and
     open /token/<that token> on the local site: the name, image and links
     you see there are the ones mainnet will keep.
     The preflight simulates with these settings and prints them into the
     STEP 4 command, each shell-quoted. ITS RULES, asked before the suites
     run [first_launch_bad in script/b20/preflight-mainnet.sh]:
       CURB_FIRST_LAUNCH   exactly 1 or 0. Unset, empty or anything else is
                           refused rather than read as either. 0 deploys
                           without $CURB, and then nothing stops another ETH
                           launch from being the hero - it exists for the
                           fork, not for this run.
       with 1, EVERY ONE of the seven below must be set (an empty
       CURB_FIRST_URI is allowed; an empty name or symbol is not), and:
       CURB_FIRST_NAME, CURB_FIRST_SYMBOL, CURB_FIRST_URI
                           printable ASCII only, and no leading or trailing
                           space - forge trims those before the script reads
                           the value, so the token would not hold what was
                           checked
       CURB_FIRST_TAX_BPS  plain digits, 100 to 500
       CURB_FIRST_CREATOR  an address, never zero, never CURB_DEPLOYER, and
                           not one key unless CURB_ALLOW_EOA_CREATOR=true
                           (section 3 reads its code)
       CURB_FIRST_DEV_BUY  plain digits: wei of ether, 0 for none
       CURB_FIRST_SHARE    exactly 1 or 0
     DeployCurb refuses again what it can see (an unset or malformed value, a
     zero or deployer creator), and the factory decides the tax band and the
     dev buy's cap in the simulation.

AHEAD OF THE DAY
================================================================================
  None of this is done on the day. Each part has a check at the end; do not
  book the day until all three pass. They run in THE ORDER's sequence: PREP
  1's points 1 to 4, then PREP 2 and PREP 3, and only then PREP 1's points 5
  and 6 and its CHECK, because the preflight they run needs both.

  PREP 1 - RE-PRICE THE ASSET LIST. DAYS AHEAD, ON A BRANCH, IN ONE COMMIT.
    WHY. A factory's phantom and threshold are immutable, a launcher can never
    be removed from the registry (32 at most), and quoteAssets() in
    script/DeployCurb.s.sol carries the prices of the day it was last
    re-priced - 15 October 2026 for the fourteen listed now (WETH at
    $2,426.06; PRE_MAINNET decision 1a). Signed later, every launch - $CURB
    included - starts and graduates wherever those prices have drifted to,
    not at the decided ~$4,000 and ~$55,000, and every asset on the list is
    listed for good. So this step runs again days ahead of the day, and the
    preflight prices the list once more on the day (6 below). WHICH assets
    to list is the owner's decision; this is how the
    decision is written down without editing pinned tests under time
    pressure. measure-thresholds.js says to run it immediately before
    deploying, and this is where that happens: early enough to fix what it
    finds, and again on the day (6 below).

    1. MEASURE, against live Base:
         RPC=<a Base RPC> node script/b20/measure-thresholds.js
       For each asset it prints the price from the pool's own slot0, the most
       held in any one pool, the threshold for $10,832 and the phantom for
       $4,000, both in the asset's own units. A public endpoint refuses
       often: the script retries, and a line saying it could not price an
       asset means run it again, never guess.
    2. CROSS-CHECK EVERY PRICE AGAINST CHAINLINK. The feeds are the quoteUsd
       entries in services/sharer/deploy/sharer.example.json (sharing.feeds);
       WETH's is ETH / USD, 0x71041dddad3595F9CEd3DcCFBe3D1F4b0a16Bb70.
       In a WSL shell (base-cast is WSL's, B20_ENVIRONMENT.txt section 2;
       it goes on the PATH only at PREP 2, so name it in full here):
         ~/.foundry/bin/base-cast call <feed> "latestRoundData()(uint80,int256,uint256,uint256,uint80)" --rpc-url <rpc>
         ~/.foundry/bin/base-cast call <feed> "decimals()(uint8)" --rpc-url <rpc>
       The second number divided by 10^decimals is the dollar price; the
       fourth is when it was last updated. A stock feed holds its last close
       outside US market hours, so measure while the market is open. A pool
       price more than 3% from its feed stops this step until the reason is
       known - a thin pool, a stale feed, a wrong address - whichever number
       is then written.
    3. CHOOSE THE LIST - the owner's call, made here and nowhere later. An
       asset left out can be admitted after launch with
       script/DeployAssetStack.s.sol and the Safe's addLauncher; an asset put
       in can never be taken out. NVDAc carries the fork suites and the runner
       scripts (DeployCurb resolves the "NVDAc" factory by name for the demo
       and for the CurbLaunchFactory line the runner scripts read), so
       dropping NVDAc is a code change with its own review, not an edit in
       this step - thin or not: NVDAc has been listed thin since 15 October
       2026. SPCXc and cbZEC are named by tests too (point 4 says which),
       and so are USDC, VIRTUAL and AERO (test/fork/B20NewQuoteAssets.t.sol,
       which trades each through CurbRouter by label): dropping any of them
       changes those tests in the same commit.
    4. WRITE IT, ALL IN ONE COMMIT, so no tree ever disagrees with itself:
       - quoteAssets() in script/DeployCurb.s.sol: each asset's phantom and
         threshold, its fillable flag as measured, and its comment's price,
         date and the start and graduation in dollars the pair gives;
       - the fixtures that copy the list or name its assets:
           test/fork/B20MultiAsset.t.sol   _fixtures() fails the suites the
                                           moment it and quoteAssets()
                                           disagree
                                           (test_theFixtureCarriesTheDeploymentsOwnThresholds,
                                           with one named lifecycle test per
                                           asset, by selector in a fixed
                                           bytes4[13] array: resize it with
                                           every test_lifecycleOn<asset>()
                                           added or removed). Each fixture's
                                           holder must still hold what one
                                           lifecycle spends, which grows with
                                           the threshold ("the recorded holder
                                           no longer holds enough"); cbZEC's,
                                           22.86 at the pinned block against

  STEP 6 - VERIFY ON THE EXPLORER
    --verify in STEP 4 does this in the same run. By hand, every contract with
    the settings the build used - solc 0.8.30, cancun, optimizer on, 200 runs,
    AND via-IR on [foundry.toml, [profile.default]]. Without via-IR the legacy

    compile at all, so verification fails. forge takes all of them from
    foundry.toml when run from the repository root on STEP 0's commit, in a
    shell where STEP 0's ETH/ETHERSCAN/VERIFIER line still prints nothing (a
    VERIFIER_URL sends the sources and the key elsewhere). One contract at a
    time, in this shape:
      ETHERSCAN_API_KEY="$BASESCAN_API_KEY" ~/.foundry/bin/base-forge verify-contract \
        --chain 8453 --verifier etherscan --skip-is-verified-check --watch \
        <address> <path>:<Contract> <--constructor-args 0x... if it takes any>
    The hook's constructor arguments include the mined salt's result - take
    them from deployments/base-mainnet/run.json (step 5) rather than
    retyping.
    THE EXPLORER KEY MUST NOT REACH THE SCREEN. Left to itself,
    verify-contract first asks the explorer whether the contract is already
    verified, with a GET whose URL carries the key (apikey=...), and when
    that request fails - a timeout, a DNS failure, the kind of network
    trouble that sends you here from STEP 4 - forge prints the URL, key
    included, into the scrollback and any log. So:
      - the key reaches forge as ETHERSCAN_API_KEY, expanded from
        BASESCAN_API_KEY as above (in a new shell, read it in with read -rs
        as in STEP 2); never --etherscan-api-key <key>, which puts it in
        shell history and the process list.
      - --skip-is-verified-check stays in. Without that GET forge sends the
        key only in POST bodies, which it does not print - measured against a
        stand-in explorer with base-forge 1.6.0, --watch's status checks
        included. A contract STEP 4 already verified is then answered
        "already verified", which is harmless. (STEP 4's --verify never sends
        that GET, so its own errors do not hold the key.)
      - do not pipe the output into tee or a log, and do not share the screen
        while it runs.
      - if any line printed holds apikey=, the key is exposed: revoke it on
        the explorer and make a new one.

    should be validated in a block explorer" [PRE_MAINNET C7], so the
    application cannot be filed until this is done.
