================================================================================
CURB - HOW THE DOCS PAGE IS WRITTEN
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
  web/docs.md IS the docs page. docs.html fetches it and web/docs.js draws it,
  so editing docs.md and reloading docs.html is the whole workflow. Nothing
  else reads it and nothing builds from it.

  docs.md is also served to anyone as a plain file, so these notes live here,
  outside web/, and docs.md keeps one comment that points here. They were a
  25-line comment at the top of docs.md until 29 September 2026: hidden on the
  page, readable by anybody who opened the file.


WHO IT IS FOR, AND IN WHAT ORDER
  First, people who launch and trade. Then integrators. The page keeps that
  order and these sections:

    Overview (3-4 sentences, under the "# " title)
    Quick start
    How a launch works      curve to graduation; the refund of an oversized buy
    Paired assets           the list; one general risk sentence; the warning
                            that a price rising in the asset can fall in dollars
    Fees                    one table; the platform-share rule in one sentence
    Creator earnings        2-3 sentences
    Holder sharing          what it is, who gets what, the hour, getting paid,
                            checking it, what CURB cannot do - one screen
    Dev buy
    What is permanent       the lock, which is forever, said plainly
    What CURB can and       the one full statement of the platform's powers
      cannot do             and their limits (below); every other page
                            that claims trust links to it
    Risks                   short bullets
    FAQ                     6-8 questions, one of them about $CURB
    ---
    Developers              Contracts, Functions, CURB router, Holder sharing
                            contracts, Events, Fee routes
    Contracts and security  CURB contracts, Paired-asset addresses,
                            Audits and security contact

  The user part (Overview to FAQ) stays at about 1,500-2,000 words. The
  owner's rule, 29 September 2026: like the competitors' docs - short, plain,
  user-first, and no extra information.


THE VOICE
  - English. Short sentences. No hype.
  - No promise of profit, no yields, no "passive income" arithmetic.
  - Say "liquidity is locked forever": the lock has no unlock time and no
    withdrawal, for anyone. Never "rug-proof" or "rug impossible": the
    creator can still sell tokens they hold.
  - Never call the contracts audited by anyone outside. The owner's decision
    of 2 October 2026: there is no outside audit; the in-house reviews
    (AUDIT*.txt, CODEX_*) end with a final internal audit before the mainnet
    deployment, and the page says exactly that. Do not write "external audit"
    anywhere, and no legal text.
  - Risks in general words. Our own measurements (liquidity notes per asset,
    what one issuer's blocklist reached) are research, not docs.
  - Say each rule once. The platform-share rule was once written six times.
  - A figure worked out by arithmetic rather than read from the code says
    "about". Dollar amounts are left out because they move.
  - No invented contact addresses, audit firms or numbers. Where the owner has
    to fill something in, leave a marked placeholder (see below).


WHAT STAYS OUT OF THE USER PART
  Function names, gas figures (WETH's 2,300-gas transfer), Permit2's one-hour
  allowance, the registry record, the logo's size limit, the virtual reserve,
  tick spacing, escrow internals, tokens stranded by a direct transfer, the
  keyboard shortcut for search. They go in Developers, or nowhere.


KEEP IT TRUE TO THE CODE
  Every rule on the page was checked against src/curb, src/vendor and
  script/DeployCurb.s.sol, and for what the site does against web/app.js,
  web/token.js and web/browse.js. A sentence about what a page shows or offers
  is a claim about that page: recheck it when the page changes.

  "The launch's page" is /token/0x..., app.html in its token view, which hides
  #launchPanel and everything in it. Checked 29 September 2026, that view:
  - shows no unlock date, because there is none: the lock box (#lockBox,
    inside #launchPanel) says "unlocks: never", and on a launch that shares
    its fees the creator wallet sees "locked forever" (sharing-token.js,
    #tvLocked) and no withdrawal;
  - has no button for PositionLock.claimFees() (no page has), and the lock
    has no withdrawal to offer;
    holder sharing's harvest collects claimFees() for a launch that shares;
  - offers ETH on both sides of a curve trade on every pair, through the CURB
    router (token.js quoteChoices), except a curve sale on a non-ETH pair where
    the router is not on the chain (then the pair's asset only).
  docs.md says so in Quick start, Creator earnings, What is permanent and
  Functions. When the view gains any of these, change those places.
  For a launch that shares its fees the view also has the "Holders earn" card
  (#tvShare): the figures, the payout in force, announced role changes, Push
  pending payouts, the holder's Claim (and Claim to another address when the
  issuer blocks theirs) and Check the payout files yourself. docs.md's Holder
  sharing section says what they do.


FACTS docs.md AND curb.html MUST AGREE ON
  Checked 29 September 2026; $CURB's line 16 October 2026. Change both pages
  together, or neither.

  - $CURB IS LIVE, since 16 October 2026: registry.launchAt(0) on Base, the
    WETH factory, 0xf8bdCD06F7a5939c8b2340E27419FaCDF16A407e. Supply
    1,000,000,000, all of it on its curve at launch (no allocation, presale,
    airdrop or dev buy). Tax 100 bps, all of it to the treasury Safe - the
    platform's 50 and the creator's rest, the treasury being its creator
    wallet; holder sharing off. No vote, no claim on income. Only that
    address is $CURB.
  - THE TREASURY POLICY, decided 22 September 2026, is a policy and not code:
    80% of the platform's fee income buys $CURB and burns it, 20% runs the
    platform, done by hand when a threshold is reached. Never call it automatic
    or on-chain. No burn has happened yet. In the contracts buyback is off:
    FeePolicy.BUYBACK_BURN_BPS is 0 and every launch records buybackBurnBps 0,
    so no launch pays a buyback vault.
  - FEES. One tax per launch, 100 to 500 bps (LaunchRegistry MIN_TOTAL_TAX_BPS
    and MAX_TOTAL_TAX_BPS), chosen by the creator, charged in the paired asset
    on buys and sells, on the curve and in the pool. The platform's share comes
    out of it: 50 bps at deployment, at most FeeHook.maxPlatformFeeBps() = 80.
    A change is announced 24 hours ahead (POLICY_CHANGE_DELAY), must be applied
    within 7 days (POLICY_CHANGE_WINDOW) and reaches only launches created
    after it; each launch freezes the terms in force when it is made. The
    pool's own LP fee is 0.05% (DeployCurb POOL_FEE 500). There is no
    launch fee: DeployCurb sets launchFee 0, so launching costs gas only.
  - THE LOCK. The LP position is locked forever at graduation: PositionLock
    has no unlock time and no withdrawal. Only its LP fees move.
  - AN OVERSIZED BUY fills to the threshold and refunds the rest in the same
    transaction. Paid in ETH through the CURB router, the refund comes back in
    that transaction: ETH on an ETH pair, the paired asset on other pairs.
    Without the router, the curve pays WETH on an ETH pair and the site unwraps
    it straight after: a second confirmation.
  - CONFIRMATIONS (the owner's call, 2026-10-01). An ETH buy on a curve is one
    transaction on every pair (CurbRouter.buyWithEth). A sale for ETH is
    sellForEth after ONE unlimited approval to the router per token - the only
    unlimited approval the site asks for; every other one is exact. The router
    address and code hash live in web/app.js CURB_ROUTER, docs.md and
    script/DeployRouter.s.sol: change all three together.
  - DOLLAR FIGURES come from each paired asset's Chainlink feed on Base
    (USD_FEEDS in web/prices.js); where there is none, from the asset's Uniswap
    price in ETH times ETH / USD. Display only.
  - CREATOR EARNINGS. One Claim on the launch's page (claimAll in web/app.js)
    sweeps this launch's curve fees first, then claims from the escrow and the
    hook; on an ETH pair it pays ETH. The locked position's LP fee is not part
    of it: PositionLock.claimFees() collects that.
  - THE DEV BUY is capped in TOKENS RECEIVED: at most MAX_DEV_BUY_BPS = 2,000
    bps (20%) of the supply (CurbLaunchFactory DevBuyAboveShare), and the
    amount it spends must be below the graduation threshold (DevBuyTooLarge).
    The cap limits only the buy made inside the launch transaction. (This
    line said "7,500 of the threshold", a quote ceiling the factory dropped
    when the cap moved to tokens; docs.md repeated it.)
  - HOLDER SHARING (core v2): a creator switches a launch's creator income to
    its holders once, one way - at launch (LaunchConfig.holderShareFactory)
    or later (Graduator.enableHolderSharing). Creator share 0% while on;
    time-weighted shares (CURB-SHARE v1, services/sharer/spec); about hourly;
    at most 2% of each payout for gas (MAX_KEEPER_FEE_BPS 200); each payout
    waits 1 hour (VETO_WINDOW) and the multisig or a vetoer can stop it;
    shares accumulate below the push threshold (about $0.05) and never expire;
    the LP principal stays locked forever, as on every launch. Say "about hourly" and "about $0.05": both are the
    keeper's, not the contract's.
  - THE SITE IS STATIC. There is no CURB server behind the site; holder
    sharing's keeper is a separate service that only sends transactions and
    publishes the payout files - and, since 17 October 2026, each launch's
    past logs (services/sharer/scripts/index-history.ts), which a launch's
    page checks and draws from before reading the chain after them. Display
    only: nothing sent is built from that copy.

  Added 18 October 2026, the one place the whole statement is made, and the
  whole list: Holder sharing says so. The site's pages keep at most one short
  line, name no role holder, and link to it: terms.html (holder sharing),
  curb.html (the fee rules), the launch form's "Share fees with holders" list
  (app.html #shareLimits) and the switch's dialog (sharing-token.js
  #shEnableLimits). The heading stays short enough for one line in the
  contents: "What the platform can and cannot do" took two, and pushed
  "Paired-asset addresses" under a 1280 by 900 window's fold (docs.html's
  .toc note, site.spec.js). Each claim, and where it was checked:
  - CANNOT: no contract is upgradeable or has a withdrawal for an admin
    (PositionLock, FeeEscrow, HolderFeeDistributor: no owner; the
    distributor "no sweep, no upgrade"); the lock is forever; a launch's tax,
    platform share, platform recipient and pool fee are stamped into its
    record (CurbLaunchFactory reads the hook's pair once; FeeHook.LaunchFee
    keeps the record's copy).
  - PAYOUTS are time-weighted (CURB-SHARE v1) and recomputable by anyone
    (services/sharer `verify`); the distributor checks a root's totals, not
    each line (HolderDistributorFactory's header).
  - CAN, read on Base on 18 October 2026: one publisher, a single key, the
    keeper's, not a vetoer, no pendingPublisher - no backup publisher
    (PRE_MAINNET C9, finding 25); vetoers the guardian, Safe B (1 of 2) and
    the policy Safe (2 of 3), which owns the hook, the registry, the
    SharingCatalog and the HolderDistributorFactory; frozen() false.
    HolderDistributorFactory: freeze() is the owner's or any vetoer's, and
    while frozen a vetoer may veto whatever the hour; revokePublisher() and
    addVetoer() are the owner's, at once; an unfreeze, a new publisher, a new
    keeperPayee and a vetoer's removal are announced and wait ROLE_DELAY (48
    hours); executePublisher() not while frozen, removeVetoer() not before
    vetoableUntil (the latest root's hour). The hook's platform share and
    recipient change for later launches only, after POLICY_CHANGE_DELAY
    (FeeHook announce/execute). SharingCatalog: a version waits
    ADMISSION_DELAY (7 days); retire() and setRecommended() are at once, and
    neither reaches a launch already switched. Graduator.rescueCurveFees is
    the platform recipient's (and the creator's) at any time. The owner of
    each can cancel what it announced (cancelPending, cancel, the hook's
    cancels; addVetoer for a removal) and hand it on (Ownable2Step).
  - THE POOL: one-sided range liquidity in a graduated pool avoids the tax on
    its side (PRE_MAINNET C9, finding 23); Fees says it in full, the section
    in one sentence that points there. Recheck the role reads when a role
    changes (ADDRESSES.txt, "changed since the run").

THE MARKDOWN docs.js UNDERSTANDS, AND ONLY THIS
  Headings: one "# " page title; "## " a section and "### " a subsection, both
  listed in the contents.
  Paragraphs are separated by a blank line; a single line break inside a
  paragraph is joined with a space.
  Lists: "- " or "* " for bullets, "1. " for numbered items. A line indented by
  two or more spaces under an item continues that item. Lists do not nest.
  Inline: **bold**, *italic*, `code`, and [text](url) where url is https:// or
  http://, a page of this site such as terms.html or terms.html#anchor, or
  #anchor alone. Any other link is shown as plain text.
  Code blocks: a line of three backticks, the code, and another line of three
  backticks.
  Callouts: lines starting with "> ". Start the first one with **Note:**,
  **Warning:** or **Tip:** for a tone.
  Tables: a header row like "| a | b |", then a separator row like
  "|---|---|", then the rows.
  A line holding only three hyphens is a divider. HTML comments are hidden.
  Raw HTML is not supported: angle brackets are shown as the characters
  themselves.

  Heading anchors are the heading in lowercase with every run of other
  characters turned into "-", so "## How a launch works" is
  #how-a-launch-works. A repeated heading gets -2, -3 on its anchor.

  TRAPS WHEN WRAPPING A LINE: a wrapped line that starts with "- ", "* ",
  "1. ", "> ", "#", "|" or "---" starts a new block. Write "times" or the
  multiplication sign, not an asterisk, which starts italics.


WHAT THE TESTS CHECK
  web/e2e/site.spec.js loads docs.html and requires more than five "##" or
  "###" sections, a contents list that names them in order with every entry
  landing on a heading, no page errors, and no sideways scroll at 375 px.
  Wide content goes in a table (it scrolls inside its own box) or a code span
  (it wraps); keep tables to three columns so they read on a phone.
