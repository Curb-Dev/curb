1. MULTI-ASSET: THEY PUT IT IN A TABLE, WE PUT IT IN A FACTORY
================================================================================

  PONS
    ONE owner-controlled factory with two mappings:
      approvedPairTokens[token]      -> bool
      pairTokenEconomics[token]      -> { phantomQuote, graduationThreshold,
                                          decimals }
    setPairTokenEconomics() and setPairTokenApproved() are onlyOwner, and
    approval REFUSES a token whose economics are unset or whose decimals do not
    match what the owner declared [PonsV2LaunchFactory.sol:472-543].
    A launch snapshots its terms, so later changes never reach a live launch.

  CURB
    One factory PER asset, terms immutable on it, and a registry that admits
    more than one launcher, add-only. No owner anywhere in the path.

  THE HONEST COMPARISON
    PONS's costs an OWNER who can change future launches' economics; ours costs
    a deployment per asset. Both freeze a live launch's terms, which is the
    property that actually matters, so this is not a safety difference - it is
    a question of who is trusted and how often you redeploy.
    NOT CHANGED. Ours is built, tested and has no admin surface, and this
    project has spent effort removing admin surfaces (C3). Swapping it for an
    owner-set table would trade that away for convenience we do not need at
    seven assets.

  WHAT WE TOOK FROM THEIRS - see section 2.


================================================================================
2. THE DECIMALS FLOOR - ADOPTED, AND IT WAS MISSING
================================================================================

  PONS
    MIN_PAIR_TOKEN_DECIMALS = 6, and the reason is written down: curve fees are
    integer basis points of the quote leg, so on a coarse asset every trade
    below BASIS_POINTS / feeBps base units rounds its fee to ZERO and a trader
    splits one order into fee-free pieces [PonsV2LaunchFactory.sol:74, :481-486].
    They also require the asset to REPORT the decimals the owner declared.

  CURB, BEFORE
    Nothing. No decimals check anywhere in src/curb. Our curve does the same
    integer-bps arithmetic, so it had the same hole.

  DONE
    CurbLaunchFactory now refuses a quote asset below 6 decimals, and refuses
    one that will not answer decimals() at all - the phantom reserve is
    denominated in that asset's units, so silence is not something to take on
    trust. Three tests: USDC at exactly 6 is accepted, a 4-decimal token is
    refused, a token with no decimals() is refused.

    EVERY B20 EQUITY CARRIES 8, so this bites nothing today. That is the
    argument for adding it now, not against it.

3. THE THRESHOLD RULE - THEIRS IS CLEARER THAN OURS, AND IT DOES NOT FIT
================================================================================

  PONS'S RULE, quoted from their own comment [PonsV2LaunchFactory.sol:182-187]:
    "Both figures are sized off chain from a target native-quote value at a
     chosen rate. Scaling the pair by one rate leaves the curve's shape
     untouched, since only threshold / (threshold + phantomQuote) determines
     the fraction of supply that reaches the graduated pool, so a custom quote
     asset trades identically to a native launch of the same size."

    In plain terms: PICK ONE TARGET VALUE, convert it into each asset's units
    at that asset's rate. Every launch raises the same amount of money. It is a
    better-stated rule than ours and it is the one a launchpad wants.

  OURS, AS BUILT: the same SHARE of each asset's largest measured pool as the
    decided 60 NVDAc is of its own - 41.38%. Every launch is equally fillable;
    the amounts raised differ.

  APPLYING PONS'S RULE TO OUR ASSETS. 60 NVDAc at the pools' own mids is
  $13,231. Converting that at each mid, and asking what share of that asset's
  pool it would be:

    asset      mid      pool $    PONS units   of its pool    ours today
    NVDAc   220.51      31,971         60.00           41%         60.00
    SPCXc   148.97      16,189         88.81           82%         44.97
    GOOGLc  331.44      38,506         39.92           34%         48.08
    AMZNc   253.04       7,873         52.29          168%         12.88
    MSFTc   495.36       6,772         26.71          195%          5.66
    AAPLc   325.93       2,692         40.59          492%          3.42
    METAc   654.42       4,026         20.22          329%          2.55

    FOUR OF THE SIX BECOME UNFILLABLE. Not expensive - impossible: the pool
    does not hold that much of the asset at any price.

  SO THREE THINGS CANNOT ALL BE TRUE AT ONCE, and this is the decision:

    (a) PONS's rule - every launch raises the same amount
    (b) D1's decided 60 NVDAc
    (c) seven assets, each fillable from the venues that exist

    KEEP (a) AND (b)  -> the enabled list is NVDAc and GOOGLc. Nothing else has
                         a pool deep enough: carrying a $13,231 threshold at
                         NVDAc's own 41% bar needs $31,971 of asset in a pool,
                         and only those two clear it.
    KEEP (a) AND (c)  -> one common target set by the tightest asset, AAPLc, at
                         $1,114. NVDAc's threshold falls from 60 to 5.05 units,
                         which REVERSES D1.
    KEEP (b) AND (c)  -> what is built today: equal fillability, and amounts
                         raised from $1,108 to $15,909.

  NOT CHANGED, AND WHY. Every branch either drops five assets or reverses a
  decision recorded on 16 September. That is a product decision with a real
  cost on every side and it is not mine to take - the same reason F1 is still
  open. What has changed is that it is now a choice between three named
  options with the arithmetic attached, instead of an open question.

  MY READING, FOR WHAT IT IS WORTH: (a)+(b) is the honest launchpad. Two assets
  that work beat seven that half-work, and it needs no decision reversed. The
  five that drop out are not lost - they come back when their liquidity does,
  which is one line in quoteAssets().

4. WHAT PONS HAS THAT WE DO NOT
================================================================================

  Listed because "do what PONS does" has to include the things they do that we
  have never built. None of these is adopted here; each needs a decision.

  4.1 A SNIPE TAX.  snipeTaxStartBps = 9_900 (99%), snipeTaxSeconds = 15
      [PonsV2LaunchFactory.sol:319-320]. A launch-second tax decaying to the
      ordinary rate over 15 seconds, with an exemption list of up to 32
      addresses. It is their anti-sniping mechanism and WE HAVE NOTHING.
      Against adopting: it changes trade economics, and D2 froze the fee model.
      For: a launchpad without it hands the first block to whoever pays most
      for it.

  4.2 A GLOBAL KILL SWITCH.  setLaunchEnabled(bool), onlyOwner [:435].
      THIS IS D4's "named halt authority", already built by somebody else. D4
      is open and asks for exactly this plus a person and a response time.

  4.3 GRADUATION RESCUE.  GRADUATION_RESCUE_DELAY = 7 days [:104]. A stuck
      graduation has a documented way out after a week. Ours is permissionlessly
      retryable, which covers the transient case and not the permanent one.

  4.4 A LAUNCH FEE.  setLaunchFee() [:427]. We charge a fixed 0.0005 ETH per
      launch, immutable per factory (DeployCurb); PONS's is settable. (This line
      used to say we charge nothing to create.)

  4.5 A CREATOR-FEE-RECIPIENT OVERRIDE, behind a 3-day timelock with a 3-day
      execution window [:99, :107, :946]. WE DECIDED THE OPPOSITE: C3 records
      that nobody can point a creator's income at an address of their choosing
      and there is no admin recovery, and names the cost. (Since core v2 a
      launch's creator income can be switched once, one way, by its creator -
      or at launch, through the launch's own config - to that launch's holder
      distributor; nobody can name the address.) PONS decided the other way
      and paid for it with a timelock. Both are defensible; ours is recorded,
      so it stands unless reversed deliberately.

  4.6 A LAUNCHER ALLOWLIST.  setWhitelistedLauncher() [:443]. A5 decided
      anyone can create a launch. Opposite, and also recorded.

  4.7 WIDER FEE CEILINGS.  MAX_CREATOR_TAX_CEILING_BPS = 1_000 (10%) and
      MAX_TOTAL_TRADE_FEE_BPS = 2_000 (20%) [:60-61], against our band of
      1%-5% with a platform share of 50 bps at deployment, which the policy
      owner can move to 0..80 bps for later launches and each launch freezes
      (it said "a fixed 50 bps"). Ours is tighter on purpose, and the band is
      frozen by D2.

  IT IS THE RIGHT ONE TO COMPARE AGAINST. PONS runs on Robinhood Chain against
  assets that are not B20; thestonks.exchange runs on BASE, prices coins in the

    MEASURED, on Base mainnet at block 51534420, read-only:
      $STONKEX 0x5ab000ff9B9FfE0349CE5ffA5fD86f217C3680F5
        symbol "STONKEX", 18 decimals, totalSupply EXACTLY 1,000,000,000
      its Uniswap v3 pools are on the 1% TIER
        WETH/1%  0x7692acc1...  26,403,449 STONKEX / 96.25 WETH

      no bonding curve; the whole supply is ONE single-sided v3 position
      the LP NFT goes to a locker and is locked FOREVER

      trading fee 1% total, split 0.7% creator / 0.3% platform
      of the platform's 0.3%: 80% buys back and burns $STONKEX, 20% is revenue
      1% to 10% of supply airdropped to a leaderboard

6.2 THE ARCHITECTURAL DIFFERENCE, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF SECTION 3

    THEY HAVE NO CURVE, NO THRESHOLD AND NO GRADUATION. The supply is seeded
    single-sided at launch: the pool opens holding 100% of the coin and NONE of
    the stock, and buyers bring the stock in.

    THAT IS WHY THEY CAN LIST ALL THIRTEEN, including COINc, CRCLc and INTCc,
    which MULTI_RWA.txt 2.2 measured as having NO POOL AT ANY VENUE. Listing an
    asset costs them nothing IN that asset, because nobody has to acquire it to
    launch. The liquidity that is missing still bites their BUYERS - a buyer
    still needs the stock - but it does not bite the launch.

    OURS FILLS A CURVE FIRST. A launch has to take in `threshold` units of the
    asset before it graduates, so the asset has to be OBTAINABLE in that size.
    That is exactly the constraint section 3 could not satisfy for four of six
    assets, and it is a constraint of the curve model, not of Base.

    SO SECTION 3'S TRILEMMA IS REAL FOR US AND ABSENT FOR THEM. Their design
    does not answer F1; it dissolves it - at the price of replacing the curve,
    the graduator, the v4 hook, the threshold and the phantom. That is not a
    parameter change and it is not proposed here.

6.3 WHAT IS COMPARABLE, PARAMETER BY PARAMETER

    supply         THEM 1,000,000,000 at 18 decimals (MEASURED on $STONKEX)
                   US   1,000,000,000 at 18 decimals
                   IDENTICAL. Nothing to change.

    pool tier      THEM 1% (MEASURED on their own pools; stated for launches)
                   US   0.05%, chosen 2026-09-16 after measuring both

                   A TWENTY-FOLD DIFFERENCE. A first version of this entry

                   worth the entry, is that NONE OF THE SEVEN IS DEEPEST AT
                   0.05%: the market for these assets picks 0.3% or 1%, and our
                   tier is six to twenty times below either.

                     0.3%  NVDAc, AAPLc, METAc
                     1%    GOOGLc, MSFTc, AMZNc, SPCXc
                     0.05% none

                   AND IT IS AN ANALOGY, NOT A READ-ACROSS. Those are the

                   WHO IT MOVES MONEY BETWEEN: the trader pays the tier on top

                   NOT CHANGED: D1 is a decided, measured parameter and this is

    trading fee    THEM 1% total: 0.7% creator, 0.3% platform
                   US   1%-5% total, chosen by the creator, of which the

                        frozen per launch (this said "a FIXED 0.5%")
                   AT ITS STARTING VALUE OUR PLATFORM TAKES MORE THAN THEIRS -
                   50 bps against 30 - and our floor equals their whole fee.

    liquidity      THEM locked forever
                   US   180 days
                   Theirs is the stronger promise and the simpler one to state.
                   Ours is a decided parameter.

    platform token THEM 80% of their 0.3% buys back and burns $STONKEX

6.4 WHAT THIS CHANGES HERE

    NOTHING IN THE CODE. Every difference above is either identical already
    (supply), frozen by a recorded decision (fee model, D2), decided and
    measured (tier, D1; lock), or a different product (the curve itself).

    WHAT IT CHANGES IS THE ARGUMENT. Section 3 offered three options for F1 and
    recommended keeping the decided 60 NVDAc with a two-asset list. A fourth
    option now exists and it is the one a competitor on this chain chose:

      (d) DROP THE CURVE. Seed single-sided, list all thirteen, and F1 stops
          being a question. It costs the curve, the graduator, the v4 hook and
          every test that covers them, and it changes what the product IS - a
          launchpad where price is discovered by trading from a fixed opening
          rather than sold up a curve to a threshold.

    I am not proposing (d). It is recorded because leaving it out would make
    the F1 decision look narrower than it is, and because the reference the
    instruction pointed at is built that way.

7. THE ADMIN SURFACE, ALL THREE SIDE BY SIDE - MEASURED 2026-09-21
================================================================================

  WHY THIS SECTION EXISTS. We added an owner to FeeHook on 2026-09-21 so the
  platform's own fee leg can be changed for future launches, following PONS.
  Adding an admin key to a product whose claim is "there is nobody to trust"
  deserves the comparison rather than an assurance, so here is what the
  references actually carry.

  PONS - READ FROM THEIR SOURCE
    FOUR owned contracts, all Ownable2Step, all with renounceOwnership
    overridden to REVERT:
      PonsV2LaunchFactory   17 owner-only functions
      PonsV2MemeHook         9
      PonsV2BuybackVault     1
      PonsV2LaunchLocker     1
    What the factory's owner can do is the part worth naming, because it is not
    only fees: setLaunchEnabled is a global kill switch for launching at all;
    setPairTokenApproved decides which quote assets exist; setMaxCreatorTaxBps
    and setSnipeTax* move the economics; setWhitelistedLauncher gates who may
    launch; setGraduationExecutor, setLaunchDeployer and setLaunchForwarder
    SWAP OUT COMPONENTS; and setCreatorFeeRecipient redirects A CREATOR'S OWN
    fee recipient - a power we deliberately do not have: our one call to it is
    the one-way switch of a launch's income to that launch's own holder
    distributor, made by its beneficiary, never to an address anybody names
    [PRE_MAINNET C3].
    Their rescueCurveFees is owner-only. Ours is open to the curve's
    creator-fee recipient and the platform recipient at once, and to anyone
    else one hour after Graduator.noteReady(token) noted the curve full and
    ungraduated [AUDIT C-1]: the same escape hatch with the operator removed
    from in front of it where it matters - a full curve is the only one
    whose pending fees block graduation - and with an hour in which
    graduate() stays open to everybody, so nobody else can force a
    creator's fees out early, nor in the transaction that filled the
    curve.

  THESTONKS.EXCHANGE - READ OFF BASE MAINNET, read-only, 2026-09-21
    Their site names three contracts. Two of them are PROXIES:

      Launcher   0x4714f6EC81639Ca59EEBE634490a4d8671DCe7B4   130 bytes
      FeeLocker  0x71D1D363176723f85d98B8B430DF33cde89f0A7f   130 bytes

    130 bytes is not logic. The bytecode is identical in both: push the
    ERC-1967 implementation slot, SLOAD it, DELEGATECALL. Their
    implementations answer proxiableUUID() with that same slot, which is the
    UUPS marker - so the upgrade authority is the implementation's own owner.

      Launcher  -> impl 0x6a9f14e7742e8972fcf86429c5aa7db56589806d  21,855 bytes
      FeeLocker -> impl 0x6c9c9fd81b914a59585d966af140211df0325273  12,750 bytes
      owner() on BOTH proxies  0x81dd3174d55fcf396e92122881ca591705c4e1e1
      that address has ZERO BYTES OF CODE - an EOA, one key, not a multisig

    AND THEN I GOT IT WRONG, WHICH IS WORTH MORE THAN THE FINDING WOULD HAVE
    BEEN. What this section said first:

        "SO ONE KEY CAN REPLACE THE LOGIC OF BOTH CONTRACTS ... the site and
         the bytecode disagree ... 'immutable' is not a property of the
         deployment."

    BOTH SENTENCES ARE FALSE. I measured two things - a UUPS proxy, and an
    owner with no code - and INFERRED a third, that the upgrade path works. I
    never called it. Then I wrote the inference up as a measurement and used it
    to say somebody else's documentation was contradicted by their own chain.

    THE MEASUREMENT I SHOULD HAVE MADE, made now: eth_call upgradeToAndCall
    FROM THE OWNER'S OWN ADDRESS, on both proxies.

        both revert, with 0xbbbfc8f8 = UpgradesFrozen()

    which is exactly the "UPGRADES FROZEN" label their own page carries. The
    upgrade function exists and refuses everyone, the owner included. Their
    claim holds; mine did not.

    SO THE ACTUAL POSITION IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT I WROTE: their two core
    contracts are UUPS-shaped and permanently frozen, which is a STRONGER
    guarantee than a plain non-upgradeable deployment would be, because the
    freeze is enforced in code that anybody can call and watch revert.

    THE WRONG VERSION IS KEPT ABOVE ON PURPOSE. It is the exact mistake this
    project keeps writing rules against - an argument presented as a
    measurement - and deleting it would leave the rule without the example.

    WHAT THEIR OPERATOR CAN AND CANNOT DO, from their own page and consistent
    with the above:
      CAN   list stocks and their price feeds; override an opening anchor; and,
            EACH BEHIND A 24-HOUR ANNOUNCED ON-CHAIN DELAY, change the fee
            split within hard caps that always leave the creator at least 20%,
            or reassign an abandoned token's creator.
      CANNOT touch any LP position or locked liquidity, upgrade either
            contract, mint or pause a launched token, or change which locker a
            launch hands to.
      NOT VERIFIED BY ME: the 24-hour delay and the hard caps. The upgrade
            freeze is the only one of these I measured. Saying which is which
            is the whole point of this section now.

  AND ON THE MEASURE THAT MATTERS, THEIR LOCKER IS TIGHTER THAN OUR LOCK.
    Their StonkFeeLocker2 has, by their description, no decreaseLiquidity, no
    burn, no NFT transfer and no approval path - the principal cannot leave at
    all, ever, for anyone. Ours releases to the beneficiary after the lock
    expires, which is a different product decision (a timed lock rather than a
    permanent one) and not a weaker version of the same one. Worth naming
    because "locked liquidity" is said of both and means different things.

  CURB - FOR COMPARISON
      FeeHook   2 owner-only functions: the platform's recipient and its rate.
                Ownable2Step. RENOUNCE LEFT WORKING - see below.
      PonsV2BuybackVault (vendored)  1, and unreachable [AUDIT A-9].
      Everything else ownerless: no proxy, no delegatecall, no selfdestruct,
      no pause, no rescue over value, and no power over a creator's share, a
      registry record, a locked position or anybody's balance.

  THE ONE PLACE WE DELIBERATELY DO NOT COPY PONS
    They override renounceOwnership to revert, and give their reason: "An
    ownerless hook could never rotate the fee sweep operator, so accrued fees
    on every graduated pool would stay stranded."
    THAT REASON IS THEIRS. Our feeSweepOperator is a constant address(0) and is
    never rotated, and the curve lets a creator sweep their own launch whenever
    no buyback is involved - which is always, because buyback is off by
    construction. An ownerless hook strands nothing of ours.
    So renouncing is a CLEAN EXIT here rather than a hazard: it freezes both
    values where they stand and the product returns to exactly the immutable
    arrangement it had before the setters existed. Measured
    [test_theFeePolicyKeyCanBeGivenUpAndThePolicyThenFreezes]. It is what makes
    this the one reversible decision at deployment.

  WHAT A TRADE COSTS, THEIRS AGAINST OURS - 2026-09-21
    Asked because the obvious question about a live competitor on our own chain
    is whether they are cheaper. On FEES the answer is close; on WHO KEEPS THEM
    it is not.

      THEIRS   one 1% Uniswap V3 tier fee, and nothing else.
               0.7% creator, 0.3% platform. No curve phase, so no second fee
               anywhere.
      OURS     the launch's own tax T, which its CREATOR picks between 1% and
               5%, plus our pool's 0.05% tier [POOL_FEE = 500].
               0.5% platform always; T - 0.5% creator; the 0.05% tier accrues
               to the locked position, whose beneficiary is the creator.

      AT OUR FLOOR, WHICH IS THE ONLY FAIR COMPARISON:
                        trader pays   platform takes   creator keeps
        thestonks          1.00%           0.30%           0.70%
        CURB at T=1%       1.05%           0.50%           0.55%

      So at the closest configuration we can offer: the trader pays about the
      same, WE TAKE TWO THIRDS MORE THAN THEY DO, and THEIR CREATOR EARNS MORE
      THAN OURS. A creator choosing between the two on economics alone has no
      reason to pick us at any T, and at T = 5% their trader pays a fifth of
      what ours does.

      AND THE COMPARISON IS NOT LIKE FOR LIKE, in both directions:
        we have a CURVE PHASE and they have none, so a CURB launch raises
          against the stock before a pool exists and charges T there too;
        their opening cap is ~$4,000 for EVERY coin, anchored to the stock's
          Chainlink feed, while ours needs 60 NVDAc - about $13,230 - before a
          pool exists at all. Those are different products at different scales,
          not the same product at different prices.
        price impact is not in the table because their launched coins' pools
          were not measured. D1 measured OURS all-in at $100: 2.13% at T=1%.

      NOT A RECOMMENDATION TO CHANGE ANYTHING. D2 froze the fee model and D1
      chose the tier on measured depth. This is the number, recorded where
      somebody comparing us can find it.

  AND THE THING ALL THREE ANSWER DIFFERENTLY: WHAT THE PLATFORM'S FEE BUYS
    PONS               buyback-and-LOCK. The slice buys the memecoin off the
                       curve and vests it five years - to the CREATOR and the
                       protocol, not to holders.
    thestonks          buyback-and-BURN. 0.3% of a 1% swap fee, of which "80%
                       of it buys $STONKEX and destroys it, 20% is operator
                       revenue".
    CURB               neither. Buyback is off by construction [E1] and the
                       platform's leg is paid out.

    THE BURN IS THE INTERESTING ONE FOR US, and it answers the question that
    started this: how do you return fees to holders of a plain ERC-20 with no
    transfer hook and no checkpoints? You do not distribute - you reduce
    supply. It needs no balance tracking, no claim step, no per-holder state,
    and every holder benefits in proportion by construction. It is the one
    shape that sidesteps the obstacle rather than engineering around it.
    NOT PROPOSED HERE, only recorded: our fees arrive in a tokenized stock
    rather than in our own token, so a burn would need a swap first, and that
    is a design rather than a line.
